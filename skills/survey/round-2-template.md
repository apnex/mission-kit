# Round 2 — question-design guidance

**Goal:** maximize the degrees of freedom informing the design phase. Round 2 is
the proposer's **choice of question type** — refine deeper, clarify an ambiguous
Round-1 pick, anchor a newly-surfaced dimension, or mix. Same brevity and
pick-list discipline as Round 1.

---

## Proposer prompt

Design three Round-2 questions informed by the Round-1 picks and interpretations.
Apply the same orthogonality + pick-list discipline as Round 1 (see
`round-1-template.md`). Pick the strategy that fits the Round-1 outcome *before*
writing the questions.

## Round-2 strategy table

| Round-1 outcome | Round-2 strategy |
|---|---|
| All three dimensions cleanly confirmed | Refine deeper (drill into the how/where/when); or explore a new dimension if one emerged |
| Ambiguity left on some dimensions | Clarify the ambiguous picks before design starts |
| Some confirmed, others ambiguous | Mix: refine the confirmed + clarify the ambiguous |
| A contradictory multi-pick surfaced | Round 2 is a clarification candidate — either disambiguate, or carry the constraint envelope forward as a design anchor |

## Round-1 context to carry forward

When designing Round-2 questions, load:

- **The original work-item text** (same anchor as Round 1).
- **Round-1 picks + interpretations** — they narrow the intent space the
  Round-2 questions probe. Round 2 builds on Round 1, it does not restart it.
- **This round's outcome-axis mapping** — Round 2 may surface a different axis
  weight than Round 1; refresh the mapping per round so the drift check stays
  honest.
- **The aggregate Round-1 surface** — each pick contextualizes the others, the
  way constraints propagate in a logic puzzle.

## Refinement heuristics

- **Probe new surfaces.** Round-2 questions should concretize the *implications*
  of Round-1 picks, not re-ask Round-1 dimensions.
- **Concretization preference.** If Round 1 anchored why/who/cadence, Round 2
  typically anchors what/where/when — the mechanism.
- **Clarification framing.** When probing an ambiguous Round-1 pick, structure the
  question to disambiguate the *constraint set*, rather than re-asking the
  original question verbatim.
- **Constraint-envelope framing.** If Round 1 surfaced a contradictory multi-pick,
  the Round-2 question can either (a) directly probe a disambiguation, or
  (b) carry the constraint envelope as a design-phase anchor.

## Capture target

Synthesize the three questions for the decision-authority. Record returned picks
into the envelope's `## §2 Round 2 picks` table, write a 1-2 paragraph
interpretation in each `### §2.Q<N>` subsection, and fill the Round-2
outcome-axis mapping + composite read. Then assemble the §3 composite intent
envelope and capture the §calibration data point (stakeholder time-cost,
comparison baseline, notes) — both are required envelope elements.
