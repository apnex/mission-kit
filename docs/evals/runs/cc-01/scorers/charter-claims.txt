Question:
A set's charter claims a scope that no current member fills. Should the charter narrow its claim to what its members cover? And is stating that scope all a charter does?

Key:
No: a charter holds the set's vision - what it is for and claims to cover - and is never cut to fit current holdings; an unfilled claim is a gap and is named. It is not all a charter does: it also manages the set against that claim - territory, boundaries (what enters, where the rest goes), faults, and how changes are made.

Rubric:
2 for no with the gap reasoning, and naming at least one management concern beyond the vision. 1 for no with the gap reasoning alone. 0 otherwise.

On territory it also says: "a cell no member fills is kept and named as a gap, and removing it to match the population erases the gap it exposes."
Stating the scope is not all a charter does. E4: "A charter holds the set's vision, and manages the set against it." The management part covers territory (where the population stands against the claim), boundaries (what enters and where everything else goes), composition, faults, a generated index, and changes made under "Changing a charter".
E4 also lists conditional concerns that apply only when the property is present: kinds, member shape, placement, enforcement boundary, sub-sets, axiom alignment and probes.
