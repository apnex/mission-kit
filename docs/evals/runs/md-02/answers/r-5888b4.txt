### charter-claims.V1
No. entities/E4-charter.md says the vision "is what the set is for and what it claims to cover; the population is measured against it ... and it is never cut to fit what the set holds today". It also says "A claim no member yet fills is a gap - the distance between the population and its charter - and naming it is how gaps are found". It adds that "a cell no member fills is kept and named as a gap, and removing it to match the population erases the gap it exposes". Stating the scope is not all a charter does: "A charter holds the set's vision, and manages the set against it". The management covers territory (where the population stands against the claim), boundaries (what enters and where the rest goes), faults (how the set goes wrong), and changes made under "Changing a charter". The required concerns are Purpose, Territory, Boundaries, Composition, Faults and Index, plus conditional concerns such as Kinds, Member shape, Enforcement boundary and Probes (E4 tables).

### charter-claims.V8
None of its own. work-types/README.md (W0) "Gaps recorded" says: "Work on the knowledge corpus is building on its domain, and has no work-type of its own". It continues: "Adding or revising an entry or a tool is a build slice, W1, or a fix, W2, where a defect has been filed". It also says: "Retiring an entry supersedes it - the entry keeps its ID as a stub - which is also a build slice; only deleting a surface outright, such as a whole layer or a tool, is a hard cut, W3". So revising means W1 (or W2 if a defect has been filed), and retiring an entry means W1, not W3, despite W3's title "retire-or-hard-cut".

### OBSERVATIONS
- artifacts/README.md (AR0) line 13 says the lifecycle document types come "each with a schema". But schemas/README.md (SC0) says they have only prose templates and that no contract governs lifecycle documents. Either "schema" is used loosely in AR0 or the two charters contradict each other.
- W3's title "retire-or-hard-cut" invites using it to retire a corpus entry. W0 says retiring an entry is W1 and only outright deletion of a surface is W3. This is easy to misroute unless a reader opens W0.
- E3 and E4 both say "thirteen charters". The INDEX lists sixteen `0`-slot charters (A0, R0, D0, W0, M0, RU0, PC0, ST0, S0, P0, K0, E0, C0, AR0, T0, SC0). The figure probably dates from before some layers were added (inferred, I did not count dates).
- M0 places PC1 at "Entering". PC1's own trigger ("about to add an entry to a curated collection") is about authoring an addition, not about beginning in an unfamiliar collection. The placement is defensible but not obvious.
- In methods/README.md, the "Rules hold what procedures record" paragraph is followed immediately by "---" with no blank line (line 110-111). This is a minor formatting issue.
- INDEX.md tells readers to resolve links against a checkout or raw GitHub main. The corpus I was given is a local copy at another path, and I did not check whether it matches main.
