### explain-routing.Q1
INDEX.md routes this to ST1 (sets/ST1-explain.md: "You are about to communicate with a human who has limited context"), whose two members are S15 and M10. S15 (style/S15-message-to-a-human.md) asks for "Point first: the first line is the conclusion", so the slip goes in the first line, with one question at the end, unseen names explained, and narrative only "where it carries understanding". If you want something decided, S15's decision frame applies: "what is being decided; why now; the options, each with its cost, including doing nothing; a recommendation, labelled and kept separate". M10 (methods/M10-guided-dialogue.md) asks that the director "ends up seeing what you see on the point - nothing missing that matters, nothing extra", and that nothing is "chosen for them or hidden". A13 asks you to economise the director's attention without going "silently dark on an action or decision the Director is owed", and never to narrow the options. S0 (style/README.md) is the layer charter, and its trigger fires on "a message a human will read". NOT SETTLED: no entry is specific to bad news, and ST1 records this as a gap: "Bad news and teaching were each tested in one pair; no move is isolated."

### explain-routing.Q2
The routing is the same: ST1, so S15 and M10, with S0 as the layer charter. M10 says to apply it "especially when they have not followed the work". It asks you to know the finish line and "say how many steps remain", and to give one step per message. S15 asks for point first, "Show where you are: the step, and the finish line", system-specific names explained or left out, and "Leave out what you did when it does not change what the human understands or decides". A13 (axioms/A13-director-intent-amplification.md) asks that "Status-chatter, ceremony, how-to ... never reach the Director", and prefers "digest over dump, decision over status". It still forbids under-surfacing. RU1 asks you to lead with yield ("Lead every summary with the actual delivered outcome"), but only when the update reports a sweep, refactor programme or audit; ST1 notes RU1 "is triggered by sweeps and audits, not by any report". NOT SETTLED: how a pure progress update squares with A13's bar on status (see OBSERVATIONS).

GUESS: AR4's tags ([MEASURED] versus [ARGUED]) are a reasonable way to mark what you observed apart from what you inferred, but AR4 governs decision records, not messages.

### explain-routing.Q5
ST1 applies, so S15 and M10 do too, with S0 as the layer charter. S15's decision frame asks for "what is being decided; why now" (the deadline goes here), "the options, each with its cost, including doing nothing; a recommendation, labelled and kept separate; then ask for a pick". The decision request goes first and is "never hidden in an optional pick". M10 step 4 asks for one decision per message, and says checking the options are complete "needs the real options, so it is this step's, not the message's form". A13 asks that the director see "the full, unbiased option set", and that you never narrow it, pre-empt the choice, or synthesise the choice for them. Any lean you add appears "as a labeled tie-break input". R4 and W23 say that the choice itself is the director's alone ("cannot be satisfied by architect narrative"). M10 step 5, with AR4 and W23, has you record the decision and its reasoning afterwards and "say where in one line". NOT SETTLED: how S15's "two or three picks" (Continue/Explore/Return) fits three options plus doing nothing (see OBSERVATIONS).

### explain-routing.Q6
ST1 applies, so S15 and M10 do too, with S0 as the layer charter. M10's decide limit requires "one decision at a time", and step 4 requires "one decision per message". So ask the first decision, record it (step 5), and only then ask the second, each in S15's decision frame. M10 step 1 ("say how many steps remain") and S15 ("Show where you are: the step, and the finish line") ask you to show that a second decision follows. M10 step 3 says to establish and confirm any understanding a decision depends on first. A13 caps this: never narrow or pre-empt either choice. AR3 is related, not a member, and ST1 notes a tension with it ("many moves at once, against one decision at a time"); its "Decisions required" must each name what it blocks, and "a move whose dependency is unmet is not legal yet". NOT SETTLED: whether to preview the second decision's options in the first message. GUESS: name it as the next step, but frame it only after the first is decided.

### OBSERVATIONS
- Two entries pull against each other on progress updates. A13 says status-chatter should never reach the Director ("decision over status"), yet updating a director who has not followed the work is a plausible need, and A13 also counts under-surfacing as a fault. No entry settles which governs a progress update that asks for no decision.
- S15 says to end with "two or three picks", each starting Continue/Explore/Return. Its decision frame lists the options "including doing nothing". For three options this gives at least four choices. Nothing says whether the options are the picks or sit inside the Continue pick.
- S5's trigger ("name a version, date or other point-in-time identifier in prose") fires on a release-slip message that names a date. But its rule and "When to apply" cover user-facing documentation, not messages. The trigger is broader than the rule's scope.
- S15 and M10 cite evidence at `docs/evals/human/runs/guidance-1-*` and `docs/evals/human/MATRIX.md`, which are not in this corpus. ST1 also says the evaluation procedure "exists only as a tool and suites in this corpus's own records, not as an entry". So the evidence claims cannot be checked from the corpus.
- ST1's gaps state that bad news and teaching (Q1, Q4) were "each tested in one pair". No entry is specific to bad news, causal "why" answers, retellable explanations, or deadlines beyond the "why now" slot.
- K5 (three questions per round) and AR3 (many moves at once) are named in ST1 as being in tension with one question/decision at a time. They are left unreconciled, so a cold agent routed by K5's or AR3's own triggers could get opposite instructions, which is a fault ST1 itself names.
