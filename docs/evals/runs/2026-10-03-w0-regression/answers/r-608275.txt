### component.C1
Y: your fix modifies X and does not modify Y. X already called Y, so Y is not "newly" depended on. That puts Y outside the change's scope (axioms/README.md: "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters"). Y is still adjacent, because X "stands on" it through a declared interface, and that is the direction with more payback (entities/E6-component.md, "Adjacent against nearby"). So A0 says improvement is "encouraged, never mandated", and you are "expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". The A14 tangent test looks met here: payback is close (it cost you 20 minutes), the work is 5 minutes, and the yield is captured. So fix Y "as a change of its own", and that change answers to every system it alters, including any consumer whose contract it changes (axioms/README.md; axioms/A14-compounding-learning.md "Friction -> root cause, never workaround"). ### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it." It has "one duty" at its altitude, and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". Its boundary is those declared interfaces, and that boundary separates it from a neighbour only where the neighbour uses them. "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing." The difference is which question you ask. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6 Discriminators; also entities/E5-system.md "A system against a component"). A system that nothing contains is not a component of anything. E6 also says a component is not a file, directory, package or SysML part by definition, and it does not need to be registered in components/.

### component.C3
No. "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces" (entities/E6-component.md, "Boundary against directory"). E5 agrees: "A directory... is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". What is wrong: nothing declares an interface, so nothing separates the two parts. For deciding what a change answers to, parts/thing and parts/other are "one system, with the traits of both" (E5 Boundaries; E6 Definition). parts/thing is still a component, but "nothing separates it yet". The undeclared dependency "is itself a fault against A3" (Air-Gap Principle: "no unit reaches into another's internals"). As a result, a change to parts/thing's internals can reach parts/other (E6 blast-radius row). The remedy is to declare the interface and move parts/other onto it. A change that alters what parts/other reaches into "owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5). The A3 "Bypass as Signal" evaluation (should the interface change to match the consumer's legitimate needs?) is stated for reaching past a declared interface. Where no interface exists yet, applying that evaluation is GUESS: the same reasoning would apply when the interface is designed.

### component.C4
Your change modifies P, so it answers to P's axioms and to the axioms of every system that contains P, which includes the service (axioms/README.md: "the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters"). Q is not altered. The interface and its behaviour are unchanged, and Q "only composes with" P through that contract, so Q's axioms as a system of its own do not bind the change (A0; entities/E6-component.md blast-radius row). Caveats: this holds only if Q honours the interface and does not reach past it into P's internals (entities/E5-system.md "Declared against honoured"). Also, the service contains Q and "has every trait any part it contains has" (E5). So traits that Q gives the service still reach your change through the service's axioms. Q is adjacent to P (they share a declared interface, and Q stands on P), so Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement (A0: "one it stands on, or one that stands on it"). E6 says this direction is "adjacency with less payback, not none". The improvement must pass A14's tangent test, be made as a separate change, and be recorded either way. Without that, the gap is recorded with a revival trigger (RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 defines touched as "newly depends on". An existing dependency that the change relies on (Y in C1) is therefore neither touched nor in scope, even though the work stands on it directly. That reading is correct as written but easy to miss, and A0 does not say whether a gap in an existing dependency "must not be built on".
- The A3 "Bypass as Signal" evaluation and E5's ending ("moved onto the interface, or the interface adjusted") cover reaching past a declared interface. Neither text says outright how the evaluation applies when no interface was ever declared (C3).
- E5/A0 say that only an altered system's axioms bind, while the containing service has the traits of every part it contains (Q in C4). In effect Q's traits still bind a change to P through the service. A reader can take "Q's axioms do not bind" to mean more than it does.
- Neither text says how an opportunistic change should be ordered against an unlanded main change (C1).
- A0's opportunistic test says "compounding learning supports it". A14 states adjacency as the tangent sitting "under load-bearing infrastructure the main line will stand on", which is narrower than E6/A0's two-way adjacency. E6 reconciles them as payback weight, but the two texts read differently.
