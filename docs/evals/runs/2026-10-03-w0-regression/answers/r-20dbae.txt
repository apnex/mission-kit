### design-rulings.R1
Your change has to take ownership of the N-T boundary. That means declaring an interface for what N needs from T and getting N to use it. Doing this is part of your change's scope, not an optional extra. entities/E5-system.md (Boundaries): "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional." A3 (Bypass as Signal) and E5 (Declared against honoured) say how the bypass ends: first evaluate the interface against T's duty, then either move N onto the interface or adjust the interface to what N legitimately needs. Until that happens, N and T stay one system for binding, with the traits of both. Both modules sit inside the service, so the change also answers to the containing service and its traits, and no chaining is needed (E5: "units inside one system need no chaining"; axioms/README.md: a change answers to "every system containing it"). The undeclared reach is itself an A3 fault (E5, A0).
