### work-layers.L2
Skills (`skills/`, prefix K): skills/README.md (K0) says a skill "is *invoked*, not followed: it names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output", and earns an entry when "executable, repeatable, and would otherwise be reconstructed from scratch".
K0 separates it from a method: "a skill packages a procedure so an agent can install and invoke it, and earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary"; a fixed output layout means templates, which are assets.
M0's boundary table agrees: "a capability you execute | skills/ | ... a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed", and M0's faults list "The procedure that is really a skill".
It also already exists: skills/K3-substrate-audit.md describes a "five-pass meta-process" (Sweep, Triangulation, Promotion audit, Synthesis, Reconciliation) over a repository and a fixed layout (`00-summary.md`, `02-architecture.md`, ... `features/<name>.md`, `sources.md`), with templates in skills/substrate-audit/templates/.
K0 adds a limit: "A skill whose steps a script could take should be that script, per A11", and "A skill invoked once, for one system, is a runbook".
GUESS: the proposal should extend K3 rather than become a new skill, unless its passes differ from K3's.

### work-layers.L4
It is a methodology entry, and methods/M1-triangulated-review.md already covers it: "require at least four genuinely independent inputs before reaching a verdict", triggered when "reviewing a patch or design that ships to production or upstream".
By M0's placement test, following it "produce[s] a result of its own - a verdict", so it is a procedure in `methods/`; M0 calls M1 the "one primitive [that] carries the set ... the independence the verifying procedures specialise".
It is not a work-type: it does not compile to a claimable node by itself, and the work-types that do cite it, W8 verify-gate-reactive and W10, both have `methods: [M1]`.
It is not a skill: it has no packaged scripts or assets and is followed rather than invoked (K0: "A method is a procedure held as guidance, the single source of its content").
Note M1 sets the floor at four kinds (upstream baseline, prior intent and review, archaeology, community signal), and that "below 3 the failure mode is predictable".
The "never trust a single reviewer" half also shows up in W0 constraints 2 and 8 (attester must not be the executor; "Author != approver").

### work-layers.L5
The corpus records this as a known gap: methods/README.md (M0) says "handing work in progress to another agent or a later session has no procedure at all", and "a lone agent hands over to its own next session".
Its layer depends on its shape, by M0's placement test: a procedure whose result is a handover artifact goes in `methods/` under the "Handing over" moment; guidance that only requires a record to carry something is a rule.
rules/README.md (RU0) names that rule form directly: "A rule for handing over - *a handover record names what is unfinished* - would leave a trace, and none exists."
practices/README.md (PC0) says "*handing over* hold[s] no practice today", and that a practice "is admitted when a difference in the work has been observed".
M0 says the gap is "not a defect until something fails there, and ... where to look first when something does".
Related standing commitment: axioms/A4-zero-loss-knowledge.md, success signal "A cold pickup from the record alone reproduces the same outputs as a warm actor ... zero decision context is lost at handover".
NOT SETTLED: whether it should be one method, one rule, or both; GUESS: both (a method producing the handover record, held by a rule naming what that record must contain).

### work-layers.L6
Method: M0 says it is a procedure that, when followed, "yields something that would not exist otherwise" (a verdict, an artifact, a record); skill: K0 says it "is *invoked*, not followed: it names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output", and "earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary"; work-type: W0 says "A candidate is taxonomy-grade iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node", with an evidence contract and a falsifier.
Method and work-type are related, not competing: "The work-type cites the procedure that conducts it, in `methods`" (W0), as W8/W10 cite M1 and W14/W22 cite M7.
Method against skill is the hard pair: both produce a result from a procedure, and K0's line is packaging and invocation, not the result.
The corpus shows the line being crossed: K0, "A skill that only describes is a method ... as `M9` was before it moved", with skills/K1-history-content-scrub.md now "Moved to M9".
M0 lists that fault too: "The procedure that is really a skill. A named capability with inputs and outputs, filed as a way of working."
Also, M0's ordered placement test (axiom, procedure, style, rule, practice) has no question for skill or work-type, so a skill gets placed in `methods/` at question 2 unless you use M0's boundary table ("invoked rather than followed").
M7 against W22 looks close (both axiom-alignment), but W22 cites `methods: [M7]`, so it is the node and M7 the procedure.

### work-layers.L7
