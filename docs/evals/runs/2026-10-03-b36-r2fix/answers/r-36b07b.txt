### design-rulings.R1
Your change has to declare and honour an interface between T and N. That work is inside your change's scope, not optional. E5 (entities/E5-system.md, Boundaries) says: "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional." Until that is done, N and T are "one system, with the traits of both" for deciding what binds the change (E5; A0, axioms/README.md: "the axioms either unit would bring all bind"). The undeclared reach is itself a fault under A3 ("no unit reaches into another's internals", Air-Gap Principle). Following A3's "Bypass as Signal", first check what N needs against T's duty. The bypass then ends one of two ways: "the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" to what N legitimately needs (also E5, "Declared against honoured"). Because the change alters N's contract, N counts as touched (A0: "any consumer whose contract it alters"), and the change answers to N and to every system that contains it.

### design-rulings.R4
Yes, A1 binds the team (the organisation), not the tool. E5 says: "A stateless product built by a team that keeps records it relies on has `A1` binding the team's records, not the product." The organisation counts as a system (E5: "The organisation doing the work is a system"). Its records pass T1's test, because the team writes them, reads them back and relies on them. So A1 governs how those records are kept: as durable truth that every authorized actor can see, with no private or opaque state (A1 Mandate). The CLI tool is stateless and has no model, so only the any-system axioms (A3, A4, A8, A9, A14) bind it, and A1 does not (A0 matrix: "a plain library takes `any-system`"). E5 also says the two questions are asked separately: the organisation's axioms "govern how the change is made and recorded, and are not one of the systems the change alters."

### design-rulings.R5
Your feature must not add to the tangle. A3 says "a change short of it adds none of them, and removes what it touches", and A0 says "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names". If your feature modifies the tangled code, A0 requires you to close as much of the gap "as the touch allows". If the feature only sits beside the tangled code, the split is outside your scope (A0: code that "merely sits beside it, in the same file or module, is not touched"). Beyond scope, improvement is "encouraged, never mandated", but you are "expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to" (A0). A3 "Composable by Default" encourages exactly this split: "within the unit's own duty, fix it, fracture it, re-cut its seams ... as a change of its own, answering to every consumer whose interface it alters". K's interface is not versioned, so the split alters all three consumers, and that separate change answers to each consumer's axioms (A0; E5). If you do not do the split, record the gap with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3), because "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (A0).

### OBSERVATIONS
- R1: E5 Boundaries says declaring and honouring the interface is "not optional". A3 "Bypass as Signal" and E5 "Declared against honoured" describe a detected bypass that first triggers an evaluation and may end with the interface adjusted instead. The two fit together, but read alone the first sentence sounds stricter than the A3 procedure.
- R2: The E5 chaining sentence ("a change to A is one system with C only when it alters something B takes from C") is hard to apply literally. It is unclear how a change to A alone could alter what B takes from C. The question's "alters data" wording does not say which unit the change modifies.
- R3/R1: "operated by several independent agents who hand work to each other" could mean the service is multi-agent (T3), or only that the operating organisation is (E5). The corpus does not say which, and it did not affect these answers.
- R5: Whether a "small feature" touches the tangled internals decides between mandatory (in scope) and encouraged (beyond scope). The question does not say. A0 also frames opportunistic improvement around *adjacent* components, while here the tangle is inside the component being changed. A3 "Composable by Default" is what covers that case.
- R6: E5 does not say how lockfiles or re-resolution interact with "adoption in advance".
