### component.C1
Y is outside your change's scope but adjacent to it. A0 (axioms/README.md) says a change touches "what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", and X already depended on Y. E6 calls components adjacent when they "share a declared interface", and X stands on Y, which is the "closer" direction for payback (entities/E6-component.md). Fixing Y is therefore "considered, never mandated", and A0 says it "may be closed opportunistically" under A14's tangent discipline: the payback is near (twenty minutes lost, five to fix), the yield is captured, and the cost does not displace the goal. If you do it, A0 says it must be "made as a change of its own" that answers to every system it alters. NOT SETTLED: whether an error message counts as part of Y's interface. If it does, X counts as an altered consumer. Z shares no declared interface with X, so it is "nearby and not adjacent" (E6), and two days would displace the goal, so it fails the test. A0 says such a gap "is recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", because the choice of next work belongs to the director (R4, AR3). For both Y and Z, "the gap is recorded", since "passing it silently is what the axiom forbids". Z's hidden state matches A1's "Hidden State Problem". GUESS: A1 binds only if the service passes the T1 stateful test, and even then nothing obliges your change to fix Z.

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it", with "one duty" at that altitude, and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". Its boundary is those interfaces, and it separates the component from a neighbour "only where they are declared and that neighbour uses them". E6 says "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing." The difference lies in the question being asked: traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system, while duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component (E6 Discriminators). entities/E5-system.md defines system as "a unit of engineering with a boundary", where traits describe it and axioms bind it. A system that nothing contains "is not a component of anything" (E6). E6 also says a component is not, by definition, a file, a directory, a registered components/ entry or a SysML part.

### component.C3
No. E6 says "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". There is no declared interface here, so nothing separates the two: parts/thing/ is "still a component, but nothing separates it yet". For deciding what a change answers to, it and parts/other/ are "one system, with the traits of both" (E6, E5 Boundaries). What is wrong is the undeclared dependency, which E5 calls "itself a fault against A3". A3's Air-Gap Principle says "no unit reaches into another's internals". The remedy is "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it" (E5, and E6 line 19). Because parts/other/ is a real consumer, A3's "Earned Exposure" supports exposing such a surface. The two are also not "adjacent" in E6's sense, because adjacency needs a declared interface.

### component.C4
Your change answers to P, as a system, and to every system containing P, which here means the service. A0 says "a change answers to every system it alters... the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters". E6's blast-radius row says the same. Q is not altered, because "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (A0). E6 adds that an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes". That holds only if Q honours the interface. If Q reaches past it into P's internals, E5's "Declared against honoured" makes the two one system, and Q is altered. Q shares a declared interface with P, so it is adjacent and is a candidate for opportunistic improvement, which is "considered, never mandated" under A14's tangent discipline. E6 says Q is in the direction with less payback, because Q stands on P rather than P on Q: "adjacency with less payback, not none". Any improvement to Q would be its own change, and an unclosed gap is still recorded (A0, RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 defines "touched" as including what a change "newly depends on", but it does not say whether an existing dependency, such as X on Y, is touched. I read it as not touched but adjacent, and this is an inference.
- The corpus does not say whether error-message text is part of a declared interface. That decides whether changing Y alters X as a consumer.
- E6 says A3 "states what it triggers" for a detected crossing. A3's "Bypass as Signal" covers reaching past a declared interface, but C3's case, where no interface exists, is covered by E5's undeclared-dependency text instead. The pointer fits that case loosely.
- A14's Mechanics say "Friction -> root cause, never workaround", while A0 says improvement beyond scope is "considered, never mandated". These agree only once you read A14 success signal 1 ("or explicitly deferred with a filed marker") together with A0's "Either way the gap is recorded". Read alone, A14 sounds stronger than A0.
