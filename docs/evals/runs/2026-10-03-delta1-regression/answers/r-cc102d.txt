### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. E5 (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured") says "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals, parsing output the interface does not promise - stays one system with it", and A0 (axioms/README.md) says that where a boundary is "written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". So for this change, O and T count as one system, and O's axioms bind your change. Your change also answers to the service that contains T ("every system containing it", A0). The internals O calls are "a contract in fact, not a declared boundary - and a change to it reaches across" (E5). The corpus asks you to treat the bypass as an A3 fault. Under "Bypass as Signal", it is "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". It "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md; E6 "Boundary against directory" points to this).

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O, and not to P. P uses the declared interface, which your change leaves as it was. A0 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". O reaches past the interface, so it is one system with T and its axioms bind (A0; E5). E5 states the split directly: "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not." The E6 blast-radius row (entities/E6-component.md) agrees: an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes", and any unit that reaches it without a declared interface is always reached. The change also answers to the service that contains T (A0, "every system containing it"). That service contains P as well, but this does not make P's own axioms bind the change.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. They are still one system. E5 says "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing". E6 says "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it". E5 "Boundaries" says "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." What has changed is how O's calls are classed. Before, they were an undeclared dependency (an A3 fault, E5 "Boundaries"). Now they are a bypass of a declared interface, so A3 "Bypass as Signal" applies: evaluate the interface, then either move O onto it or adjust the interface. NOT SETTLED: whether the change that declares the interface must also move O. A0's rule for what a change "touches" (what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and consumers whose contract it alters) does not clearly include O.

### boundary-bypass.B4
A3 "Bypass as Signal" says each bypass is "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface": it "may mean the interface falls short of the component's duty, and the consumer has not signalled the change it needs". A detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 success signal 5 adds that each detected violation "is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". Until that evaluation is resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; E5). If the interface is adjusted, that change answers to every consumer whose contract it alters, or, for a versioned interface, only to the consumers that adopt it (A0; E5). If the work is not done now, A0 says the gap is recorded with evidence and a revival trigger (RU3), because choosing larger work is the director's call (R4, AR3). NOT SETTLED: whether several bypasses call for anything beyond the evaluation for one. GUESS: several consumers bypassing the same interface makes "the interface falls short of the component's duty" the more likely reading.

### OBSERVATIONS
- Adjacency gap: E6 makes "adjacent" depend on a shared declared interface, so a bypassing O is not adjacent to T, yet it is one system with T. A0 covers a change's own scope and opportunistic fixes to adjacent components. It does not say which of these covers fixing O's bypass while changing T, and A0's definition of "touches" does not clearly include O.
- The question's premise ("does not alter T's behaviour") conflicts with E5. The internals O calls are "a contract in fact" for O, so changing those internals may alter what O depends on even though the declared interface and behaviour stay the same.
- A0 line 118 ends by calling "the undeclared dependency" an A3 fault. In the declared-but-bypassed case the dependency is not undeclared; A3 "Bypass as Signal" covers that case, but A0's wording does not.
- The E6 blast-radius row says "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface". Read literally, this misses a unit that bypasses a declared interface; E5 covers that case explicitly.
- In A3, "the component's duty and its vision" is ambiguous: "its" could mean the component's or the interface's. Also, no entry says whose vision document applies to a component that is not registered.
- A3 does not say who runs the bypass evaluation or who decides between moving the consumer and adjusting the interface.
