### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. Because O bypasses the interface, O and T are one system when you decide what your change answers to. entities/E5-system.md ("Declared against honoured") says: "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it". axioms/README.md (A0) says that where a boundary is "one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". entities/E6-component.md (Blast radius row) also counts "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface" as reached by a change.
On the bypass itself, axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md ("Bypass as Signal") calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". It says a detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted", and that "Until then the two remain one system for what a change answers to". NOT SETTLED: whether your internals change must itself carry out that evaluation or move O. GUESS: A0's "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows" points toward at least recording it, and A0 calls for an RU3 revival trigger if you defer it.

### boundary-bypass.B2
O. P does not bind your change, though the service that contains T does. entities/E5-system.md says: "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not." axioms/README.md (A0) says: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind." That rules P out, because its interface is unchanged. O is one system with T through the bypass (A0; A3 "Bypass as Signal"), so O's axioms bind. The change also answers to T itself and to "every system containing it" (A0), which here is the service. P sits inside that service, so P's traits still count through the service's axioms, but not because P is P.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. Declaring the interface separates nothing from O while O's calls still bypass it. entities/E5-system.md: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing." entities/E6-component.md: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it." entities/E5-system.md (Boundaries): "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." Until O is moved onto the interface (or the interface is adjusted, per A3 "Bypass as Signal"), T and O stay one system with the traits of both.

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat every bypass as a fault in that consumer and also as a signal about T's interface, not only as a consumer fault to correct. axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md ("Bypass as Signal") says the interface may fall "short of the component's duty". It says a detected bypass triggers "an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 Success signal 5: "each one detected is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". entities/E6-component.md ("Boundary against directory") says the same thing. Until that is resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; A0). If the work falls outside your change's scope, A0 says to record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger" (rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md) rather than skip it silently. NOT SETTLED: whether several bypassers change the procedure. GUESS: several consumers bypassing in the same way is stronger evidence that the interface, not the consumers, falls short.

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 "Bypass as Signal" says to evaluate the interface "against the component's duty and its vision". Nothing I read defines a component's vision. AR6 (Vision) is described in INDEX.md as a programme's purpose, so it is unclear which vision is meant.
- entities/E6-component.md's Blast radius row says "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface". Read literally, that covers only the undeclared case, not bypassing a declared interface. E5 and A0 explicitly cover the bypass case ("reached past"), so E6's wording is narrower than its siblings.
- Neither A0 nor A3 says who carries out the bypass evaluation, or whether a change that only touches T's internals must do it. A0's in-scope rule ("work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows") and its out-of-scope rule (record with a revival trigger) could both be read to apply, because O counts as part of the system being changed.
- When O and T are "one system", the corpus does not say how that relates to the service that already contains both. The service's axioms already bind through A0's "every system containing it", so in this setup the O-T merge may add nothing more.
