PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-ae44890f ---
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward. It is not a pass/fail test. A0: "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
When you change a system it binds, the axiom sets a direction in two parts: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" - "never backwards, and forward with every step" (A0).
In practice, new work never repeats a named gap, and work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows. Gaps beyond scope are considered and recorded, not mandated (A0).
The axioms in force bind together and cannot be traded off. Moving toward thirteen limits and away from the fourteenth "is a regression with thirteen consolations" (A0 How the set composes).
A0 names the reading it rejects as the fault *The conformance reading*: treating an axiom as pass/fail means "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent".
A gate checks only the binary part of this: that the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched" (A0).
