### system-boundary.S1
A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14, which are the launcher's floor axioms. The launcher has no traits: no state (T1), no model (T5), it is watched by its operator so it is not autonomous (T4), and it is one actor (T3). axioms/README.md says "`any-system` ... binds every system", and the matrix lists "A3 ... A4 ... A8 ... A9 ... A14" for that tag. E5 says "A one-shot script with no traits is a system, bound by the axioms every system carries". The service is not bound by this change. A0 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". E5 confirms that "A launcher that hands sessions to a service through a declared interface composes with it", and here that interface is declared, honoured and unchanged.

### system-boundary.S2
The change answers to the launcher and the service together. That is the union A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14, and not A2, A11 or A12. A0 says a change answers to "any system whose contract with it the change alters". E5 says "changing an unversioned interface alters every consumer at once, so the change answers to all of them immediately". The service is stateful, which brings in A1 (T1). It is multi-agent, which brings in A5, A6, A7, A10 and A13 (T3). It is autonomous, which brings in A7, A10 and A13 (T4). It is not declarative (no A2) and has no model in the loop (no A11, A12). E5 also says that adding versioning to the interface "is encouraged, not required; if it is not done, the gap is recorded".

### system-boundary.S3
The change answers to the service's full set: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14. A0 says a change answers to "the system it modifies, every system containing it". E5 says "A module inside a service is a system, and the service contains it, whether or not the module's interfaces are declared. A system has every trait any part it contains has". The module being stateless on its own does not remove any axiom. E5 warns against exactly that move: "a stateful system escapes its own axioms by being described as stateless modules". (T0 makes the same point under "How traits compose".)

### system-boundary.S4
A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14 only, which are the library's floor axioms (it has no traits). The ten consumers compose with the library through a declared, versioned contract that the change leaves unchanged. A0 says that in this case "its axioms do not bind". E5 says "Publishing a new version of an interface alters no consumer that has not adopted it; each adoption is a change to that consumer, and answers to that consumer's axioms". E5 "Why precision matters" rejects the opposite reading, in which "a small shared library would carry the axioms of every system that uses it". One caveat comes from E5: "A declared version range ... is adoption in advance". So if any consumer had pinned a range such as "any 2.x", a release inside that range would alter that consumer. The question says each consumer adopts as its own separate change, so I take it that no consumer has a range.

### system-boundary.S5
The launcher and the service count as one system, so the change answers to A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13 and A14 (not A2, A11, A12). E5 says "Where one unit depends on another and nothing declares the boundary between them ... they are one system, with the traits of both". A0 says the same: "the axioms either unit would bring all bind". Something else is wrong: the undeclared dependency is itself a fault. E5 and A0 both say it "is itself a fault against A3", and A3's Air-Gap Principle requires that "units interact only through declared adapters and contracts". The change also owns that boundary. E5 says "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional". So the change must declare a log/session interface and move the service's parsing onto it. Changing the format without doing that would also break the service's parsing. (That last point is my own inference; it is not text from the corpus.)

### system-boundary.S6
It is defined in entities/E5-system.md under "Definition". The text is: "A system is a unit of engineering with a boundary: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it." It continues: "The boundary separates it from a neighbour only where it is declared and honoured ... where one unit depends on another across a boundary nobody declared, or reaches past one that was declared, the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to." Traits describe a system and axioms bind it. Systems relate in exactly two ways, contain and compose. E5 "Boundaries" adds that a system is "Not a repository, a deployment unit or a process by definition" and is "Not defined by size or by traits". E6 defines a component as "a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it".

### OBSERVATIONS
- Possible inconsistency in S2 about versioning. A3 (Semantic Bit-Masking) says interfaces at the limit are "explicit, versioned contracts". A0 says a change "that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows", and defines that as "the part the change already alters". A change that rewrites an unversioned interface arguably already alters the part that is missing versioning. That reading would make versioning required. E5 says it is "encouraged, not required". This is NOT SETTLED by the text. GUESS: E5's explicit ruling is meant to win.
- E5 says the organisation doing the work is a system, with its own axioms that "govern how the change is made and recorded". It also says the organisation is "not one of the systems the change alters". My answers leave the organisation's axioms out of every list. A reader could count them in, which is a possible ambiguity in what "answers to" means.
- S1: the corpus does not say outright whether "one operator runs it and watches it" makes the launcher multi-agent. T3 lists "a human reviewing an agent's output" as multi-agent. GUESS: it does not, because a script is a tool rather than an independent actor, and the prompt says the launcher has no traits.
- E5's sentence on chaining merges (line 85) is dense, and it was hard to tell whether it limits S5. It does not seem to matter here, because only two units are involved.
- I found no conflicts between the axioms' applies-to tags, the A0 applicability matrix and the trait entries' "Axioms it brings into force" lists. I checked all fourteen frontmatter tags.
