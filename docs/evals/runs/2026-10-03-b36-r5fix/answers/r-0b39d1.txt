### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful (T1: it "writes something it will later read back") and multi-agent (T3), so A1 Sovereign State Transparency is in force, plus A5 and the rest of the multi-agent row (axioms/README.md, "Applicability matrix"); a component keeping truth only in process memory is A1's named "Hidden State Problem" ("No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth", A1 Mandate/Faults). A0 says an axiom binds every change in two directions: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" - so your own feature must keep its state in the shared, durable backplane and must never repeat the gap ("new work never repeats a gap the axiom names"). What you owe the existing component depends on whether your change touches it: "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters"; if you modify it, close "as much of it as the touch allows"; if you newly depend on it, its gaps "must not be built on" (A0, "Standing commitment"). If it is only adjacent (shares a declared interface, E6), closing it is "encouraged, never mandated" and you are "expected to weigh doing it", under A14 tangent discipline (near payback, yield captured, no significant distraction), and done "as a change of its own". If it fails that test, record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", since choosing larger work is the director's (R4, AR3); "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids." Also, if your feature reaches into that component with no declared interface, the two are one system and "declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5, Boundaries; A3). NOT SETTLED: whether this component is touched, adjacent, or neither - the question does not say, so which branch applies cannot be decided from the corpus alone.

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a direction to build toward, not a test to pass: it is "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward", and "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it" (axioms/README.md, "Standing commitment, not situated move"). When you change a system it binds, the axiom requires two things at once - never move the system backwards from the limit, and move it forward "as far as the change's own scope allows" ("Preserving and improving are one rule: never backwards, and forward with every step"). Inside your scope that means not repeating the gaps it names and closing those you touch; beyond your scope, improvement is encouraged and gaps you see are recorded rather than passed silently. Reading it as pass/fail is a named fault, "The conformance reading", because then "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent" (A0 Faults; see also the CORRECTION banner in "How the set composes"). A gate checks only the direction rule: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched". It is also not negotiable against deadlines and cannot be traded against another axiom ("It is not advice you weigh against deadlines"; "no axiom may be traded against another").

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 defines adjacency for opportunistic improvement as "one it stands on, or one that stands on it", while A14's tangent discipline describes adjacency as the infrastructure "the main line will stand on". E6 reconciles them (the stood-on direction is "closer", the other has "less payback, not none"), but a reader of A0 or A14 alone could read them as conflicting.
- A0 says a component the change newly depends on is "touched but not altered... its axioms do not bind the change", and also says "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows". For a dependency, how much the touch allows seems to be zero ("must not be built on"), but A0 does not say this directly.
- In Q1 the in-memory component sits inside the same service, so by E5 ("A system has every trait any part it contains has") the service's axioms bind any change to the service whether or not the component is touched. Touch decides only how far you must move, not whether A1 binds. The corpus supports this, but you have to put E5 and A0 together to get it.
- A1's Rationale argues from "multi-actor" systems, but A1 is tagged only `stateful`. That is consistent, but it could mislead a reader into thinking A1 needs multiple actors.
- I did not check A0's counted claims ("thirty-five relations", citation counts per file).
