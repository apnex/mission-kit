### axiom-direction.Q1
The service passes the T1 test ("owns and mutates state that persists ... that something else treats as authoritative", traits/T1-stateful.md) and the T3 test (traits/T3-multi-agent.md), so A1 plus A5/A6/A7/A10/A13 bind it on top of the any-system floor (A3, A4, A8, A9, A14), since "tags are alternatives" and traits bind by union (axioms/README.md "What brings an axiom into force"; traits/README.md). The component is a textbook A1 fault: "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth" and faults "Hidden State Problem" / "Ephemeral Truth Loss" (axioms/A1-sovereign-state-transparency.md). But because your task does not touch it, A0 does not require you to fix it: scope is "what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters; code that merely sits beside it ... is not touched", and "Beyond the scope, improvement is encouraged, never mandated" (axioms/README.md "Standing commitment"). What A0 does require is that you do not pass it silently: if it is adjacent (shares a declared interface, entities/E6-component.md) and passes A14's tangent test (near payback, yield captured, does not distract from your goal), you may close it as a separate change of its own; otherwise "recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger ([`RU3`])", and "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids." For your feature itself, "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names", so any state it introduces must be durable and queryable by other authorized actors (A1 mechanics and success signals 1-2), not held in process memory. If your feature newly depends on the component through a declared interface, "its gaps must not be built on" - so do not make your feature rely on that in-memory state; if it reaches into the component with no declared interface, the two are one system and "declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (entities/E5-system.md "Boundaries"). If your change does alter the component, you must close "as much of it as the touch allows", meaning the part your change already alters (axioms/README.md).

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward, not a pass/fail test; "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it" (axioms/README.md "Standing commitment, not situated move"). So when you change a system it binds, the axiom does two things at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" - "never backwards, and forward with every step". In practice that means new work inside your scope never repeats a gap the axiom names, and work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows; gaps beyond your scope are optionally improved (if adjacent and A14's tangent test passes) or recorded with a revival trigger, never passed silently (same section). The charter explicitly rejects reading axioms as conformance checks ("The conformance reading" fault, and the CORRECTION banner in "How the set composes"), because under that reading nothing ever moves. A gate still checks something binary, but only the direction rule: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched". All axioms in force apply together and cannot be traded against each other ("conjunctive in force").
