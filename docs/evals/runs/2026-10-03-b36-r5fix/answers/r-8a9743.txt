### component.C1
Y: X already calls Y, so your fix does not "newly depend" on Y. Y is not in the change's scope (axioms/README.md: "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters"). But Y is adjacent, because X consumes Y's declared interface (entities/E6-component.md: adjacency is "a shared declared interface"). It is also the closer direction, since X stands on Y (E6, "Adjacent against nearby"). A0 says improvement here is "encouraged, never mandated", and also that the agent "is expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". It passes A14's tangent discipline: payback is near, the work stands on Y, and five minutes does not displace the goal. So you may fix it, "as a change of its own, so its yield is captured", and that change answers to every system it alters (A0). Z: X does not use Z, so Z is "nearby and not adjacent" (E6). Two days would also "displace the goal". So Z fails the test, and you must record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", leaving the choice to the director (A0, citing R4/AR3). Either way, A0 forbids passing either gap silently ("Either way the gap is recorded").

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it". It "has one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part", and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". Its boundary is those declared interfaces. It does not differ in kind: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing" (E6). The difference is which question you ask. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system". "Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6 Discriminators; E5 says the same). A system that nothing contains "is not a component of anything" (E6). Separately, E6 says a component registered in components/ (C0) is only a property of a few components, not part of the definition.

### component.C3
No. E6 says "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces". E5 agrees: "A directory places a component's documents; it is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". parts/thing/ is still a component, "but nothing separates it yet" (E6 Definition). Because parts/other/ depends on it with no declared boundary, the two are "one system, with the traits of both" for deciding what a change answers to (entities/E5-system.md, Boundaries; A0). What is wrong: the undeclared dependency "is itself a fault against A3" (E5). A3's Air-Gap Principle says "no unit reaches into another's internals". The fix is to declare thing's interface and move other onto it. Evaluate that interface against thing's duty, and adjust it to what other legitimately needs (E5; A3 "Bypass as Signal"). A change that alters what other reaches into "owns that boundary" (E5).

### component.C4
Your change answers to P's system and to "every system containing it", which means the service and anything containing the service (A0, "An axiom binds a system..."; E5 Contain). Q is not altered, and its axioms do not bind your change: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered" (A0). E6's blast-radius row agrees: a change reaches "an adjacent component only if the interface between them changes". That holds only if Q honours the interface. If Q reaches past it into P's internals, Q and P are one system and Q is reached (E5, "Declared against honoured"). Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement, because Q consumes P's declared interface. That makes Q adjacent to P, which you touched, and A0 counts "one that stands on it" as adjacent. It is the lower-payback direction: "the other direction is adjacency with less payback, not none" (E6). So weigh it under A14's tangent discipline. Then either fix it as a separate change, or record it with a revival trigger (A0, RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- C1: The corpus does not say whether an error message is part of Y's declared interface. If it is, the five-minute fix alters Y's consumers and answers to their axioms too (A0; E5 on versioned contracts). NOT SETTLED.
- C1: The scope rule covers what a change "newly depends on". It is not clear whether a fix that keeps using an existing dependency (X->Y) counts as touching Y. I read it as not touching Y, which makes Y adjacent, not in scope.
- C1/Z: A1 would bind the service through containment (E5: "A system has every trait any part it contains has"). That happens only if Z's in-process state is "stateful" under T1, which requires persistence beyond a single operation plus authority. "Hidden in process memory" leaves this NOT SETTLED. It does not change the answer, because Z is outside the change's scope either way.
- A0 says beyond-scope improvement is "never mandated", yet recording the gap is effectively mandatory ("Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids"). This is consistent, but a quick reader could take "never mandated" to mean nothing is owed.
- A3's "Bypass as Signal" is written for reaching past a declared interface. The C3 case, where no interface exists, is handled by E5's undeclared-dependency text instead. The two remedies match, but they live in different files.
- INDEX.md tells the reader to fetch from a remote URL when no checkout is present. That is irrelevant in this sandbox and was not used.
