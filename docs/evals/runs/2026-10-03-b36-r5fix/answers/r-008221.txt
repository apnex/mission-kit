### component.C1
Y: the fix to X does not modify Y, newly depend on it, or alter its contract, so Y is outside the change's scope (axioms/README.md: "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters"). But Y is adjacent, and X stands on it through a declared interface, which entities/E6-component.md calls "the closer for compounding learning". So A0 asks you to weigh fixing it: "Beyond the scope, improvement is encouraged, never mandated ... is expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". It passes A14's tangent discipline: the payback is near (the vague message cost 20 minutes), the cost is 5 minutes, and A14 says "Friction -> root cause, never workaround". If you do it, make it "a change of its own" (A0), separate from the unlanded X fix, capture the yield, and let it answer to every system it alters. Z: X does not use Z, so Z is at most "nearby", not adjacent (E6: "nearby and not adjacent"). It also costs two days, which "displace[s] the goal". So A0 says to record Z "with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", and the director (R4, AR3) decides whether to do it. Z's hidden in-process state is an A1 gap ("No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth"). A1 binds the service, because the service contains Z (E5: "A system has every trait any part it contains has"), and so it binds your X change too. It only asks that the change not repeat or worsen that gap; it does not ask you to close it. "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (A0).

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it", with "one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part", and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". entities/E5-system.md defines a system as "a unit of engineering with a boundary". It is not a different kind of thing: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system". The difference is which question you are asking. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6). A system that nothing contains, such as a whole product seen from outside, "is not a component of anything" (E6). A component is not a file, directory, package or registered `components/` entry by definition, and it is not a SysML part (E6 Boundaries).

### component.C3
No. "A component's directory addresses it ... Its boundary is its declared interfaces" (E6). E5 adds: "A directory places a component's documents; it is the boundary of neither the system nor the component." Nothing declares an interface, so "nothing separates it yet". For deciding what a change answers to, parts/other/ and parts/thing/ are "one system, with the traits of both" (E5 Boundaries; A0). The direct reach into internals is a fault against A3's Air-Gap Principle ("no unit reaches into another's internals"). E5 calls the undeclared dependency "itself a fault against A3". Under A3's "Bypass as Signal", the remedy is to declare an interface and evaluate it against parts/thing/'s duty. Then either move parts/other/ onto it or adjust the interface to what it legitimately needs. A change that alters what parts/other/ reaches into "owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5).

### component.C4
The change answers to P and to "every system containing it": the service and anything above it (A0: "a change answers to every system it alters"; E5 Contain). Q is not altered: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (A0). E6's blast radius agrees: "an adjacent component only if the interface between them changes". This holds only if Q actually honours the interface. If Q reaches past it, or depends on undeclared behaviour (E5: "a contract in fact"), the two are one system (E5 "Declared against honoured"). Q is adjacent, because it shares a declared interface with P (E6), so it is a candidate for opportunistic improvement under A0/A14 tangent discipline. It is the weaker direction, though: Q stands on P, not the other way round, and E6 calls this "adjacency with less payback, not none". If you improve Q, do it as a separate change. Otherwise, record a gap you saw in Q with a revival trigger (A0, RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 says a change touches "what it newly depends on". It does not say whether an existing dependency the change keeps using (Y in C1) is touched. I treated Y as out of scope but adjacent. NOT SETTLED whether a pre-existing dependency is in scope.
- NOT SETTLED whether an error message's wording is part of Y's declared interface. If consumers match on it, changing it would alter their contract and they would be altered systems (A0). GUESS: wording is usually not promised by the interface.
- A14's tangent discipline defines adjacency in one direction only ("sits under load-bearing infrastructure the main line will stand on"). A0 allows both directions ("one it stands on, or one that stands on it"). E6 reconciles them with a payback gradient, but A14 read alone suggests the reverse direction (C4's Q) does not qualify.
- The C1 Z case mixes two things. A1 binds the X change through the containing service, but Z's gap is outside the change's scope. A reader could wrongly conclude that A1 obliges the X fix to close Z.
