### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful (`traits/T1-stateful.md`), so `A1` is in force. It is also multi-agent, so `A5`, `A6`, `A7`, `A10` and `A13` are in force too, along with the any-system axioms (`axioms/README.md`, "Applicability matrix"). The in-memory component is exactly the gap `A1` names: "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth". The faults that apply are "Hidden State Problem" and "Ephemeral Truth Loss" (`axioms/A1-sovereign-state-transparency.md`). For your feature, `A0` requires that "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names". So the state your feature adds must live in the shared, durable backplane, where other agents can query it, and must not stay in process memory. What you owe the component depends on whether your change touches it, not on whether the task mentions it. If the change modifies the component, newly depends on it, or alters its contract, it is in scope: close "as much of it as the touch allows". If you only depend on it through a declared interface, do not build on its gap. If the component is beyond scope but adjacent (`entities/E6-component.md`), you are "expected to weigh" closing it, as a separate change, under `A14` tangent discipline. Otherwise, record it with evidence and a revival trigger (`rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md`), because "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (`axioms/README.md`, "Standing commitment, not situated move").

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is not a pass-or-fail test. It is an "asymptote - a limit a system is built toward", and "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it" (`axioms/README.md`, "Standing commitment, not situated move"). So when you change a system an axiom binds, the axiom gives the change a direction in two parts: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows." The change's scope (what it modifies, newly depends on, or whose contract it alters) sets how far you must move. Beyond that scope, improvement is "encouraged, never mandated". An axiom is a "standing commitment" that holds for every task, not advice you weigh against deadlines. And no axiom can be traded against another: "a change that moves the system toward thirteen limits and away from the fourteenth is a regression with thirteen consolations" (`axioms/README.md`, "How the set composes"). Reading an axiom as a conformance test is itself a named fault, because then "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent" (`axioms/README.md`, "Faults").
