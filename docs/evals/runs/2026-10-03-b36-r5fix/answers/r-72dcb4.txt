### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful and multi-agent, so A1 Sovereign State Transparency is in force (traits/T1-stateful.md; axioms/README.md applicability matrix), along with A5, A6, A7, A10 and A13 (traits/T3-multi-agent.md) and the any-system axioms. The component is contained by the service, and "a system has every trait any part it contains has" (entities/E5-system.md), so your change answers to the service's axioms: "a change answers to every system it alters ... every system containing it" (axioms/README.md). Process-memory-only truth is exactly A1's named fault: "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth" ("Hidden State Problem", "Ephemeral Truth Loss", axioms/A1-sovereign-state-transparency.md). For your own feature, the direction rule says "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names", so keep its state in the durable, queryable backplane and keep the components that act on it stateless (A1 Mechanics; axioms/README.md). Whether you must fix the component depends on whether your change touches it, not on whether your task mentions it: you are obliged only if you modify it, newly depend on it, or alter its contract, and then only to close "as much of it as the touch allows". If you depend on it without altering it, do not build on the gap. If it is only nearby, fixing it is "encouraged, never mandated". If it is adjacent (it shares a declared interface with what you touch), you may close it as a separate change under A14 tangent discipline; if not, record it with evidence and a revival trigger (rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md), because "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (axioms/README.md).

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom gives a change its direction. It is not a pass-or-fail test. The corpus calls an axiom "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward", and says a system "is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it" (axioms/README.md, "Standing commitment, not situated move"). So when you change a system it binds, the axiom does two jobs at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" - "never backwards, and forward with every step". The charter keeps a correction banner because readers once treated axioms as pass/fail invariants, which "gives no direction". It names that reading as a fault ("The conformance reading"): it leaves the gaps permanent. A gate checks only the direction: whether the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched". The success signals in each axiom are what you measure to show the change moved toward the limit. Because the axioms are "conjunctive in force", you cannot trade one limit against another.

### OBSERVATIONS
- axioms/README.md says a dependency that is "touched but not altered" is in scope ("its gaps must not be built on, and its axioms do not bind the change"). For a component inside the same service, this sits awkwardly with E5, where the containing service's axioms bind anyway. The corpus does not plainly say whether newly depending on a sibling component inside one system creates any obligation to close its gap. "As much as the touch allows" is defined as "the part the change already alters", which would be nothing for a pure dependency. I read it that way, but it is an inference.
- A1's Mandate is written as an absolute invariant ("All system truth lives in a single ... backplane"). A0 frames axioms as asymptotes. The two are reconciled only by the success-signals preamble and A0, so a reader of A1 alone could fall into the conformance reading.
- A14 tangent discipline defines adjacency in one direction only ("sits under load-bearing infrastructure the main line will stand on"). A0 and E6 allow both directions. E6 reconciles the two, but A14 alone reads narrower.
- The scenario does not say whether the component's boundary is declared. If your feature reaches into it with no declared interface, E5 says your change "owns that boundary", which would make declaring the interface mandatory. My answer is conditional on this.
- I did not verify A0's numeric claims ("thirty-five relations ... fifteen fall within a row", the citation counts).
