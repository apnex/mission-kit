### component.C1
Neither Y nor Z is in your change's scope: axioms/README.md (A0) says "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", and X already depended on Y and does not use Z at all, so "Beyond the scope, improvement is encouraged, never mandated" applies to both, but "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids."
Y is adjacent to X: they share a declared interface and X stands on Y, which E6 calls "the closer" direction for compounding learning. It passes A0's tangent test ("the payback is near, strongest where the work stands on the component; the yield is captured; and the cost does not significantly distract"), backed by A14 "Friction -> root cause, never workaround". So you are "expected to weigh" fixing Y, and should, "as a change of its own", separate from the X fix, answering "to every system it alters" (A0; A3 "Composable by Default").
Z is "nearby and not adjacent" (E6 "Adjacent against nearby"), and two days would displace the goal. So A0 says it is "recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed, because larger choices of what work happens next are the director's (R4, AR3)."
The gap in Z is A1's "Hidden State Problem" ("No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth"). A1 binds because the service contains Z and "has every trait any part it contains has" (E5), as long as Z passes T1's stateful test. Your X change therefore answers to A1 too: it must not build on or repeat Z's gap, but it does not have to close it.
NOT SETTLED: whether making Y's error message more precise alters Y's interface. GUESS: if any consumer depends on the message text, it is "a contract in fact" (E5), and that consumer is altered too.

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it. It has one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part, and it exposes and consumes interfaces".
"Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing" (E6).
The difference is the question you are asking. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6 "Component against system"; E5 says the same under "A system against a component").
A system is "a unit of engineering with a boundary" (E5). A system that nothing contains "is not a component of anything" (E6).
E6 also rules out other readings: a component is not a file, module, package, repository or deployable "by definition", not a SysML part, and not necessarily registered or shareable. The components/ layer registers only "a few" (E6 Boundaries).

### component.C3
No. E6 "Boundary against directory" says: "A component's directory addresses it ... Its boundary is its declared interfaces". E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component."
With no interface declared, parts/thing/ "is still a component, but nothing separates it yet" (E6 Definition). parts/other/ "has crossed the boundary, and it stays one system with the component until it uses the interface" (E6). For deciding what a change answers to, the two are "one system, with the traits of both" (E5 Boundaries; A0).
What is wrong is the undeclared dependency, which "is itself a fault against A3" (E5). It breaks A3's Air-Gap Principle: "no unit reaches into another's internals".
The fix is to declare the interface and move the dependency onto it: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again" (E5).
Until then, a change to parts/thing/ that alters what parts/other/ reaches into "owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5).
NOT SETTLED: how the new interface is designed. A3's "Bypass as Signal" evaluation is written for a declared interface that someone reached past; here nothing was declared. GUESS: shape it to the component's one duty and to what parts/other/ legitimately needs.

### component.C4
Your change answers to P and to every system that contains P, so the service's axioms bind it: "a change answers to every system it alters ... the system it modifies, every system containing it" (A0; E6 "Blast radius").
Q is not altered. "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (A0). E6 agrees: the change reaches an adjacent component "only if the interface between them changes".
One caveat: the service contains Q and "has every trait any part it contains has" (E5). Whatever axioms Q's traits bring therefore still bind your change, but through the service, not because Q changed (E5: "units inside one system need no chaining").
This holds only if Q actually uses the interface. If Q reaches past it into P's internals, the two are "one system" (E5 "Declared against honoured"), and the internal change could break Q.
Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement: it is adjacent ("one consumes what the other exposes", E6), and A0 covers both "one it stands on, or one that stands on it". Q stands on P, though, which is "adjacency with less payback, not none" (E6). So it is encouraged, not mandated, and goes through A0's tangent test: closed as a separate change, or else recorded with a revival trigger (RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 says improvement beyond scope is "encouraged, never mandated" and, in the next sentence, that an agent "is expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". These are compatible: weighing is the duty, doing is not. But the two sentences read as being in tension.
- The corpus does not say whether an error message is part of a declared interface. That decides whether improving Y alters X and Y's other consumers (C1).
- T1 requires state that "persists beyond a single operation", and E6 does not discuss in-process memory. Whether Z's process-memory state makes the service stateful, and so brings A1 into force, is a judgement call against T1's test.
- A3's "Bypass as Signal" covers reaching past a declared interface. The case where nothing was declared (C3) is covered only by E5's general "declare and move the dependency" remedy. The corpus gives no guidance on shaping the first interface.
- A10's trigger ("Friction has surfaced during work") matches C1, but A10 applies only to multi-agent or autonomous systems. So it may not bind an ordinary service, while A14 (any-system) does.
- I did not open C0, which E6 cites as "the source of duty is singular at an altitude", so I have not checked that citation.
