### component.C1
Y: Y is adjacent to X: they share a declared interface, and X stands on Y. E6 says "Of two adjacent components, the one the other stands on is the closer for compounding learning" (entities/E6-component.md). Your change does not touch Y. A0 says a change touches "what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", and X's dependency on Y is not new (axioms/README.md). So for Y, improvement "is encouraged, never mandated", and you are "expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". The vague error is friction that cost twenty minutes, and the fix takes five, so it meets A14's tangent discipline: payback is near, the cost is small, and you must capture the yield. A0 then says to close it "as a change of its own", which answers to every system it alters. GUESS: if callers depend on the exact error text as part of Y's interface, they are altered too. Z: Z is not adjacent ("X does not use it"; adjacency needs a shared declared interface, E6), and two days of work would push aside the goal of your fix. So A0 says to record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", because what work comes next is the director's choice (R4, AR3). If Z's in-memory state passes T1's test, the service that contains Z is stateful and A1 binds it ("No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth"). Your X change must not move further from A1, but it does not have to close Z's gap. Either way the gap is recorded: "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids."

### component.C2
entities/E6-component.md defines it: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it." It has "one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part", and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". Its boundary is those interfaces, where they are declared and the neighbour uses them. It is not a different kind of thing: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system". The difference is the question you ask. Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component (E6 Discriminators; E5 "A system against a component"). A system that nothing contains, such as a whole product seen from outside, is not a component. E5 (entities/E5-system.md) defines system, contain and compose.

### component.C3
No. E6 says "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". The fault: parts/other reaches into the internals of parts/thing, and no interface is declared. That breaks A3's Air-Gap Principle ("no unit reaches into another's internals"). E5 calls it "itself a fault against A3". Until it is fixed, thing is "still a component, but nothing separates it yet". For deciding what a change answers to, thing and other are one system with the traits of both (E6 Definition; E5 Boundaries; A0). The remedy is to declare thing's interface and move other onto it (E5: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again"). A change that alters what other reaches into "owns that boundary": declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional (E5).

### component.C4
Your change answers to P and to every system that contains P, including the service. No consumer contract changes, so no other system is altered (A0: "the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters"). Q is not altered: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (A0). E6's blast-radius row agrees. One subtlety: the service contains Q, and E5 says the containing system "has the traits of every part it contains". So axioms that Q's traits bring in can still bind your change through the service, but not because Q is altered. Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement. It is adjacent to P, and A0 counts "one that stands on it, through a declared interface". E6 adds that this direction is "adjacency with less payback, not none", so it must pass A14's tangent test, be made as a change of its own, and be recorded either way. This assumes Q honours the interface. If Q reaches past it, P and Q are one system (E5 "Declared against honoured").

### OBSERVATIONS
- C1: A0 says the Y gap may be closed "opportunistically", but it also says Y's gaps "must not be built on" only for a component the change *newly* depends on. Whether Y counts as touched, given that X already depends on it and the change was diagnosed through it, is not explicit. NOT SETTLED. I read it as not touched.
- T1 requires state that "persists beyond a single operation". It is unclear whether "important state hidden in process memory" passes, because it would be lost on restart. I treated it as stateful because it passes the test, but this is a GUESS.
- Whether an error message is part of a declared interface is NOT SETTLED, and that decides whether improving Y alters Y's consumers.
- A14 states adjacency in one direction only ("sits under load-bearing infrastructure the main line will stand on"). A0 and E6 allow both directions. E6 reconciles the two, but A14 read alone suggests the narrower reading.
- In C4, A0 says Q's axioms "do not bind", while E5 says the containing service has Q's traits. These can be reconciled (binding comes through the service, not through Q being altered), but a cold reader could read them as conflicting.
