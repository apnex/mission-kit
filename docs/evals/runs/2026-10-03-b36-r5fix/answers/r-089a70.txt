### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a limit, or "asymptote", that the system is built toward, and it is not a test the change has to pass (axioms/README.md: "a limit a system is built toward ... a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it"). When you change a system it binds, the axiom gives your change a direction, and it works two ways: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows." So it does two jobs together: it stops regression, and it requires forward movement inside whatever your change touches, so new work "never repeats a gap the axiom names". Outside that scope it encourages improvement but does not require it, and it still forbids passing a gap silently. The charter explicitly retracted the pass/fail reading under a CORRECTION banner, and it names that reading as a fault ("The conformance reading"), because under it "no change advances the system". All axioms in force bind at once, and no axiom may be traded against another ("a regression with thirteen consolations"). A gate checks only the binary part: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched."

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 says improvement beyond scope is "never mandated", but then says "Either way the gap is recorded" and "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids". So recording is mandatory even when fixing is not. This is consistent if you read carefully, but the "never mandated" headline makes it easy to miss the recording duty.
- A0 says a component the change newly depends on through a declared interface is "touched but not altered ... its axioms do not bind the change". Read alone, this seems to let a component inside the same service escape. It does not: the containing service's axioms still bind through containment (E5). The interaction is not stated where that sentence is.
- A0 says "Either way the gap is recorded" but does not say where the record goes. A14 lists candidate places (backlog entries, defect records) and RU3 requires a revival trigger. No single destination is named.
- The scenario's "invisible to the other agents" could also be read as an A5 (Perceptual Parity) gap, not only an A1 one. The corpus does not say which axiom owns that overlap. A0 calls the axioms "orthogonal in statement", which leaves open whether one defect can be a fault under two axioms.
