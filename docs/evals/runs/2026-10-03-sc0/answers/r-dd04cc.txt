### sc0.Q1
Not in `spec`. schemas/README.md (SC0), "Resource convention", says: "`spec` holds all configuration that affects entity meaning or behaviour" and "Runtime observations do not belong in `spec`. A kind only gains `status` when a real reconciler needs observed state."
So observed state such as a last-checked time goes in a `status` block, and that block is added only once a real reconciler needs it.
No existing kind has one: question.schema.json and context-frame.schema.json both set `additionalProperties: false` at the top level and list only apiVersion/kind/metadata/spec, so adding `status` means changing the schema.
It also does not belong in `metadata`. That holds "portable identity and machine-oriented labels or annotations" (SC0), and the ContextFrame section lists "observed state" among the things that "remain outside the resource".
NOT SETTLED: the shape of a `status` block. No schema in the set defines one.

### sc0.Q2
No. SC0, "Question": "It contains no Survey, round, Director, interpretation, or outcome-axis semantics" and "`metadata.name` identifies a Question, while a composing process owns placement such as round and ordinal."
"Composition" says a process "must not inject process fields into the Question resource". It shows a `SurveyQuestion` wrapper holding the whole Question under `spec.question` and the round under `spec.survey.round`. This "keeps the neutral resource closed and prevents Survey evolution from changing the Question contract."
The schema enforces this mechanically: question.schema.json has `additionalProperties: false` on both the root and `spec`.
Putting it in a label or annotation is also ruled out if respondents see the round, because "Labels and annotations must not carry text that a respondent needs in order to answer correctly."
NOT SETTLED: the `SurveyQuestion` kind is described only as "a future" one. No schema for it exists in schemas/ or catalog.json.

### sc0.Q3
Structural validation (JSON Schema) comes first, then semantic validation (the kind's `semanticValidator` in catalog.json). SC0, "Validation": "A consumer must run structural validation before semantic validation."
The stated division of labour: "JSON Schema validates structure", and the semantic validators check "ordered-array invariants that JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 cannot express" (unique option IDs, satisfiable cardinality, duplicate scope, givens and terms).
NOT SETTLED: the text gives the order without stating why.
GUESS: the semantic validators assume input that is already structurally valid. For example, question.validator.mjs reads `option.id` and `constraint.optionIds` without checking their types, so on malformed input it could throw or report misleading issues.
SC0 also says consumers "can preload every schema listed in `catalog.json` to resolve absolute URN references", which structural validation of the `$ref` fragments needs.

### sc0.Q4
NOT SETTLED: SC0 never names kinds of contract, even though E4 makes a "Kinds" section conditionally required "when members come in more than one sort".
Two distinctions can be read from the files themselves.
(1) catalog.json `role`: "fragment" (resource-metadata, choice-response; these are only `$ref`'d) versus "resource" (question, context-frame, catalog-entry, standing-context, skill, entry-body).
(2) Shape: enveloped resources with `apiVersion: schemas.mission-kit/v1alpha1`, `kind`, `metadata`, `spec` (SC4 Question, SC5 ContextFrame, SC6 EntryBody) versus frontmatter contracts, which are bare objects with no envelope and validate a markdown document's YAML header (SC1 catalog-entry, SC2 standing-context, SC3 skill; their titles say "frontmatter").
A third split: only Question and ContextFrame appear under catalog.json `resources` with a `semanticValidator`. EntryBody is enveloped but is not listed there, and its enforcement is `tools/check-entry-body.sh` (SC6).
GUESS: the useful kinds are "envelope resource with semantic validator" (SC4, SC5), "envelope resource enforced by a corpus tool" (SC6), "frontmatter contract" (SC1, SC2, SC3) and "fragment" (common metadata, choice-response). The fragments carry no SC ID.

### sc0.Q5
It is a change to the existing contract SC1 (catalog-entry.schema.json), not a new schema.
The root README, "Adding and retiring a layer", lists among admission costs: "Entry of its contents into the contract in schemas/, if the layer is ID-bearing."
The catalog-entry schema already handles fields used by only one layer inside the one contract: `sovereignty` and `internalisation-trigger` (components), `trace` (rules) and `members` (sets), each added to `properties` and required through an `allOf` if/then keyed on `category` and `id`.
The change must also extend the `category` enum and the `id` prefix pattern. Without that, `additionalProperties: false` means the new fields, the new category and the new prefix would all fail validation.
SC6 gives the same reasoning for its own single resource: splitting per category "would fork the mechanism into copies free to drift". The root README's "forked constraint" fault names the same risk.
NOT SETTLED: whether such an addition needs a new version (beyond v1alpha1). No versioning policy is stated.

### sc0.Q6
Yes. Example: schemas/catalog.json itself. SC0 says it "maps resource identity to schema identity and its semantic validator", and consumers preload from it. No schema in catalog.json governs its own shape (`catalogVersion`, `schemas[].role`, `resources[]`), and no SC entry covers it.
Other examples: bundles/*.yaml. bundles/README.md says they are "managed by convention on filename" and tools/skill-graph.mjs checks only that each `skills:` entry names a real skill; no SC contract governs them.
Partial cases: skills/arc-lifecycle/assets/workgraph-lifecycle-v1.json and workgraph-skill-selection-v1.json declare `$schema` (the JSON Schema meta-schema) but live outside schemas/ and are not in catalog.json.
The SC6 charters shape is also only loosely governed. In entry-body.schema.json, `charters` has no `default`/`enum` constraints and is not required.

### sc0.Q7
NOT SETTLED by SC0: schemas/README.md has no `Faults` section. entry-body.json `spec.charters` requires one of every charter ("Territory", "What earns an entry", "Faults", "Index"), and SC0 carries only the Index (as "Contracts").
E4 defines what the section is for. Population faults are "visible only across the set", such as "a member admitted for importance rather than fit, two members restating one rule, a population that has stopped being searched". E3 adds that with no territory, a population "can be audited for validity and never for completeness".
GUESS, applying those definitions and the root README Faults to this set: two contracts restating one rule (the "forked constraint"); a structured file the corpus depends on that no contract governs (see Q6); process fields leaking into neutral resources against the Composition rule; catalog.json drifting from the schema files or the SC entries; and a contract whose enforcer or test nothing runs (the "unread checker").
In practice that would show up as package.json `test` missing a contract's suite, or a schema in catalog.json with no SC entry, or the reverse.

### OBSERVATIONS
- SC0 (schemas/README.md) lacks the charter sections entry-body.json requires ("Territory", "What earns an entry", "Faults"). It also has no "Kinds" section, although its members are visibly of more than one sort. tools/check-charter-shape.sh is advisory, so this does not block.
- The SC0 "Layout" tree lists only common/, context-frame/ and question/. It omits catalog-entry/, standing-context/, skill/ and entry-body/, which exist and are in catalog.json.
- catalog.json gives `role: "resource"` to the frontmatter contracts (catalog-entry, standing-context, skill), but they do not follow the apiVersion/kind/metadata/spec "Resource convention". EntryBody does follow it, but is missing from the catalog.json `resources` list. So "resource" means two different things.
- The catalog-entry.schema.json `description` lists eight categories and says "methodology", but the enum has seventeen and uses "method".
- SC1 to SC5 bodies begin with a lowercase sentence copied from the title, which looks generated. SC0 `related` names only SC1 and SC6.
- The Q3 rationale (why structural validation comes before semantic) is not stated anywhere I read.
- check-charter-shape.sh says the debt is "Tracked as B4", but the backlog uses MREQ-n IDs. E3 and E4 say "thirteen charters", but INDEX.md shows eighteen ID-bearing layers (point-in-time content, which the root README's admission rule forbids).
- The root README "Adding an entry" step 5 says to add the entry to INDEX.md by hand, which contradicts INDEX.md's generated region ("do not edit by hand"). bundles/README.md lists entry prefixes as only A, S, M, R, D, W, P, K, which is stale.
- The SC6 schema leaves `spec.charters` far less constrained than `categories` (no pattern on sections, not required).
