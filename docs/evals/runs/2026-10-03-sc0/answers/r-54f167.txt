### sc0.Q1
Not in `spec`: schemas/README.md (SC0), "Resource convention", says "Runtime observations do not belong in `spec`. A kind only gains `status` when a real reconciler needs observed state."
So observed state such as a last-checked time goes in a top-level `status` stanza, and only once a real reconciler needs it.
It does not go in `metadata` either, which "holds portable identity and machine-oriented labels or annotations".
Neither existing resource has that stanza today: question.schema.json and context-frame.schema.json are `additionalProperties: false` at the top level, and tests/question/question-sovereignty.test.mjs asserts "Question has no observed status contract" (the context-frame sovereignty test asserts the same).
SC0 also says of ContextFrame that "observed state remain[s] outside the resource".
So for Question or ContextFrame now, the observation lives outside the resource, for example in a composing wrapper, until a reconciler justifies adding `status` to that kind.

### sc0.Q2
No.
SC0 says `Question` "contains no Survey, round, Director, interpretation, or outcome-axis semantics", and that "a composing process owns placement such as round and ordinal".
Under "Composition" it says a process "must not inject process fields into the Question resource", and it gives a `SurveyQuestion` wrapper that carries `spec.question` (the whole Question) next to `spec.survey.round` and `ordinal`.
The stated reason is that "this wrapper boundary keeps the neutral resource closed and prevents Survey evolution from changing the Question contract".
The schema enforces this mechanically: `spec` and the root are `additionalProperties: false`, and question-sovereignty.test.mjs forbids Survey vocabulary in the schema.
Putting it in a label or annotation would also be injection into the resource, which the "must not inject" rule covers.

### sc0.Q3
Structural validation (JSON Schema, via the schema `catalog.json` maps to the resource's apiVersion/kind) runs first, then the semantic validator: SC0 says "A consumer must run structural validation before semantic validation."
Consumers can preload every schema in `catalog.json` so absolute URN `$ref`s resolve.
The prose gives no reason for the order, so the "why" is NOT SETTLED by the text.
The source shows the mechanism: semantic validators assume structure and fail open on malformed input.
`validateQuestionSemantics` in question/v1alpha1/question.validator.mjs returns `[]` whenever `spec.response.type !== "Choice"`.
Both validators also replace non-array fields with `[]` (`Array.isArray(...) ? ... : []`).
So a semantic pass alone would report a structurally broken resource as clean.
The semantic layer only adds the invariants SC0 says Draft 2020-12 "cannot express", such as unique option IDs and satisfiable cardinality.

### sc0.Q4
NOT SETTLED: SC0 names no kinds and has no Kinds section, although E4 (entities/E4-charter.md) makes "Kinds" a required concern when "members come in more than one sort".
The measured distinctions are these.
(a) catalog.json marks each schema `"role": "fragment"` (resource-metadata, choice-response; these have no SC entry) or `"role": "resource"`.
(b) Some contracts use the apiVersion/kind/metadata/spec envelope from SC0's "Resource convention": SC4 Question, SC5 ContextFrame and SC6 EntryBody.
Others are bare frontmatter objects with no envelope: SC1 catalog-entry, SC2 standing-context and SC3 skill.
(c) Among the envelope resources, only Question and ContextFrame appear in catalog.json `resources` with a `semanticValidator`; EntryBody is enforced by tools/check-entry-body.sh instead.
GUESS: the intended kinds may be "process-neutral domain resources" (SC4, SC5) against "corpus-governance contracts" that check files in this repo (SC1, SC2, SC3, SC6), with fragments as building blocks; the text does not say this.
SC0 is the charter, not a contract.

### sc0.Q5
It is a change to an existing contract, SC1 (catalog-entry/v1alpha1/catalog-entry.schema.json), not a new schema.
The root README, "Adding and retiring a layer", lists "Entry of its contents into the contract in schemas/, if the layer is ID-bearing" as one of the costs of admitting a layer.
SC1 describes itself as "the frontmatter every catalogue entry must satisfy".
It already holds layer-specific fields with per-category `if/then` clauses (for example `trace` required for `RU`, `members` for `ST`, `sovereignty` for `C`).
It is `additionalProperties: false`, so the two new fields fail validation until they are added there, along with the new `category` enum value and `id` prefix.
SC6 states the same reasoning for body shape: one resource "rather than one per category, because ... splitting it would fork the mechanism into copies free to drift". The root README's Faults section names this "the forked constraint".
GUESS: the new fields would get their own `if/then` clause keyed on the new category, following the RU/ST/C precedent.

### sc0.Q6
Yes.
schemas/catalog.json itself has no schema: catalog.json lists none for it, and its only checks are the partial assertions in tests/question/question-sovereignty.test.mjs ("schema catalog uniquely maps every declared ID to a portable file").
The bundle files (bundles/*.yaml) are another case.
bundles/README.md says they are "managed by convention on filename", and tools/skill-graph.mjs checks only that each `skills:` entry names a real skill.
No contract governs their `name`/`description`/`skills` shape or the "no harness-specific field" rule.
Also, SC6's own declaration does not govern every category: its enum covers only axiom, entity, component, artifact and trait. It calls the rest "ungoverned by design ... a stated gap rather than a silent one", so that gap is deliberate.

### OBSERVATIONS
- SC0 (schemas/README.md) lacks the charter sections that entry-body.json `charters` requires ("Territory", "What earns an entry", "Faults", "Index"). It also lacks the "Kinds" concern that E4 calls for, though its members are of several sorts.
- E4's fixed charter headings (Purpose, Territory, Faults, Index) disagree with entry-body.json `charters.sections` (Territory, What earns an entry, Faults, Index). That is the "enforcement and the thing it enforced ... drifting apart" failure E4 itself describes.
- SC0 says "this contract does not use [JSON Schema `default`] as implicit configuration". But entry-body.schema.json gives `ordered` a `"default": true`, and check-entry-body.sh treats absence as ordered. standing-context.schema.json says "Defaults are applied when absent". Whether "this contract" means only Question/ContextFrame is ambiguous.
- SC0's "Layout" tree omits the catalog-entry/, standing-context/, skill/ and entry-body/ directories and their tests.
- catalog.json `resources` lists Question and ContextFrame but not EntryBody, though EntryBody uses the same apiVersion/kind envelope.
- The catalog-entry.schema.json `description` lists eight categories, but its `category` enum has seventeen. bundles/README.md likewise lists only eight entry prefixes.
- The `entry-body.schema.json` `charters` sub-object carries no descriptions, unlike the rest of that schema.
- The SC1-SC5 stub bodies open with a lowercase, pasted title fragment ("the frontmatter every catalogue entry must satisfy.").
- check-charter-shape.sh says the debt is "tracked as B4". A search of backlog/ for "B4" found nothing (backlog IDs are MREQ-n), so the pointer does not resolve within this corpus.
- The root README links docs/README.md, which does not exist in this corpus copy.
