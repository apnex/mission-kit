### system-boundary.S1
Only the launcher's axioms: the floor, A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14 (axioms/README.md applicability matrix, `any-system` row; frontmatter of each axiom checked and agrees).
The launcher has no traits: no persistent state (T1), one actor (T3: "One actor using several tools is not multi-agent"), a human watching (T4 test), no model (T5), no reconciled spec (T2) - and E5 says "A one-shot script with no traits is a system, bound by the axioms every system carries."
The service is not bound: A0 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind", and E5 gives exactly this launcher/service pair as its *compose* example.
Within that scope the direction rule of A0 applies: the change must not move the launcher away from any of those five limits and moves it toward them as far as its scope allows.

### system-boundary.S2

A0: "a change answers to every system it alters... and any system whose contract with it the change alters"; E6's blast-radius row says an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes".
The service is stateful (T1 -> A1), multi-agent (T3 -> A5, A6, A7, A10, A13) and autonomous (T4 -> A7, A10, A13), and traits bind by union (T0 "How traits compose").
It is not declarative (no A2) and has no model in the loop (no A11, A12; A5 still binds via multi-agent).

### system-boundary.S3
The service's full set: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14 (not A2, A11, A12).
E5: "A module inside a service is a system, and the service contains it, whether or not the module's interfaces are declared", and "changing a module changes the service it sits in."
A0: a change answers to "the system it modifies, every system containing it"; the module's own trait-less status adds only the floor, which the service already carries.
E5 "Why precision matters" names the exact escape this blocks: "a stateful system escapes its own axioms by being described as stateless modules, each of which passes on its own."

### system-boundary.S4
Only the library's own axioms: the floor, A3, A4, A8, A9, A14.
The library composes with its consumers through a declared, versioned interface (E5 *Compose*), and E5 states: "Publishing a new version of an interface alters no consumer that has not adopted it; each adoption is a change to that consumer, and answers to that consumer's axioms."
Here even the interface and its behaviour are unchanged, so no consumer's contract is altered (A0: a composed system "is not altered, and its axioms do not bind").
E5 "Why precision matters" rejects the alternative reading: "a small shared library would carry the axioms of every system that uses it, and the most-reused code would carry the most obligations."
Each consumer's adoption of the new version is that consumer's change, bound by its own axioms (stateful/multi-agent consumers bring A1, A5 etc. then, not now).

### system-boundary.S5
All of the service's axioms as well as the launcher's: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14 (not A2, A11, A12).
E5 *Boundaries*: "Where one unit depends on another and nothing declares the boundary between them... they are one system, with the traits of both"; A0 says the same and that "the axioms either unit would bring all bind".
Something else is wrong: "The undeclared dependency is itself a fault against A3" (E5; A0), which A3's Air-Gap Principle forbids ("units interact only through declared adapters and contracts"), and the launcher's output is a "contract in fact" that your change "reaches across" (E5) - so the change may break the service's parsing.
E5: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again."
Whether this change is obliged to do that declaration is NOT SETTLED explicitly; GUESS: A0's "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows" means at minimum you must not deepen the undeclared coupling and should declare the interface or record the gap with a revival trigger (M5).

### system-boundary.S6
Defined in entities/E5-system.md: "A system is a unit of engineering with a boundary: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it."
The boundary separates it from a neighbour "only where it is declared and honoured"; otherwise the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to.
Systems relate in exactly two ways, contain and compose; traits describe a system and axioms bind it.
It is not a repository, deployment unit or process by definition, and not defined by size or traits.
E6 adds that a component is "a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it."

### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful and multi-agent, so A1 and A5 are in force, and the component is a textbook A1 fault: "Hidden State Problem - state lives inside a process; other actors reason about a different reality" (A1 Faults; mandate: "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth").
But A0 limits what is mandated by scope: a change touches "what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters; code that merely sits beside it... is not touched", and "Beyond the scope, improvement is considered, never mandated."
So you are not required to fix it; if it is adjacent (shares a declared interface with what you touch, E6) you may close it opportunistically as a separate change when A14's tangent discipline supports it, otherwise you record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5)" - "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (A0).
For your feature: "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names", so any state your feature holds goes in durable, queryable shared state, not process memory (A1 Mechanics, A5).
If your feature newly depends on that component, it is "touched but not altered, so its gaps must not be built on" (A0) - do not rely on its hidden state.
If your feature actually modifies that component, it is in scope and you must close as much of the gap as the touch allows.
Capture the finding at discovery (A14 Capture-on-discovery).

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a limit (an "asymptote") the system is built toward to stay aligned with its architecture's intent, not a pass/fail test (A0 "Standing commitment, not situated move").
For a change it gives direction in two senses at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" - "never backwards, and forward with every step."
A system is "not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
A0 retains a correction banner recording that the earlier invariant reading ("hold or fail") was replaced by the director's asymptote ruling because pass/fail "gives no direction", and names "The conformance reading" as a fault.
In my words: when I change a bound system, the axiom tells me which way every step must point and forbids both regressing and silently leaving a touched gap in place; a gate can only check the binary floor of that (no further from any limit, no gap repeated).
All axioms in force bind at once and none may be traded against another (A0 "How the set composes").

### OBSERVATIONS
- The applicability matrix in axioms/README.md agrees with every axiom's `applies-to` frontmatter (checked all fourteen).
- E5 gives one "contain" example (a module in a service) and one "compose" example; it does not say directly when a linked or vendored library is contained by, rather than composing with, its consumer. E6's blast radius ("every system containing it") could pull consumer axioms onto a library change under a containment reading; S4 rests on reading "separately owned and published, declared versioned interface" as composition.
- The "one system with the traits of both" rule (S5) makes the operator-watched launcher part of a system carrying `autonomous`; that follows mechanically from the union rule but is a little counter-intuitive.
- S5: A3's Bypass-as-Signal describes what a detected bypass of a *declared* interface triggers; nothing states the equivalent obligation when no interface is declared at all, beyond "declaring the boundary... makes them two again".
- A0 says "a change answers to every system it alters" including "any system whose contract with it the change alters", while E5 says a versioned contract alters a consumer only on adoption; for an unversioned declared interface (S2) the corpus does not say whether that one-adoption-at-a-time rule applies.
- INDEX.md tells readers to fetch from GitHub when no local checkout is present; I did not, per instructions.
