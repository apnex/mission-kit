PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-698baca5 ---
An axiom is a limit the system is built toward, not a test to pass or fail. axioms/README.md: "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward to stay aligned with its architecture's intent".
When you change a system it binds, the axiom does two things. Your change must not move the system further from that limit. It must also move the system toward the limit as far as the change's scope allows ("never backwards, and forward with every step").
A system is not wrong because it is short of the limit, only because it moves away from it. That is why the charter's CORRECTION banner rejects the old reading of axioms as invariants that hold or fail.
In practice, your new work must not repeat a gap the axiom names, work that touches a gap must narrow it, and gaps you find outside your scope must be recorded, not passed silently.
All axioms in force bind at once and cannot be traded against each other ("conjunctive in force"). A gate checks only the backwards part: that the change moved the system no further from any limit and repeated no gap it touched.
