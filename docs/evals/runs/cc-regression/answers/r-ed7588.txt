### composition.P7
(a) The work-type's list governs.
D0 "How domains compose": "Which domains a work-type may act on is declared on the work-type, and a pairing outside that list is rejected", and "Each domain entry shows the work-types that act on it, generated from the work-types' own lists, so the two cannot disagree". W0 Faults ("The doubly-declared pairing"): "only the work-type's list governs, and the domain's view is generated". Each domain entry's table carries the heading "Generated from each work-type's `domainEligibility`, the list that governs".
(b) The axiom's `applies-to` governs. axioms/README.md: "`applies-to` answers *whether* an axiom binds, and it is the only field that does"; traits/README.md: "An axiom names the traits it governs in its `applies-to` field".
The corpus does not explicitly rule on what happens if the two disagree, so that is a reading of the "only field" sentence, not a quotation.
Unlike the domain views, the trait entries' "Axioms it brings into force" lists carry no GENERATED marker. tools/check-traits.mjs checks only that the applies-to enum equals the trait names, not the per-trait axiom lists, so nothing I found would mechanically catch a disagreement.
The five trait lists match the axioms' `applies-to` fields today; I confirmed this by reading all five trait files and all fourteen axiom frontmatters.

### OBSERVATIONS
- W8 body ("if the live roster collapses attester and executor, the seed fails or downgrades to `kind:review`") conflicts with W0 constraint 2 ("a same-agent review never stands in") and constraint 9 ("Same-agent review is never a valid degradation"). R3's "verifier-held gates use plain kind:review" makes the downgrade path even less clear.
- W0 "Gaps recorded" has two adjacent sentences that contradict each other: "The domains set has not said which, and its answer settles this." followed by "The domains charter's tie-break, applied to one such change, settles which."
- Domain-side work-type lists are generated from the work-types, but the trait-side axiom lists are hand-maintained and not checked against `applies-to`. They agree today, but they could drift, which is the "two lists of one thing drift" fault check-traits.mjs describes for the enum.
- No entry maps "apply a merged migration" to a work-type by name. P3's W7 answer rests on D7's merge-and-land/publish-deploy analogy plus W7's domainEligibility.
- P1's "long-running" does not determine T4 autonomous. That makes no difference to P1, because multi-agent already brings in A7, A10 and A13.
- D1's A1 citation sits on a domain the corpus calls free across stateless and stateful systems. A reader could take it as binding A1 on any codebase work, which contradicts A0's "only field" rule; the citation reads best as an obligation on the organisation's work records (E5).
- D3's subject surface names specific tools (ois/pi/claude launchers), which D0's "domain named after a tool" fault acknowledges.
- INDEX tells readers to fall back to fetching from GitHub. Under the scoping of this task I did not do that and used only the local corpus.
