PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-5192c241 ---
An axiom is a limit to steer toward, not a test to pass. A0 calls it "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward". A system "is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it." When you change a system an axiom binds, the axiom does two things: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows" ("never backwards, and forward with every step"). A0 records the pass/fail reading as a fault ("The conformance reading") and keeps a CORRECTION banner retracting the older invariant wording. A gate checks only the direction: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched". Axioms are "conjunctive in force", so you cannot trade one against another: "a regression with thirteen consolations".

--- answer a-c079f64f ---

A gate checking a change can only check the binary part: the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched" (A0).
