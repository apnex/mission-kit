### work-layers.L1
It belongs in the rules layer (`rules/`, prefix RU). The placement test in methods/README.md (M0, "Placement") settles it: guidance that "only *requires* a record to carry something - a field, a reason - produces nothing itself, and is a rule". M0 even uses this exact case: "*every deferral records its revival trigger* is visible in text and is still a rule, because it governs what the work records." Yes, it is already covered by rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md: "When you defer, park, or cut a unit of work, record - at the moment of deferral - a concrete revival trigger", with trace "every deferred, parked or cut item's record carries a revival trigger". RU3 adds two corollaries: "Terminal is reopenable" and "Revival re-triages, it does not resume." The document that carries the field is artifacts/AR5-backlog.md, which requires a "revival trigger | required unless closed; an observable condition" on every row and rejects "a date or the word 'later'". methods/M5 is the old home, now superseded and pointing to RU3.

### work-layers.L2
It belongs in the skills layer (`skills/`, prefix K), and it already exists as skills/K3-substrate-audit.md: a "five-pass meta-process" (Sweep, Triangulation, Promotion audit, Synthesis, Reconciliation) that writes a "fixed artefact layout" under `docs/<domain>/<substrate>/`. Reason: skills/README.md (K0) says a skill "is *invoked*, not followed: it names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output, and it is expected to be executed largely as written". K0 also says a capability earns a skill entry "when it is executable, repeatable, and would otherwise be reconstructed from scratch by whoever needs it next". It also says a skill "earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary", and K3 ships templates and references in `skills/substrate-audit/`. M0's table points the same way: "a capability you execute -> skills/ ... a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed". M0 names the opposite mistake as a fault: "The procedure that is really a skill ... It belongs in `skills/`, where it can be invoked." One caveat: M0's ordered five-question placement test does not list skills at all, and its question 2 ("produce a result of its own - ... an artifact?") would read literally as sending this to methods/ (see OBSERVATIONS).

### work-layers.L3
It belongs in the work-types layer (`work-types/`, prefix W). work-types/README.md (W0) defines a work-type as something that "compiles to a closeable claimable node", and its schema has fields for who (`roleEligibility`, `evidenceAuthority`), what it may target (`domainEligibility`, `parameters`) and what proof closes it (`evidenceContract`, `falsifier`). It is already covered by work-types/W10-adversarial-design-review-upstream.md: "A verifier red-teams a design **before** build or merge, producing a critique doc that raises specific, falsifiable failure hypotheses". W10 sets `roleEligibility: [verifier]` and `evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation`, and the attester "cannot be the executor" (constraint 2). It may be pointed at all seven domains (`domainFreedom: free`), with `target` = "the design under review", bound `provided-by-trigger`. It closes on a `kind:review` critique doc, and its falsifier is "review after the decision, or no falsifiable concerns raised". It cites `methods: [M1]` as the procedure that conducts it.

### work-layers.L4
It is a methodology entry, and methods/M1-triangulated-review.md already covers it: "require **at least four genuinely independent inputs** before reaching a verdict", triggered by "reviewing a patch or design that ships to production or upstream". It is a method, not a rule, because it produces a result of its own (a verdict); see M0 placement question 2 and M0's "One primitive carries the set. M1 - independent inputs before a verdict". It is not a skill, because nothing in it is packaged to be invoked with scripts or assets (K0). It is not a work-type, because it is how work is done, not a claimable unit of work: work-types cite it instead (W10 has `methods: [M1]`, and W0 says "The work-type cites the procedure that conducts it, in `methods`"). The work-type layer covers the neighbouring structural point that the reviewer must not be the author (W0 constraints 2 and 8, W13 code-owner-approve). work-types/W15-convene-a-council.md covers "multi-lens deliberation + synthesis". Note that M1 softens its own floor: "The number isn't sacred (sometimes 3 is enough; sometimes 5 is needed)."

### work-layers.L5
This is M0's "handing over" moment. methods/README.md lists it as one of six moments shared by methods/, rules/ and practices/. Which layer it goes in depends on its shape. If it is a procedure that produces a record, it is a method. If it is a requirement such as "*a handover record names what is unfinished*", it is a rule; rules/README.md gives that example and says it "would leave a trace, and none exists". The corpus does name the gap openly. M0: "handing work in progress to another agent or a later session has no procedure at all", and "a lone agent hands over to its own next session". M0 also says the gap is "Neither ... a defect until something fails there", and that it is "where to look first when something does." practices/README.md: "*handing over* hold[s] no practice today". The nearest standing commitment is axioms/A4-zero-loss-knowledge.md ("zero decision context is lost at handover"). traits/T1 and T3 classify the case as stateful (the notes are persistent state) and not multi-agent ("one actor across time, not two actors").

### work-layers.L6
Methods vs skills is the hard pair, because both are procedures. M0 separates them by whether the guidance is followed or invoked: method = "steps that produce a result of their own"; skill = "a capability you execute ... a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed". K0 puts it as: "A method is a procedure held as guidance, the single source of its content; a skill packages a procedure so an agent can install and invoke it, and earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary". The work-type test is different in kind. W0: "A candidate is taxonomy-grade **iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node**" (roles, domains, evidence contract, falsifier), and a work-type cites its method (`methods: [M..]`) rather than containing it. The method/skill line has been redrawn in practice: skills/K1 "Moved to M9 by Delta-1", and K0 says "A skill that only describes is a method, rule or practice filed in the wrong layer, as `M9` was before it moved". M9 still contains concrete git-rewrite mechanics, and K3 is written under a "## Rule" heading, so packaging rather than content decides the call. M0's placement list (axiom, procedure, style, rule, practice) does not mention skills or work-types, so it does not on its own separate a method from a skill.

### OBSERVATIONS
- Contradiction on independence: W0 constraint 2 says that if the roster collapses attester and executor, the seed fails and "a same-agent review never stands in". Yet W10 says "with a collapsed roster this falls back to plain `kind:review`", and W9 says it "fails the seed or downgrades to plain `kind:review`".
- entities/E2-layer.md says "The three mechanism layers", but the README Mechanics table lists four no-prefix layers (bundles/, tools/, plugins/, docs/). docs/ (and the README link `docs/README.md`) is absent from this corpus copy.
- M0's ordered placement test (axiom, procedure, style, rule, practice) leaves out skills, patterns and work-types, although its own table includes skills and patterns. Read literally, question 2 sends a packaged artifact-producing capability like K3 to methods/.
- K3's artefact layout lists `05-coverage.md`, but `skills/substrate-audit/templates/` has no 05 template, and it has a `02-code-trace.md.tmpl` that the layout does not mention.
- practices/README.md lists only three empty moments (verifying, deciding what to keep, handing over), but committing to a design and treating the record also hold no practice.
- M0's "Handing over" row defines the moment narrowly as leading a human to understand and decide. That framing hides the agent-to-agent / next-session gap the same file admits.
- M1's title says "minimum 4", while its body says "sometimes 3 is enough". K4 (research-artefacts) consists of "four non-negotiable disciplines", which looks like rule- or practice-shaped guidance in skills/ (GUESS: possibly K0's "skill that only describes" fault).
- R4 has two "Axiom alignment" bullets, both citing A13.
