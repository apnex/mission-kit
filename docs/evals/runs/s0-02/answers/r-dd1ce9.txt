### system-boundary.S1
A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14 bind this change, which are the launcher's axioms only.
The launcher has no traits: it holds no state (T1), has one actor (T3), has an operator watching it (T4), and has no model in the loop (T5).
So only the `any-system` floor applies: "A3 ... A4 ... A8 ... A9 ... A14" (axioms/README.md, Applicability matrix), and E5 says "A one-shot script with no traits is a system, bound by the axioms every system carries".
The service's axioms do not bind: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md, What brings an axiom into force).
E5 gives this exact case as composition: "A launcher that hands sessions to a service through a declared interface composes with it."
This assumes the service honours the interface and reads nothing else from the launcher, which matches "nothing crosses ... except a declared session interface".
The organisation's own axioms govern how the change is made and recorded, and they are asked about separately (E5, "The organisation doing the work is a system").

### system-boundary.S2
The answer is the union of both systems' axioms: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13 and A14 (it excludes A2, A11 and A12).
A0 says a change answers to "any system whose contract with it the change alters".
E5 says: "changing an unversioned interface alters every consumer at once, so the change answers to all of them immediately."
The service is stateful (A1), multi-agent (A5, A6, A7, A10, A13) and autonomous (A7, A10, A13). It is not declarative (so not A2) and not llm-in-the-loop (so not A11 or A12). The floor A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14 applies as well.
A5 binds because of its `multi-agent` tag, because "Tags are alternatives, not a conjunction" (axioms/README.md).
E5 also says "Adding versioning to an interface you are changing moves it toward A3's limit and is encouraged, not required; if it is not done, the gap is recorded."

### system-boundary.S3
The service's axioms bind: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13 and A14.
A0 says a change answers to "the system it modifies, every system containing it".
E5 says "A module inside a service is a system, and the service contains it, whether or not the module's interfaces are declared", and "changing a module changes the service it sits in."
It does not matter that the module holds no state on its own. E5, "Why precision matters", rejects the reading in which "a stateful system escapes its own axioms by being described as stateless modules", and T0 agrees ("a stateful system cannot escape its axioms by being described as stateless parts").
The module's own traits (none) bring only the floor, and the floor is already a subset.

### system-boundary.S4
Only the library's axioms bind: A3, A4, A8, A9 and A14, which is the `any-system` floor ("a plain library takes `any-system`", axioms/README.md).
The ten consumers are not altered, because the interface and its behaviour do not change. E5 says "Publishing a new version of an interface alters no consumer that has not adopted it; each adoption is a change to that consumer, and answers to that consumer's axioms; the component is not altered by being adopted."
E5 states the intent directly: otherwise "a small shared library would carry the axioms of every system that uses it, and the most-reused code would carry the most obligations."
Each consumer's later adoption is a separate change that answers to that consumer's axioms, not to this change.
One caveat: E5 says "A declared version range - *any 2.x* - is adoption in advance: a release within it alters every consumer bound by the range". The question says each consumer adopts separately, so I assume no consumer declares such a range. The question also does not say whether any consumer reaches past the interface. Either case would widen the answer.

### system-boundary.S5
The launcher and the service count as one system here, so all of A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13 and A14 bind.
E5 says: "Where one unit depends on another and nothing declares the boundary between them ... they are one system, with the traits of both", and E5's "declared boundary against an assumed one" says "Behaviour another unit depends on without any declaration is a contract in fact ... and a change to it reaches across."
Other things are wrong too. First, the undeclared dependency is itself a fault: "The undeclared dependency is itself a fault against A3, which requires units to interact only through declared contracts" (E5; A3 Air-Gap Principle).
Second, your change takes ownership of that boundary: "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5, Boundaries).
Third, the change will probably break the service's parser. GUESS: the corpus does not say this outright. I infer it because the service parses the exact lines you are changing.

### system-boundary.S6
It is defined in entities/E5-system.md: "A **system** is a unit of engineering with a **boundary**: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it."
E5 adds that the boundary separates it from a neighbour "only where it is **declared and honoured**", and that "Traits describe a system, and axioms bind it."
Systems relate in exactly two ways, contain and compose. A system is "Not a repository, a deployment unit or a process by definition" and "Not defined by size or by traits."
E6 says a component is "a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it", and E5 also says "The organisation doing the work is a system."

### OBSERVATIONS
- S4: the question does not say whether any consumer declares a version range or reaches past the interface. E5 makes both widen what binds, so my answer assumes neither.
- S1 and S2 assume the service honours the declared interface. If it did not, E5's "Declared against honoured" rule would merge the two systems.
- A9 is tagged `any-system`, so it binds even a log-format change to a one-shot script that an operator watches. A9's mechanics (for example "Full-Stack Simulation", "Deterministic Trunk Gate") fit such a change badly. A0 itself calls A9 "the least exercised axiom in the set".
- A13 is tagged `multi-agent` and `autonomous`, and it assumes a "single human Director". The service has no human in the synchronous loop, and the corpus does not say who plays the Director for a product system as opposed to the organisation: NOT SETTLED.
- The way E5 separates the organisation's axioms from the product's axioms is clear, but both apply to every change, so any answer that lists only product-system axioms is incomplete on purpose.
