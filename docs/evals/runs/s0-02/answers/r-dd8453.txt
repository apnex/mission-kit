### work-layers.L2
Skills (`skills/`, prefix K). skills/README.md (K0) says a skill "is *invoked*, not followed: it names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output", and it earns an entry when it is "executable, repeatable, and would otherwise be reconstructed from scratch", with packaging justified "where scripts or assets make it necessary" (fixed templates for a fixed layout qualify). M0's boundary table agrees: "a capability you execute -> skills/ ... a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed", and M0 names the fault "The procedure that is really a skill". The capability already exists as K3 substrate-audit (skills/K3-substrate-audit.md): a "five-pass meta-process" (Sweep, Triangulation, Promotion audit, Synthesis, Reconciliation) with a fixed layout under `docs/<domain>/<substrate>/` (00-summary.md ... sources.md), and its body and templates are in skills/substrate-audit/. It is also bundled as `nanoprobe` (bundles/nanoprobe.yaml). So a new entry should strengthen K3, not sit beside it (M0: "Prefer strengthening an existing entry to adding a neighbour"). Caveat: M0's five ordered placement questions never mention skills, so read literally, question 2 ("produces a result of its own") would send it to methods/; see OBSERVATIONS.

### work-layers.L3
Work-types (`work-types/`, prefix W): W0 says a candidate is taxonomy-grade "iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node", and the entry schema has fields for exactly these things - `roleEligibility` (who), `domainEligibility`/`parameters.target` (what it may be pointed at), `evidenceContract`/`evidenceAuthority`/`falsifier` (what proof ends it). It is already covered by W10 adversarial-design-review-upstream (work-types/W10-adversarial-design-review-upstream.md): "A verifier red-teams a design **before** build or merge". Who: `roleEligibility: [verifier]`, with `evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation`, and the attester "cannot be the executor" given the live roster (W0 constraint 2). What it may target: `target` = "the design under review", `provided-by-trigger`, and any of the seven domains (`domainFreedom: free`). Proof: a single `kind: review` - "critique doc before build/merge with specific failure hypotheses". Falsifier: "review after the decision, or no falsifiable concerns raised". It cites M1 as the method that conducts it (`methods: [M1]`).

### work-layers.L4
It is a methodology entry (methods/, prefix M), and M1 Triangulated review (methods/M1-triangulated-review.md) already covers it: "require **at least four genuinely independent inputs** before reaching a verdict", for "patches or designs that ship to production or upstream". By M0's placement it is a method because following it produces "a result of its own" - a verdict (M0 places M1 at the *Verifying* moment). It is not a skill: K0 says a skill packages a procedure with scripts or assets, and M1 is guidance with no such packaging. It is not a work-type: work-types cite it instead - W8 verify-gate-reactive and W10 both carry `methods: [M1]`, per W0 ("The work-type cites the procedure that conducts it, in `methods`"). The "never a single/self reviewer" part is held by W0 constraints 2, 8 and 9 (independence structural; author != approver; "Same-agent review is never a valid degradation") and by W13 code-owner-approve.

### work-layers.L6
The test is in M0's placement list, which asks in order: axiom? then "**Does following it produce a result of its own** - a verdict, an artifact, a record? A procedure, here [methods]"; M0's table adds "a capability you execute -> skills/ ... a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed". K0 draws the method/skill line: "A method is a procedure held as guidance, the single source of its content; a skill packages a procedure so an agent can install and invoke it, and earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary". W0 defines a work-type differently: a kind of work that "compiles to a closeable claimable node" (role x work-type x domain), which *cites* its method in `methods` and does not contain the procedure. Method vs skill is the hard pair: both are procedures that produce results, and the dividing line is packaging (invoked vs followed; scripts/assets), not kind. The corpus shows it went wrong: M9 history scrub moved *from* skills to methods (K1: "Moved to M9 by Delta-1"), and K0 says "A skill that only describes is a method ... as `M9` was before it moved". Both READMEs name the confusion as a fault ("The procedure that is really a skill" in M0). Method vs work-type is easier because the edge `methods: [M1]` in W8/W10 shows the relation, though M7 and W22 (axiom alignment audit vs gate) are close.
