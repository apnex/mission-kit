### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. A declared interface "separates two units only for the neighbours that use it", and "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). axioms/README.md (A0) says the same: where a boundary is "one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". So your change to T's internals answers to O's axioms too. axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md ("Bypass as Signal") says the bypass "is a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". A detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision". That evaluation ends with "the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted", and until then "the two remain one system for what a change answers to". NOT SETTLED: whether fixing the bypass is mandatory inside your change. E5 says a change "that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary", but O's interface is declared and bypassed, not undeclared.

### boundary-bypass.B2
Your change answers to O, not to P as a separate system. E5 says "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not" (entities/E5-system.md). A0 says a system "it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md). P composes through T's interface, which is unchanged, so P's own axioms do not bind. O bypasses the interface, so O is one system with T and its axioms bind. The change also answers to "every system containing it" (A0), meaning the service. E5 says a containing system "has every trait any part it contains has", so P's traits reach the change through the service anyway (see OBSERVATIONS).

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. They are still one system. E5 says "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). E6 says the interface separates T from a neighbour "only where they are declared and that neighbour uses them", and "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (entities/E6-component.md). E5 "Boundaries" adds: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." To separate them, O must be moved onto the interface, or the interface must be adjusted to what O legitimately needs and O must then use it (A3 "Bypass as Signal"; E5).

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat each bypass as "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface": the interface "may ... fall short of the component's duty" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, "Bypass as Signal"). The detection "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision". The evaluation ends with each consumer "moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 success signal 5 says each detected violation "is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". So do not only patch the consumers. Until each bypass is resolved, every bypassing component is one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; E5). The bypasses are also A3 faults: "The undeclared dependency is itself a fault against A3" (E5 "Boundaries"; A0). NOT SETTLED: who runs the evaluation, when, and whether it must be done now or can be recorded with a revival trigger (A0's RU3 route for gaps outside a change's scope). GUESS: several consumers bypassing T is stronger evidence that the interface falls short than a single bypass is.

### OBSERVATIONS
- E5's rule "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary" names undeclared neighbours only. A0 and E5 "Declared against honoured" treat a declared-but-bypassed boundary the same as an undeclared one for binding. It is unclear whether the "owns that boundary" scope obligation also applies to a bypassed declared interface.
- The questions assume the change does not alter T's behaviour, but it changes the internals O calls directly. The corpus does not say whether the merge depends on the change actually altering the specific internals O reaches. E5's "The merge chains only through what a change can reach" suggests it might, but that sentence is about chaining through a third unit.
- Here P and T are inside one service, and that service contains T. A0 makes the change answer to "every system containing it". E5 says a containing system "has every trait any part it contains has". So P's traits bind the change through the service even though P "composes" and "is not altered". The per-pair rule therefore matters mainly across systems, not inside one service. The corpus does not discuss this interaction.
- Adjacency (E6) needs a shared declared interface that "one consumes". A bypassing consumer does not use the interface, so it is arguably not adjacent to T. A0's opportunistic-improvement route is written for adjacent components, so it is unclear how it applies to bypassers.
