### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. `entities/E5-system.md` (Declared against honoured) says "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it", and `axioms/README.md` (A0, "What brings an axiom into force") says that where a boundary is "one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". So a change to T's internals answers to O's axioms as well as T's. It also answers to the service, which contains T: A0 says a change answers to "every system containing it". About the bypass itself, `axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md` (Bypass as Signal) calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". It says a detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 adds "Until then the two remain one system for what a change answers to". If your change does not fix the bypass, A0 says a gap the change passes is "recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3)", because "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids". NOT SETTLED: whether changing internals that O calls counts as "touching" the gap, which would oblige you to close as much of it as the touch allows (A0 line 79).

### boundary-bypass.B2
O, but not P. E5 says "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not." A0 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". The interface is unchanged here, so P's axioms do not bind. O is one system with T through its bypass (E5; A3 Bypass as Signal), so O's axioms bind. The change also answers to T itself and to the containing service (A0: "the system it modifies, every system containing it"). E6's blast-radius row agrees: the change reaches "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface", and reaches an adjacent component "only if the interface between them changes".

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. E5 says "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing". E5 Boundaries says "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again". `entities/E6-component.md` says "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it." T and O therefore stay one system with the traits of both. What changes is the kind of fault. Before, it was an undeclared dependency (E5: "itself a fault against A3"). Now O's calls reach past a declared interface, which is a bypass under A3's Bypass as Signal and triggers the interface evaluation described there. GUESS: under A0's rule that "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows", a change that declares the interface should move O onto it, or record why it did not (RU3). The text does not say this for this exact case.

### boundary-bypass.B4
A3 Bypass as Signal treats each bypass as "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface: it may mean the interface falls short of the component's duty". It asks for "an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision", ending in each consumer "being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 Success signal 5 says each detected violation is "evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". So you should not only patch the consumers. Until this is resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; E5; A0). If the work falls outside the current change's scope, A0 says to record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3)" and not close it there. Larger choices of what work comes next belong to the director (R4, AR3). NOT SETTLED: whether several bypassers change the procedure. GUESS: several consumers bypassing T is stronger evidence that the interface falls short of T's duty, so adjusting the interface becomes more likely.

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 says the evaluation is "against the component's duty and its vision", but no definition says what a component's "vision" is. AR6 defines Vision for a programme, not a component. So whose vision applies is ambiguous.
- No work-type or method names who runs the bypass evaluation, or how. A3 says it is triggered but not what procedure carries it out. W9 (audit) and W14 (design a contract) are candidates, but the texts do not link to them.
- A0 says a change "touches" what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and consumers whose contract it alters. It does not say whether a bypassing consumer whose called internals are modified counts as touched (as opposed to bound). So it is unclear whether the "close the gap as far as the touch allows" duty reaches O's bypass.
- T and O are in the same service, so the service contains both. The corpus does not say how O's own axioms relate to the service's axioms when they differ. The merge rule ("traits of both") covers this, but the result is not stated.
- E5 Discriminators line 34 says a unit reaching another with no declared contract "and neither is part of the other" does neither. Units inside one service are contained by it, not by each other, so that case still applies here. Wording that relies on "part of" could be misread.
