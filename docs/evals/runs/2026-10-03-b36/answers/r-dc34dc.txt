### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. O reaches past T's declared interface, so T and O are one system for deciding what a change answers to. E5 "Declared against honoured" says: "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it". The A0 charter (axioms/README.md) says that where no boundary is "declared and honoured - none written, or one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". The change also answers to the service that contains T (A0: "every system containing it").
On the bypass, A3 "Bypass as Signal" says it is "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". A detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted".
Whether this change has to close the bypass is NOT SETTLED in so many words. GUESS: O's dependence on T's internals is a "contract in fact" (E5), and your change alters it, so O counts as touched. A0 then says the change "closes as much of [the gap] as the touch allows", and anything left over is recorded with a revival trigger (RU3).

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O and not to P, and to the service that contains both. E5 says: "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not."
P composes with T through a contract the change leaves unchanged. A0 says: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind." E6's blast-radius row agrees: an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes".
O bypasses the interface, so it is one system with T, and O's axioms bind (A0, E5).
The change also answers to the containing service (A0, "every system containing it"). P sits inside that service too. Whether P's traits then reach the change through the service's traits is NOT SETTLED.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. For deciding what a change answers to, they are still one system. E5 says: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing." E5 Boundaries says: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." E6 says: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it."
They are still separate units in the containment sense. E5 says "A module inside a service is a system ... whether or not the module's interfaces are declared". That sense does not separate them for binding.
Because a declared interface now exists, O's calls are a bypass as A3 defines it: a consumer fault, and a trigger for evaluating the interface (A3 "Bypass as Signal").

### boundary-bypass.B4
A3 "Bypass as Signal" treats each detected bypass as a fault in that consumer and also as a signal that the interface "may ... fall short of the component's duty".
It asks you to evaluate T's interface "against the component's duty and its vision". That ends with each consumer moved onto the interface, or with the interface adjusted.
A3 success signal 5 says each detected bypass "is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". Until that is resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T (A3, citing A0).
Under A0, if this work falls outside your change's scope, record it with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than passing it silently. An interface adjustment is its own change, and it answers to every consumer it alters (or, for a versioned interface, to each one that adopts it) (A0, E5).
That several bypasses make the shortfall reading stronger is NOT SETTLED. GUESS: several independent bypasses point more toward the interface than toward any one consumer.
Who runs the evaluation, and when, is NOT SETTLED.

### OBSERVATIONS
- E6's "Blast radius" row says a change reaches "any unit that reaches it, or that it reaches, with no declared interface". Read alone, that seems to leave out units that reach past an interface that is declared. E6 "Boundary against directory" and E5 "Declared against honoured" do cover that case, but the table row is narrower than the prose.
- The question premise "does not alter T's ... behaviour" does not fit well with O calling internals. Changing internals can change the behaviour O depends on. E5 calls that a "contract in fact", and A0 counts "any consumer whose contract it alters" as touched. The corpus never says outright that a contract in fact is a "contract" in A0's sense of touch.
- A3 offers "the interface being adjusted" as one outcome of a bypass, yet also says a new capability is made by composition "never by modifying" existing units ("Composable by Default"). The corpus does not reconcile these.
- No entry names a role or a deadline for the bypass evaluation A3 asks for. It also does not say whether the author of an unrelated change to T must run it.
- The corpus does not say whether a containing system takes on the traits of its contained parts. This matters for whether P's traits bind through the service in B2.
