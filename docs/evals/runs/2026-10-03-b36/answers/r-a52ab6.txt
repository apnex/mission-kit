### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. A declared interface separates two units only where it is "declared and honoured"; "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it" (entities/E5-system.md, Definition and "Declared against honoured"). So for deciding what your change answers to, T and O are one system "with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind" (axioms/README.md, "What brings an axiom into force"; E6 blast radius row: a change reaches "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface"). The change also answers to the containing service (A0: "every system containing it").
On the bypass itself, A3 "Bypass as Signal" calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". A detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md). E5 also says "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional" (E5, Boundaries).
NOT SETTLED: whether that "owns that boundary" clause, worded for an "undeclared neighbour", covers a neighbour that bypasses a declared interface. GUESS: it does, because A0 treats "none written, or one written and reached past" as the same case.

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O, not to P. E5 says "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). For P: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md). For O: T and O stay one system, so O's axioms bind (A0; E5). Either way the change still answers to T and to the service that contains it (A0: "every system containing it").

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. E5: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). E5 Boundaries: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again". E6: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (entities/E6-component.md, Definition). A3 says they remain one system "Until then", meaning until O is moved onto the interface or the interface is adjusted (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, "Bypass as Signal").
NOT SETTLED: whether the change that declares the interface must also migrate O in that same change. GUESS: A0's rule that "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows", and E5's "owns that boundary" clause, both point toward yes. At minimum, the remaining bypass must be recorded rather than passed over silently (A0, RU3).

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat each detected bypass as A3's "Bypass as Signal" directs. It is a fault in each consumer and also a signal that "the interface falls short of the component's duty, and the consumer has not signalled the change it needs" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md). Evaluate T's interface "against the component's duty and its vision". End with each consumer "moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". A3 success signal 5 says each detected violation is "evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". So do not simply patch the consumers. E6 also says "A detected crossing is a signal about the interface as well as a fault in the consumer". Until each bypass is resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; E5).
NOT SETTLED: the corpus says nothing specific about several bypassers. GUESS: several bypassers make it more likely that the interface falls short of T's duty.

### OBSERVATIONS
- E5 Boundaries says "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary". That wording covers undeclared boundaries. It is not explicit for a consumer that bypasses a declared interface, although A0 and E5 "Declared against honoured" treat the two cases as equivalent.
- E6's blast-radius row says "any unit that reaches it, or that it reaches, with no declared interface". Read literally, that could exclude O, because T does have a declared interface; O just does not use it. E5's "declared and honoured" wording settles this, but E6's row is looser.
- E5's chaining clause says a change binds only through "what a change can reach", with the condition "only when it alters something B takes from C". That condition is stated only for transitive chains. For the direct case it is unclear whether a change to T internals that O never calls still makes T and O one system. E6 and A0 read as unconditional.
- The questions say the change does not alter T's behaviour. But a change to internals that O calls directly can alter what O observes. E5 calls such dependency "a contract in fact". So that premise may not hold for O.
- A3 says to evaluate the interface "against the component's duty and its vision". The corpus does not say whose vision this is or where a component's vision is recorded. AR6 vision is described at programme level.
