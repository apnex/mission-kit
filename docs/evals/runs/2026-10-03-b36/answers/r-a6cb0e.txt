### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. E5 (entities/E5-system.md, Definition) says that where one unit "reaches past one that was declared, the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to". A0 (axioms/README.md) agrees: with no boundary "declared and honoured - none written, or one written and reached past", the two are "one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". So your change to T's internals answers to O's axioms too. On the bypass itself, the corpus calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface" (A3, *Bypass as Signal*; E6, *Boundary against directory*). It asks that the bypass trigger "an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision", ending in one of two ways: "the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" (A3; E5, *Declared against honoured*). A3 adds: "Until then the two remain one system for what a change answers to". NOT SETTLED: whether your internals change must itself do that fix. E5 *Boundaries* says a change that "alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary", but O is a neighbour of a declared interface, not an undeclared one (see OBSERVATIONS).

### boundary-bypass.B2
O, not P. E5 *Declared against honoured*: "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not." A0: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". The interface is unchanged, so P is not bound as P. O stays one system with T (A3 *Bypass as Signal*; E6 Blast radius row: "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface"). The change also answers to T and to the service that contains it (A0: "every system containing it"). Caveat: E5 says "A system has every trait any part it contains has". So P's traits can still bind through the containing service, but not through P (see OBSERVATIONS).

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. E5 *Declared against honoured*: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing". E6 Definition: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it". E5 *Boundaries*: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." So T and O are still one system with the traits of both. O's calls have also changed category: they were an undeclared dependency, and are now a bypass of a declared interface. That is an A3 fault, and it triggers A3's evaluation (move O onto the interface, or adjust the interface). NOT SETTLED: whether the change that declares the interface must also move O. GUESS: A0's "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows" points that way, but no text says so for this case.

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat each bypass as "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface: it may mean the interface falls short of the component's duty" (A3, *Bypass as Signal*; E6 *Boundary against directory*). The detection "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision". The evaluation ends with each consumer "moved onto the interface" or "the interface being adjusted" to what consumers "legitimately need" (A3; E5 *Declared against honoured*). A3 Success signal 5 asks that each detected violation be "evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". So the corpus asks you not to simply fix each consumer. Until the bypasses are resolved, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3; A0). GUESS: several bypasses point more strongly to an interface shortfall than one does, but the corpus says nothing about how many bypasses there are. NOT SETTLED: who must run the evaluation, and when. If it is outside the current change's scope, A0 suggests recording it with evidence and a revival trigger (RU3), but A0 says that about gaps in general, not bypasses.

### OBSERVATIONS
- E5 *Boundaries* says "A change that alters what an undeclared neighbour reaches into owns that boundary: declaring and honouring the interface is inside its scope, not optional." It does not say whether this covers a neighbour bypassing a *declared* interface (B1/B4). The text around it uses "undeclared boundary", while A0 and E5's Definition handle the declared-but-bypassed case together with it. So the scope obligation's reach is ambiguous.
- The question premise says the internals change "does not alter T's interface or its behaviour". But O calls internals, so the change probably does alter what O takes. E5's chaining rule ("only when it alters something B takes from C") conditions merging on what is altered. A3 and E6's Blast radius row make the bypassing unit part of T's system unconditionally. These two readings differ when the internals change does not touch what O calls.
- B2: A0 binds "every system containing it", and E5 gives a containing system "every trait any part it contains has". So P's traits reach the change through the service, even though P is "not altered". The per-pair separation answers "does P bind as P" but not "do P's traits bind". The corpus does not reconcile these two statements.
- E6 Definition talks about "neighbours using" interfaces, and E5 about "declared and honoured". Both agree, but neither says what happens when a consumer uses the interface for some calls and bypasses it for others.
