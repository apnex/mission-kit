Question:
While adding a small feature to component K, you notice K's internals tangle two concerns, and splitting them would give K's three consumers a cleaner interface. K's interface is not versioned. The split is within K's own duty. What do the axioms ask?

Key:
The new feature arrives as its own unit, composed with K, not bolted onto K. The split is encouraged as opportunistic improvement within K's duty - not mandated - including re-cutting K's seams and recomposing for its consumers; it is made as a change of its own, and since it changes K's interface, it answers to each consumer it alters.

Rubric:
2 for the feature as a new unit, the split encouraged not mandated, and the consumers' axioms binding the interface change. 1 for two of the three. 0 for 'never modify K' or 'the split is required'.

--- answer a-68aac03d ---
What is mandatory: your feature must not add to the tangle ("new work never repeats a gap the axiom names", A0). If the feature modifies the tangled code, it must also close as much of that gap as the touch allows (A0). The split itself is encouraged, not mandated. A3 Composable by Default says "within the unit's own duty, fix it, fracture it, re-cut its seams and recompose it so it makes better sense for every consumer downstream - as a change of its own." A0 says you are "expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". Do it as a separate change. Because K's interface is unversioned, that change alters all three consumers at once and answers to each of their axioms (A0; E6 Blast radius: "only on adoption" applies only to versioned interfaces). Decide with A14's tangent discipline: near payback, the yield captured, and no significant distraction from the feature. If the split fails that test, record it with evidence and a revival trigger (RU3). Either way the gap is recorded (A0). NOT SETTLED: whether the tangle counts as "touched" depends on whether the feature modifies the tangled code, because A0 says code that "merely sits beside it, in the same file or module, is not touched".
