### layer-charters.H1
NOT SETTLED by any charter: no `rules/` layer exists in this corpus. It has no directory, no `RU0` charter, no row in `README.md` Mechanics and no section in `INDEX.md`.
The contract does anticipate one. `schemas/catalog-entry/v1alpha1/catalog-entry.schema.json` accepts category `rule` with prefix `RU`, and its `trace` field says "A rule earns its layer by leaving one [an observable trace]; a practice leaves none."
A committed credential leaves a trace in the record, because it is visible in commits and blobs. So by that test the proposal is a rule, not a practice.
It is not style, because S0 covers the form of a document ("judged by reading the output"), not what may enter the repository. It is not an axiom either: A0 calls an axiom a limit a system is built toward, and this is a situated constraint.
Related: `skills/K1-history-content-scrub.md` covers what to do after a leak ("Live credential ... Rotate it first"), but it does not hold the prohibition itself.
GUESS: it belongs in `rules/` as an `RU` entry with a `trace` such as "no credential pattern in any committed blob". It might also carry `enforced-by` naming a secret-scanning tool. Until that layer exists, the nearest existing home is `methodology/`, under M0's test of "how you arrived at it".

### layer-charters.H5
A rule entry (category `rule`, id matching `^RU[1-9][0-9]*$`) must declare `trace`. This is a string of at least 20 characters that states "the observable trace a check could test - what in the record shows the rule was kept or broken". The `allOf` clause in `catalog-entry.schema.json` makes it required, and `catalog-entry.test.mjs` tests that a rule without it is refused.
A practice does not declare a trace: "a practice leaves none".
Optional for a rule: `enforced-by`, a path under `tools/` that names the checking tool. The schema says naming a tool "is enforced-by's job, and is optional". `tools/check-enforcers.sh` also checks `rules/RU*.md`, so an enforcer that is named must exist.
Both kinds share the base requirements (`id`, `category`, `title`, `status`, `hydrate-when`) and the other optional fields, such as `related` and `supersedes`.
These answers come from the schema only. No `rules/` or `practices/` charter exists to confirm or extend them.

### layer-charters.H6
NOT SETTLED. No document in the corpus gives an ordered decision procedure across axiom, method, style, rule and practice. The layers that would hold the last of these (`methods/`, `rules/`, `practices/`) have no charters.
The corpus does supply the separate tests. A0 separates a standing commitment from a situated move. M0 says to ask "what a reviewer would inspect": the output means style, how you arrived at it means methodology. The schema separates a rule (leaves a trace) from a practice (leaves none).
GUESS at the order:
(1) Is it in force whether or not you reach for it, for any system whose traits bring it in? Then it is an axiom.
(2) Otherwise, would a reviewer judge it by reading the output? Then it is style.
(3) Otherwise, is it a named procedure that binds once you are in its situation? Then it is a method.
(4) Otherwise, does it leave an observable trace that a check could test? Then it is a rule, and if it does not, it is a practice.

### layer-charters.H7
Handing over: passing work in progress to another agent or to a later session.
`methodology/README.md` (Territory) says: "*Handing over* - passing work in progress to another agent or a later session - has no procedure at all." It adds that "a lone agent hands over to its own next session". `entities/E4-charter.md` gives the same gap as the one a trial territory found ("no procedure for handing work over").

### OBSERVATIONS
- The catalogue-entry schema accepts categories `method`, `rule` and `practice`, with prefixes `RU` and `PC`. `catalog-entry.test.mjs` lists `methods`, `rules` and `practices` as directories. `tools/check-enforcers.sh` loops over `rules/RU*.md` ("from Delta-1"). But none of those directories exist. They have no `0` charter, and neither `README.md` Mechanics nor `INDEX.md` lists them. So questions about these layers can only be answered from schema descriptions and test comments.
- The schema's top-level `description` lists only eight categories (axiom ... mission-required), but its `enum` has seventeen.
- The `README.md` layer table lists `docs/` as a layer, but this corpus has no `docs/` directory.
- `methodology/README.md` says decisions "fall at five moments", but then records handing over as a sixth moment that is outside the table. It also calls "Committing to a design" thin, even though that moment is in the table.
- H7 assumes rules and practices could govern a moment. The corpus only says handover has "no procedure".
- The `methodology/` to `methods/` rename appears only in test comments. No charter mentions it.
