### component.C1
Neither Y nor Z is inside your change's scope: per axioms/README.md (A0), "A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", and your fix modifies only X and depends on no new unit, so "Beyond the scope, improvement is considered, never mandated."
Y is adjacent, since X consumes Y's declared interface (entities/E6-component.md: adjacent means "the two share a declared interface"), and it is the closer kind of adjacent, because "the one the other stands on is the closer for compounding learning".
So A0 permits closing Y's gap opportunistically when A14's tangent discipline supports it: "the payback is near, strongest where the work stands on the component; the yield is captured; and the cost does not significantly distract". A five-minute fix to a defect that has already cost twenty minutes appears to pass all three tests.
If you make it, make it "as a change of its own" rather than folding it into the X fix, and it "answers to every system it alters". If the error text is part of Y's declared contract, that includes Y's consumers. NOT SETTLED: whether an error message counts as part of the interface.
Z fails the test on two counts: it is not adjacent ("X does not use it"), and two days is "large enough to displace the goal". So A0 says it "is recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3) rather than closed", because choosing what work happens next belongs to the director (R4, AR3).
A0 is explicit that "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids", and that applies to Y too if you do not fix it. A14's success signal 1 says the same: friction is "mined to root cause or explicitly deferred with a filed marker".
Z's hidden state matches A1's "Hidden State Problem", but A1 binds only if the service is stateful (traits/T1-stateful.md requires persistence that something treats as authoritative). Even then, A1 imposes no obligation on your X fix, because the fix does not touch Z.

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it. It has one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part, and it exposes and consumes interfaces".
It is not a different kind of thing from a system. "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing" (E6). entities/E5-system.md says the same: "A component is a system seen as a part of a containing system."
What separates the two words is the question you are asking. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6).
A system is "a unit of engineering with a boundary" (E5). "A system nobody contains, such as a whole product seen from outside, is not a component of anything" (E6).
E6 also says a component is "Not a file, module, package, repository or deployable by definition", and that being registered in components/ is "a property of a few, not part of what a component is".

### component.C3
No. E6 says "A component's directory addresses it - where its code and documents live. Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 agrees: "A directory places a component's documents; it is the boundary of neither the system nor the component."
Because no interface is declared, nothing separates thing from other. E6: "A part whose interfaces are undeclared is still a component, but nothing separates it yet: for deciding what a change answers to, it is one system with whatever reaches into it".
So, for binding purposes, thing and other are "one system, with the traits of both" (E5, Boundaries; also A0). A change to thing's internals reaches other, and the axioms of either unit bind it.
The direct reach is itself a fault: "The undeclared dependency is itself a fault against A3, which requires units to interact only through declared contracts" (E5). A3's Air-Gap Principle says "no unit reaches into another's internals".
The remedy, from E5: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." Declaring an interface while leaving the bypass in place "separates nothing".
GUESS: A3's "Bypass as Signal" evaluation is written for reaching past a declared interface, so whether it formally applies when no interface exists is NOT SETTLED. The spirit of A3's Earned Exposure (other is a real consumer) suggests the interface you declare should be shaped to what other actually needs.

### component.C4
The change answers to P and to every system containing P, including the service. A0 says "a change answers to every system it alters. That is the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters."
Q is not altered. Per A0, "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind." E6's blast radius reaches "an adjacent component only if the interface between them changes".
This assumes Q honours the interface. If Q relies on behaviour the interface does not promise, E5 says that is "a contract in fact" and the two "stay one system", so Q would be reached after all.
Q is adjacent, because it consumes what P exposes (E6), so it is a candidate for opportunistic improvement under A0, which covers both directions: "one it stands on, or one that stands on it".
It is the weaker direction. E6: "the other direction is adjacency with less payback, not none". So the A14 tangent test (near payback, yield captured, no significant distraction) is harder to pass.
Any improvement to Q is made as its own change and answers to Q's systems; any gap you leave is recorded with a revival trigger (A0, RU3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A14's tangent discipline defines adjacency narrowly: the tangent "sits under load-bearing infrastructure the main line will stand on". A0 and E6 extend it to both directions. E6 reconciles the two ("the other direction is adjacency with less payback, not none"), but read on its own, A14 suggests that a consumer such as Q in C4 is not adjacent at all.
- The corpus does not say whether error messages, or other behaviour not stated in a signature, are part of a declared interface. That decides whether fixing Y in C1 alters Y's consumers.
- A0 says a component the change "newly depends on" is touched but not altered. It says nothing explicit about a dependency the change merely keeps using, as X does with Y. I inferred "not touched" from the enumerated list.
- A3's "Bypass as Signal" covers reaching past a declared interface. E5 covers undeclared dependencies separately as an A3 fault. No text says whether the interface-evaluation trigger also fires when no interface exists.
- T1 requires persistent state that something treats as authoritative, so it is unclear whether state held only "in process memory" (Z) makes a service stateful and brings A1 into force.
- A14 calls A10's loop "the mining apparatus", but A10 applies only to multi-agent or autonomous systems while A14 applies to any system. For a plain system, the apparatus A14 points to may not be in force.
