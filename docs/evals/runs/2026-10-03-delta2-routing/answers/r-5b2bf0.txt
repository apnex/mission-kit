### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. A declared interface separates T only from neighbours that use it: "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals, parsing output the interface does not promise - stays one system with it" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). For binding, T and O are therefore "one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind" (axioms/README.md, "What brings an axiom into force"; also entities/E6-component.md Definition and "Boundary against directory"). E6's blast-radius row adds that a change reaches "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface". About the bypass itself, the corpus calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, Mechanics, "Bypass as Signal"). A3 then asks for an evaluation of T's interface against T's duty and vision, "ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". Until that happens, O and T stay one system for what a change answers to (A3, same bullet).

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O, and not to P. P uses the declared interface, which your change leaves as it was. "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md). O reaches past the interface, so it stays one system with T and its axioms bind (axioms/README.md; entities/E5-system.md). E5 says "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not." The change also answers to T and to the service that contains T ("every system containing it", axioms/README.md).

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. E5 says "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured"). E6 says the same: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (entities/E6-component.md, Definition). E5 Boundaries also needs both steps: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." Until O's calls move onto the new interface, T and O are one system with the traits of both, and the bypass is still a fault under A3.

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat each bypass as two things: a fault in that consumer, and a signal that T's interface may "fall short of the component's duty" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, "Bypass as Signal"). The detection "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision". The evaluation ends with the consumers moved onto the interface, or with the interface adjusted. A3 Success signal 5 says each detected violation must be "evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer" (also entities/E6-component.md, "Boundary against directory"). Until that ends, each bypassing component stays one system with T for binding. If the fix adjusts T's interface, the change also answers to every consumer it alters, or, for a versioned interface, to each consumer that adopts it (axioms/README.md; entities/E5-system.md). If the fix falls outside the scope of your current change, A0 says to record it with its evidence and a revival trigger under RU3 rather than close it silently (axioms/README.md, "Beyond the scope"). NOT SETTLED: the corpus gives no special rule for several bypassers compared with one. GUESS: several bypassers make it more likely that the interface is the thing that falls short.

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 "Bypass as Signal" says "the two remain one system", but it is written for one consumer. When several components bypass T, the corpus does not say whether the bypassers (for example O1 and O2) also become one system with each other through T. It also does not say whether "one system" is transitive.
- B1/B2 premise conflict: the prompt says the change does not alter T's behaviour, but O depends on internals that the interface does not promise. The corpus handles this by treating O and T as one system, so the conclusion is the same. Still, "does not alter behaviour" cannot be measured against O's contract-in-fact (E5, "A declared boundary against an assumed one").
- T and O are both inside one service, and the service contains both, so the service's axioms bind the change anyway (axioms/README.md, "every system containing it"). The corpus does not say whether a contained component's traits are also counted as the container's traits. So it is unclear how much the "O's axioms" ruling adds beyond the containment rule.
- A0 and E5 use the phrase "one unit depends on another", while E6 and A3 talk about "reaching into" or calling internals. They clearly mean the same thing, but the wording is different.
