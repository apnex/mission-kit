### system-boundary.S3
The change answers to the service's axioms: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14.
The module is a system contained by the service: "A module inside a service is a system, and the service contains it" (entities/E5-system.md, "Contain"). A change answers to "every system containing it" (axioms/README.md).
The module being stateless on its own does not matter. E5 ("Why precision matters") names that exact escape as the error: "a stateful system escapes its own axioms by being described as stateless modules, each of which passes on its own."
The module's own traits add only the floor, which is already in the set.

### system-boundary.S4
The change answers only to the library's axioms, which are the floor: A3, A4, A8, A9, A14. axioms/README.md says "a plain library takes `any-system`".
The ten consumers compose with the library through a declared, versioned contract that the change leaves unchanged. So they are not altered, and their axioms do not bind (axioms/README.md; entities/E5-system.md, "A versioned contract changes only for the systems that adopt the new version").
Each consumer's adoption is a separate change, and it answers to that consumer's axioms. "the component is not altered by being adopted" (E5).
E5 gives the reason: otherwise "a small shared library would carry the axioms of every system that uses it" ("Why precision matters").

### system-boundary.S6
It is defined in entities/E5-system.md: "A system is a unit of engineering with a boundary: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it."
The boundary separates a system from a neighbour only where it is "declared and honoured". Otherwise the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to.
Systems relate in exactly two ways: they contain or they compose. Traits describe a system, and axioms bind it.
A system is not a repository, a deployment unit or a process, and it is not defined by size or traits. "A one-shot script with no traits is a system" (E5, Boundaries).
entities/E6-component.md adds that a component is a system seen as a part of a containing system.

### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful and multi-agent, so A1 binds it. A1 says "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth", and it names this fault: "Hidden State Problem - state lives inside a process" (axioms/A1-sovereign-state-transparency.md). A5 also binds, through multi-agent.
For your feature (inside scope), the direction rule applies: "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names" (axioms/README.md). So your feature's own state must be durable and perceivable by the other agents. It must not be held in process memory.
If your feature newly depends on that component, the component is touched but not altered, and "its gaps must not be built on" (axioms/README.md). So do not make your feature rely on the hidden in-memory state.
For the component itself: if your change does not touch it, "improvement is considered, never mandated" (axioms/README.md).
If the component is adjacent (it shares a declared interface with what you touch, per entities/E6-component.md), you may close the gap opportunistically under A14 tangent discipline, as a separate change. Otherwise record it with evidence and a revival trigger (rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md).
"Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids." What work happens next is the director's choice (axioms/README.md, citing R4 and AR3).

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward. It is not a pass/fail test (axioms/README.md, "Standing commitment, not situated move").
When you change a system the axiom binds, it gives your change a direction. Your change must not move the system away from the limit, and it must move it toward the limit "as far as the change's own scope allows". In A0's words: "never backwards, and forward with every step."
The charter keeps a correction banner that retracts the earlier invariant/pass-fail reading, and it names that reading as a fault: "The conformance reading". Under that reading no change advances the system, and gaps become permanent.
A gate can still check the direction rule as a binary question: the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched.
Beyond your scope, the axiom asks you to consider improvement and to record any gap you pass. It does not mandate fixing it.
