### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes, your change answers to O's axioms. O reaching past T's declared interface means the boundary is not "declared and honoured", so "the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to" (entities/E5-system.md line 16). A0 says that in that case "the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind" (axioms/README.md line 118). E6 says the same: code that "reaches past the interface ... stays one system with the component until it uses the interface" (entities/E6-component.md line 44). On the bypass, the corpus treats it as "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, Bypass as Signal). A detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" (A3 line 24). A0 line 118 also calls the undeclared dependency "itself a fault under A3". Separately, the service that contains T binds as well: a change answers to "every system containing it" (axioms/README.md line 116).

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O, not to P (and also to T and to the containing service). "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not" (entities/E5-system.md line 43). P composes with T through a contract your change leaves alone, and "a system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md line 117). O bypasses, so O and T are one system with the traits of both, and O's axioms bind (axioms/README.md line 118; E6 line 44). E6's blast-radius row agrees: a change reaches an adjacent component "only if the interface between them changes", plus "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface" (entities/E6-component.md line 101).

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. They are still one system for deciding what a change answers to. E5 says it directly: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing" (entities/E5-system.md line 42). E6 says the same: a component is separated from a neighbour "only where [its interfaces] are declared and that neighbour uses them", and "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (entities/E6-component.md lines 17-19). E5 line 71 agrees: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again." So you have done the first half only. O's calls are now a bypass of a declared interface, which is an A3 fault and triggers A3's Bypass-as-Signal evaluation (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md line 24).

### boundary-bypass.B4
Apply A3's Bypass as Signal to each bypass. Treat each one as "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface: it may mean the interface falls short of the component's duty". Evaluate T's interface "against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md line 24). A3 success signal 5 asks that each detected violation be "evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". Until that resolves, each bypassing component stays one system with T for what a change answers to (A3 line 24; E5 line 43). If you adjust the interface, that change answers to every consumer whose contract it alters, or for a versioned interface to those that adopt it (axioms/README.md line 83; E5 lines 50-52). If you do not fix it now, A0 says gaps outside a change's scope are recorded with evidence and a revival trigger under RU3 rather than passed silently (axioms/README.md lines 82-84). NOT SETTLED: the corpus says nothing about several bypassers as opposed to one, or about who owns the evaluation and when it must happen. GUESS: several independent bypasses are stronger evidence that the interface, not the consumers, falls short of T's duty.

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 says "Until then the two remain one system", where "then" is the end of the evaluation. One possible ending is "the interface being adjusted", but by E5/E6 adjusting the interface does not separate anything until O actually uses it. A3's wording could be read as saying the evaluation alone separates them.
- E6's blast-radius row (line 101) names "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface". That is narrower than E5/A0's "declared and honoured", which also covers reaching past a declared interface. The B1/B2 case is settled by E5/A0/E6 line 44, not by that row.
- A3's Bypass as Signal does not say who runs the evaluation, how soon it must happen, or whether it falls inside the scope of a change that merely detects the bypass. A0's RU3 recording rule is the closest fallback.
- The questions don't say whether O and T together carry traits that bring in extra axioms. "Traits of both" means the set of binding axioms can grow, but which axioms that is depends on facts that aren't given.
