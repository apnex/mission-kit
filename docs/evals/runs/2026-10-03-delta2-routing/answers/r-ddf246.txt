### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. A boundary "separates it from a neighbour only where it is declared and honoured"; a unit that "reaches past one that was declared" is "one system for deciding what a change answers to" (entities/E5-system.md, Definition and "Declared against honoured": "calling internals ... stays one system with it").
axioms/README.md ("What brings an axiom into force") says that where a boundary is "one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". E6's blast-radius row also includes "any unit that reaches it, or that it reaches, with no declared interface" (entities/E6-component.md, "Why precision matters").
On the bypass itself, the corpus calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, Mechanics, "Bypass as Signal").
A3 says a detected bypass "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted"; until then the two stay one system (A3; also E6 "Boundary against directory").
NOT SETTLED: whether your internals change must do that evaluation or fix itself, or may record it as a gap with a revival trigger (axioms/README.md: "work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows", otherwise record per RU3). GUESS: you at least record the bypass, because "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids".

### boundary-bypass.B2
It answers to O and not to P (assuming, as stated, that T's interface and behaviour do not change).
P uses the interface, so P composes with T: "a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not" - "The separation is per pair" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured").
"A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md), so P's axioms do not bind.
O reached past the declared interface, so O is one system with T, and O's axioms bind (axioms/README.md; E6 blast-radius row).
The change also answers to T itself and to "every system containing it", which includes the service (axioms/README.md: "the system it modifies, every system containing it").

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing" (entities/E5-system.md, "Declared against honoured").
E6 says separation needs both conditions: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (entities/E6-component.md, Definition). E5 Boundaries agrees: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again".
For deciding what a change answers to, T and O stay one system with the traits of both. Any neighbour that does use the new interface is separated from T, pair by pair.
O's continued reach into T's internals is now a bypass of a declared interface, which A3 calls a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md, "Bypass as Signal").

### boundary-bypass.B4
Treat each detected bypass as more than a consumer fault to correct. A3 Success signal 5 says each detected boundary violation "is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md).
Evaluate T's interface "against the component's duty and its vision". That evaluation ends either "in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted", because the bypass "may mean the interface falls short of the component's duty" (A3, "Bypass as Signal"; E6 "Boundary against directory").
Until that is resolved, every bypassing component stays one system with T, so changes to T answer to all their axioms (A3; axioms/README.md).
If the interface changes, the change answers to every consumer whose contract it alters, or for a versioned interface each consumer as it adopts the new version (axioms/README.md; entities/E5-system.md, versioned-contract discriminator).
NOT SETTLED: whether several bypassers count as stronger evidence than one, and who must do the evaluation. GUESS: several independent bypassers point to the interface falling short of T's duty more than to separate consumer faults. If the evaluation is out of the current change's scope, record it with its evidence and a revival trigger (RU3, cited in axioms/README.md).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 asks for evaluation "against the component's duty and its vision". It does not say whose vision (T's, the containing service's, or a programme vision in the AR6 sense), and a component is not shown to have a vision of its own.
- A3 "Bypass as Signal" says a detected bypass "triggers an evaluation" but does not say who must do it, or whether it falls inside the scope of a change to T's internals. axioms/README.md scope ("what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters") does not clearly include a bypassing consumer that you did not modify. Yet that consumer is still bound, through the one-system collapse. So O's axioms bind the change, but whether the change must also close O's bypass gap is left open.
- axioms/README.md defines "touched" through modification, dependency and contract change, and "altered" as the binding set. A bypassing O is altered (collapsed with T) without being touched in that sense. The two sets are said to differ ("Scope and binding are different sets"), but the text does not work through the bypass case explicitly.
- E5 "Contains against composes" says two units that reach each other with no declared contract, "neither ... part of the other", "do neither". T and O are both contained in the service, so they are siblings. The text treats them as one system for binding, but it does not say how that one-system collapse relates to their shared container.
- None of the texts covers several bypassers specifically; the rules are stated per pair or per bypass.
