### component-a3.C5
A3 names the existing module as a fault: "God-Object Accretion - "utils", "helpers", "managers" accumulate unrelated concerns." The Mandate binds a change that falls short of the limit: it "adds none of them, and removes what it touches". So your feature must not go into `utils`, and any part of `utils` that your change touches should be separated out rather than left mixed. The feature goes into its own unit, because "a module does exactly one thing" (Law of One) and "a concern earns an internal boundary by being one concern" (Earned Exposure). Assemble it by composition, because "a new capability is assembled by composing existing units, never by modifying them", and have it interact only "through declared adapters and contracts" (Air-Gap Principle). Do not promote the new unit to a stable, depended-upon surface until "a real consumer outside its origin needs it"; doing so earlier is the "Speculative Surface" fault. NOT SETTLED: how much of `utils` counts as "what it touches". GUESS: only the concerns your change reads or modifies, not a full decomposition of the module.

### OBSERVATIONS
- "removes what it touches" (Mandate) does not define its scope. It could mean only the code the change edits, or every accretion the change reaches, such as everything in `utils` if the change calls into it.
- Possible tension: "Composable by Default" says capabilities arrive "never by modifying" existing units. But removing the accretion you touch in `utils` requires modifying an existing unit. The file does not say which rule governs when the two conflict.
- The file defers to `A0` ("How an axiom binds a change is stated in A0") for how an axiom binds a change, and also cites it in Bypass as Signal. A0 is outside this file, so the exact obligation on a partial change is not fully settled here.
