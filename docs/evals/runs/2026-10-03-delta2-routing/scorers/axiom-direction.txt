PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-12e26220 ---
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward. It is not a pass/fail test (axioms/README.md, "Standing commitment, not situated move").
When you change a system the axiom binds, it gives your change a direction. Your change must not move the system away from the limit, and it must move it toward the limit "as far as the change's own scope allows". In A0's words: "never backwards, and forward with every step."
The charter keeps a correction banner that retracts the earlier invariant/pass-fail reading, and it names that reading as a fault: "The conformance reading". Under that reading no change advances the system, and gaps become permanent.
A gate can still check the direction rule as a binary question: the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched.
Beyond your scope, the axiom asks you to consider improvement and to record any gap you pass. It does not mandate fixing it.

That scope is what the change modifies, what it depends on for the first time, and any consumer whose contract it alters. Outside that scope, improvement is optional, and a gap you leave must still be recorded.
