### component.C1
Y: the axioms do not require you to fix it, but they expect you to consider it. Y is adjacent to your change because X "stands on" it through a declared interface, and A0 (axioms/README.md) says "Beyond the scope, improvement is encouraged, never mandated... expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". Tangent discipline (A14) supports closing it: the friction is in a part your work stands on, which E6 calls the "closer" direction with the highest payback, the cost is five minutes, and A14 says "Friction -> root cause, never workaround". A0 says to make it "as a change of its own" that "answers to every system it alters". So if the error text is part of Y's declared interface, Y's consumers are also altered (or, for a versioned interface, only the consumers that adopt it). NOT SETTLED: whether an error message is part of Y's contract, and whether the change must wait until your X fix has landed. Z: X does not use Z, so Z is not adjacent, and two days of work would "displace the goal". A0 therefore says to record the gap "with its evidence and a revival trigger ([RU3])" rather than fix it, and to leave larger choices to the director; "Either way the gap is recorded", and passing it silently is forbidden. GUESS: if Z's in-memory state passes the T1 test, the service that contains it is stateful (E5: "A system has every trait any part it contains has"), so A1 binds your X change. In that case your change may not add hidden state, but it does not have to close Z's gap, because it does not touch Z.

### component.C2
The term is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it". It has "one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part" and "exposes and consumes interfaces", and "Its boundary is those interfaces". It is not a different kind of thing: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system". E6 tells them apart by the question being asked. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system", while "Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component". entities/E5-system.md defines a system as "a unit of engineering with a boundary" that traits describe and axioms bind, and says that a system nobody contains is not a component (E6). E6 also says a component is not a file, module, package, repository or deployable by definition, and it does not have to be registered in components/ or be shareable.

### component.C3
No. E6 "Boundary against directory": "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". parts/thing/ is still a component, but "nothing separates it yet". For deciding what a change answers to, it is one system with parts/other/, with the traits of both (E6 Definition, E5 Boundaries). The fault is the undeclared dependency itself, which is "a fault against A3" (E5). A3's Air-Gap Principle says "no unit reaches into another's internals". The remedy is to declare the interface and move parts/other/ onto it: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it" (E6). Under E5, a change that alters what parts/other/ reaches into "owns that boundary", so declaring and honouring the interface is inside that change's scope.

### component.C4
Per A0 ("a change answers to every system it alters"), your change answers to P and to every system containing P, which here means the service. It does not answer to Q, because "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (A0; E6's blast-radius row says the same). Q is not altered, but only if Q honours the interface. If Q reaches past it into P's internals, the two are one system and Q is reached (E5 "Declared against honoured"). Q is adjacent, since it consumes what P exposes (E6), and A0 counts both "one it stands on, or one that stands on it". So Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement, subject to A14's tangent discipline. E6 says this direction (Q stands on P, your work is on P) is "adjacency with less payback, not none". GUESS: Q's own traits still reach your change indirectly through the service, because the service has every trait its parts have (E5).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 says the axioms of a composing system that is not altered "do not bind", but E5 gives the containing service every trait of every part it contains, Q and Z included. So axioms that Q or Z bring in still bind a change to P or X through the service. The corpus does not reconcile these two statements, which matters for C1 (Z and A1) and C4 (Q).
- A0 defines "touched" as "what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters". It does not say whether an existing dependency that the change uses unchanged (X's call to Y) counts as touched or only as adjacent.
- A14's tangent discipline describes adjacency in one direction only (infrastructure "the main line will stand on"), while A0 and E6 count both directions. E6 reconciles this by saying A14 means "the closer direction", but A14's own text does not say so.
- The corpus does not say whether error-message text is part of a declared interface. E5 only says that parsing output "the interface does not promise" is a bypass.
- A3 Bypass as Signal covers reaching past a declared interface. The C3 case, where no interface is declared at all, is covered by E5 and E6 rather than by an A3 mechanic.
- A3's mandate says "Every module is a self-contained sovereign unit", while E6 says a module is not a component by definition. This is a minor terminology mismatch.
