PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-9e2cb4c4 ---
An axiom is a direction, not a pass/fail test. A0 calls it "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward to stay aligned with its architecture's intent", and a system "is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
So when I change a bound system, the axiom tells me two things at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
In practice: do not repeat a gap the axiom names, close what my change touches as far as the touch allows, and record (not silently pass) gaps beyond my scope.
A0 names the opposite reading as a fault: "The conformance reading", under which "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent". The CORRECTION banner in A0 records the director ruling for the asymptote reading.
Every axiom in force binds every change, and none can be traded against another: "a regression with thirteen consolations, not partial progress".
A gate checks the reachable part of this: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched."
