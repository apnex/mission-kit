### system-boundary.S6
It is defined in `entities/E5-system.md`: "A **system** is a unit of engineering with a **boundary**: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it."
"The boundary separates it from its neighbours only where it is **declared**; where one unit depends on another across a boundary nobody declared, the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to."
"Traits describe a system, and axioms bind it." Systems relate in exactly two ways: *contain* and *compose*.
E5 says a system is not a repository, a deployment unit or a process by definition, and is not defined by size or by traits.
`entities/E6-component.md` adds that a component is "a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it".

### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful and multi-agent, so A1 binds it. That component is A1's named fault, the "Hidden State Problem - state lives inside a process; other actors reason about a different reality". It also breaks A1's mandate that "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth".
Under A0, how far a change must move is "set by its scope", which is what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and consumers whose contract it alters. "Beyond the scope, improvement is considered, never mandated."
If your feature does not touch the component, you are not required to fix it. If the component is adjacent (it shares a declared interface with your work, E6), you may fix it as a separate change under A14's tangent discipline. Otherwise you record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5)". A0: "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids."
For your feature, "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names". So your feature keeps its own state in the shared, durable, queryable backplane (A1 mechanics), and other agents can perceive it (A5).
If your feature would depend on that component's in-memory state, that is a gap you would be building on. A0 says gaps in what a change newly depends on "must not be built on", and a touched gap is closed "as much ... as the touch allows".
NOT SETTLED: whether your feature touches or is adjacent to the component. The scenario does not say, so whether you must fix it, may fix it, or should only record it is open.

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom is a limit you build the system toward. It is not a pass-or-fail test. A0: "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward". "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
So when you change a bound system, the axiom points the change in a direction: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
A0's correction banner keeps this reading on purpose. Under pass-or-fail, "nearly every real system fails nearly every axiom, and the rule gives no direction". A0 names "the conformance reading" as a fault.
Axioms are conjunctive: none may be traded against another (A0 *How the set composes*).
A gate checks the direction rule: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched" (A0).
