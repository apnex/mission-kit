PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-d6bd3390 ---
An axiom is a limit you build the system toward. It is not a pass-or-fail test. A0: "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward". "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
So when you change a bound system, the axiom points the change in a direction: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
A0's correction banner keeps this reading on purpose. Under pass-or-fail, "nearly every real system fails nearly every axiom, and the rule gives no direction". A0 names "the conformance reading" as a fault.
Axioms are conjunctive: none may be traded against another (A0 *How the set composes*).
A gate checks the direction rule: "that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched" (A0).

--- answer a-6a2f6a2c ---
An axiom is a direction, not a pass/fail test: it is "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward" (`axioms/README.md`).
When you change a bound system it binds that change two ways at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
A system short of the limit is not wrong for that; "it is wrong for moving away from it."
The CORRECTION banner in `axioms/README.md` retracts the earlier "hold or fail" wording, and the fault "The conformance reading" explains why: treated as pass/fail, "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent."
Gates fit this: they check that the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched." Axioms in force also cannot be traded off against each other ("no axiom may be traded against another").

--- answer a-b8f6d586 ---
An axiom is a limit (an "asymptote") that the system is built toward so it stays aligned with its architecture's intent. It is not a checklist the change must pass (`axioms/README.md`, "Standing commitment, not situated move").
For a change, it does two things at once: the change must not move the system away from the limit, and it must move the system toward the limit as far as its scope allows. "never backwards, and forward with every step."
A system short of the limit is not wrong for that. It is wrong only if it moves away. The "conformance reading" is a named fault because, under it, "no change advances the system" (A0 Faults).
Axioms are conjunctive and cannot be traded against each other. A gate can check the direction rule: the change moved no further from any limit and repeated no gap it touched.
