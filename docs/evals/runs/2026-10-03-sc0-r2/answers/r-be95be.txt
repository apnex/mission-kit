### sc0.Q1
Not in `spec`. schemas/README.md (SC0), "Resource convention": "`spec` holds all configuration that affects entity meaning or behaviour" and "Runtime observations do not belong in `spec`."
The text continues: "A kind only gains `status` when a real reconciler needs observed state."
So observed state such as a last-checked time goes in a `status` block, and that block is added to a kind only once a real reconciler needs it. Until then the kind gets no such field at all.
`metadata` is also ruled out: it "holds portable identity and machine-oriented labels or annotations", not observations.
NOT SETTLED: what shape `status` takes. No schema in the set defines one today. GUESS: Kubernetes-style `status.<field>`, given the "Kubernetes-like envelope".

### sc0.Q2
No. schemas/README.md "Composition": a process "must not inject process fields into the Question resource". Instead it composes a complete Question inside its own wrapper, and the example `SurveyQuestion` carries `spec.survey.round: 1` beside `spec.question`.
The stated reason: "This wrapper boundary keeps the neutral resource closed and prevents Survey evolution from changing the Question contract."
schemas/SC4-question.md agrees: Question "contains no Survey, round, Director, interpretation, or outcome-axis semantics", and "a composing process owns placement such as round and ordinal".
The schema enforces it too: question/v1alpha1/question.schema.json sets `"additionalProperties": false`.

### sc0.Q3
Structural first (JSON Schema), then semantic (the kind's validator, found through `catalog.json`). schemas/README.md "Validation": "A consumer must run structural validation before semantic validation, because each semantic validator assumes the shape the schema guarantees - it reads fields without first checking they exist."
The split exists because JSON Schema validates structure, while the semantic validators check invariants that "JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 cannot express". Examples are unique option IDs, min <= max selections, and no statement in both included and excluded scope.
To resolve absolute URN references, consumers can first preload every schema listed in `catalog.json`.

### sc0.Q4
schemas/README.md "Territory" divides the contracts "by whose instances they govern" into two kinds:
(1) "The corpus's own files": "documents this corpus is made of, held to their shape on every change", checked by the gate `tools/check-all.sh`. Members are SC1 (catalogue frontmatter), SC2 (standing context), SC3 (skill bodies) and SC6 (entry bodies).
(2) "Process resources": "documents a process writes - a question, the context it is answered against - neutral of any one process", checked by their semantic validators and the schema suite. Members are SC4 (Question) and SC5 (ContextFrame).
"Every contract sits in one row." SC2 and SC3 are portable, but they stay in row 1 because their instances are documents of the kind this corpus is made of.
SC0 is the charter of the set, not a contract itself, even though its frontmatter says `category: contract`.
Also, `common/v1alpha1/resource-metadata` and `choice-response` are listed in `catalog.json` as "fragment", not as contracts.

### sc0.Q5
A change to the existing contract, SC1. schemas/README.md "Boundaries and composition" says: "one concern, one contract - catalogue frontmatter is one schema with per-category conditions, not a schema per layer."
catalog-entry.schema.json already works this way: one `category` enum plus per-category `if`/`then` blocks (for example for `axiom`, `work-type`, `component`, `rule`). So the new layer means adding its category to the enum and adding an `if category == new` block that allows or requires the two fields.
Why: a second schema for the same concern would be the "Two contracts for one concern" fault ("each authoritative, free to drift").
Also, if the layer gets entry-body shapes, SC6's categories must change too, because "Contracts that must agree, such as `SC6`'s categories and `SC1`'s category list, are held together by a test."
GUESS: the generator may also need editing to see a new layer (MREQ-4's trigger suggests so). I did not read that.

### sc0.Q6
Yes. schemas/README.md "Gaps the territory exposes" lists "the evaluation suites and their results, the human-evaluation suites, `catalog.json` itself, and `bundles/*.yaml`". Each is "checked only by hand-written code in a tool or not at all".
One example is `bundles/*.yaml` (bundles/nanoprobe.yaml, sysml-modelling.yaml, workgraph-arc.yaml). No contract governs these, so they show the "ungoverned file" fault: "its shape is whatever the last writer chose."

### sc0.Q7
schemas/README.md "Faults" names six signs:
- the unchecked contract (a schema nothing validates against: "reads as enforced and holds nothing");
- two contracts for one concern;
- the drifting pair (contracts that must agree with nothing holding them together);
- the unmapped resource (a kind missing from `catalog.json`; "`EntryBody` is one today");
- the contract ahead of its consumer (no process uses it; "`SC4` and `SC5` today");
- the ungoverned file.
Its "Boundaries" section adds a seventh risk: a tool that re-implements a contract by hand can drift from it (`check-standing-context.sh` vs SC2), and in that case the contract is authoritative.
By its own account, today's population already shows three of these faults: unmapped resource, contract ahead of consumer, and ungoverned files.

### OBSERVATIONS
- SC6 does not fit its row cleanly. SC0 places it under "The corpus's own files", but its schema uses the process-resource envelope (`apiVersion: schemas.mission-kit/v1alpha1`, `kind: EntryBody`). Its instance (entry-body/v1alpha1/entry-body.json) is a resource and is not in `catalog.json` `resources`. The claim "Every contract sits in one row" is weaker than it reads.
- `catalog.json` `resources` maps only Question and ContextFrame. The SC1/SC2/SC3 schemas appear in `schemas` with `"role": "resource"`, but they have no apiVersion/kind envelope and no `resources` entry. "Resource" therefore means different things in the two arrays.
- SC0 says `catalog.json` is itself ungoverned, yet it is the file consumers rely on to resolve schemas. A fault in it would quietly break the "preload every schema" path.
- SC0's own frontmatter says `category: contract`, even though it is the charter rather than a contract. Its `related` cites E4, which suggests charter.
- Q1: no schema defines a `status` shape, so where observed state goes is settled only in principle.
- I did not verify that `tools/check-all.sh` actually validates SC1/SC2/SC3/SC6 instances. The "Checked by" column is SC0's claim, not something I measured.
