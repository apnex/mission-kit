### sc0.Q1
Not in `spec`: schemas/README.md (SC0), "Resource convention", says "`spec` holds all configuration that affects entity meaning or behaviour" and "Runtime observations do not belong in `spec`."
The place for observed state is `status`, but only under a condition: "A kind only gains `status` when a real reconciler needs observed state."
So a last-checked timestamp would go in a `status` block, and that block may be added only if a real reconciler consumes it. Without such a reconciler the kind gets no `status`, and the observation stays outside the resource.
It also does not go in `metadata`, which SC0 reserves for "portable identity and machine-oriented labels or annotations".
GUESS: if nothing reconciles on it, the timestamp belongs in whatever process or tool record does the checking, not in the resource at all. The corpus does not say where.

### sc0.Q2
No.
SC0 "Composition" says a process "must not inject process fields into the Question resource". It gives the example of a `SurveyQuestion` wrapper that holds the full `Question` under `spec.question` and the survey fields, including `round: 1`, under `spec.survey`.
SC4 (schemas/SC4-question.md) agrees: Question "contains no Survey, round, Director, interpretation, or outcome-axis semantics", and "a composing process owns placement such as round and ordinal."
The reason given is that "This wrapper boundary keeps the neutral resource closed and prevents Survey evolution from changing the Question contract."
The schema enforces this too: question.schema.json sets `additionalProperties: false`, so an added `round` field would fail validation.

### sc0.Q3
Structural validation (JSON Schema) comes first, then semantic validation (the `*.validator.mjs` for the kind).
SC0 "Validation" gives the reason: "each semantic validator assumes the shape the schema guarantees - it reads fields without first checking they exist."
The schema covers structure, and the semantic validators check invariants JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 "cannot express". Examples are unique option IDs, cardinality bounds, constraint references, and duplicate scope, given or term entries.
I read part of question.validator.mjs, and it matches this: for example it calls `constraint.optionIds.length` and `conflicts.get(left).add(...)` with no existence checks.
SC0 also says consumers can preload every schema in `catalog.json` to resolve the URN references.

### sc0.Q4
SC0 "Territory" splits the contracts by "whose instances they govern", which it calls "the denominator". There are two kinds.
(1) "The corpus's own files": documents this corpus is made of, checked on every change by the gate `tools/check-all.sh`. Members are SC1 catalogue frontmatter, SC2 standing context, SC3 skill bodies and SC6 entry bodies.
(2) "Process resources": documents a process writes, "neutral of any one process", checked by their semantic validators and the schema suite. Members are SC4 Question and SC5 ContextFrame.
SC0 notes that SC2 and SC3 are portable too, but they sit in row (1) because "their instances are documents of the kind this corpus is made of."
SC6 is unusual: its instance, entry-body.json, uses the `apiVersion`/`kind: EntryBody` resource envelope, yet SC6 is placed in row (1).
SC0 itself is the charter (category `contract` in its frontmatter), not a member of either row.

### sc0.Q5
It is a change to an existing contract, SC1 (catalog-entry), not a new schema.
SC0 "Boundaries and composition" says: "one concern, one contract - catalogue frontmatter is one schema with per-category conditions, not a schema per layer."
The schema follows that design: catalog-entry.schema.json has a category enum and several `if: {category: ...}` conditional blocks. It also sets `additionalProperties: false`, so the two new fields must be declared there or they will fail.
The new category must also be added to SC1's category enum. If the layer's bodies are governed, it must be added to SC6's entry-body.json as well, because "SC6's categories and SC1's category list are held together by a test."
A separate schema would create the fault SC0 calls "Two contracts for one concern ... free to drift."

### sc0.Q6
Yes.
SC0 "Territory" says: "Structured files this corpus relies on that no contract governs ... the evaluation suites and their results, the human-evaluation suites, `catalog.json` itself, and `bundles/*.yaml`."
Example: `bundles/*.yaml` (bundles/nanoprobe.yaml, sysml-modelling.yaml, workgraph-arc.yaml). Another is `schemas/catalog.json`, which no schema in schemas/ governs.
SC0's Faults list calls this "The ungoverned file", a file whose "shape is whatever the last writer chose."

### sc0.Q7
SC0 "Faults" lists the signs:
- "The unchecked contract": a schema no check validates against.
- "Two contracts for one concern": two authoritative schemas for one kind of document.
- "The drifting pair": two contracts that must agree, with nothing holding them together.
- "The unmapped resource": a resource kind missing from `catalog.json`. SC0 says `EntryBody` is one today, and catalog.json does list the entry-body schema but has no EntryBody entry under `resources`.
- "The contract ahead of its consumer": maintained on speculation. SC4 and SC5 are this today.
- "The ungoverned file": a structured file the corpus relies on that no contract governs.
"Against tools/" adds one more risk: a tool that re-implements a contract by hand (check-standing-context.sh against SC2) can drift from it.

### OBSERVATIONS
- The population shows some of its own faults today, as SC0 admits: EntryBody is unmapped in catalog.json, SC4 and SC5 have no consumer, and several files are ungoverned. The charter itself is consistent on this.
- catalog.json gives `"role": "resource"` to the catalog-entry, standing-context and skill schemas, but they are frontmatter contracts with no apiVersion/kind envelope, and they are absent from `resources`. "resource" therefore means two things: schema role versus envelope resource kind. SC0 names only EntryBody as unmapped, which is consistent only under the envelope meaning.
- SC6 sits in the "corpus's own files" row while its instance uses the process-resource envelope (`apiVersion`/`kind: EntryBody`). SC0 does not explain this.
- SC1, SC2, SC3, SC4 and SC6 entries have `related: []` even where the text depends on another contract (SC6 cites SC1; SC4 cites ContextFrame/SC5).
- SC0 says the semantic validators read fields without checking they exist. I checked this only for part of question.validator.mjs; the ContextFrame validator was not read.
