### sc0.Q1
Not in `spec`: schemas/README.md (SC0) "Resource convention" says "Runtime observations do not belong in `spec`" and "A kind only gains `status` when a real reconciler needs observed state."
So observed state such as a last-checked time goes in a `status` block, and only once a real reconciler exists that needs it.
No kind has one today; SC5 adds that "observed state remain[s] outside the resource" for ContextFrame.
The Question schema sets `additionalProperties: false` at top level with only apiVersion/kind/metadata/spec, so a `status` field would currently fail validation (measured in question/v1alpha1/question.schema.json).
GUESS: if no reconciler exists yet, record the observation outside the resource entirely.
NOT SETTLED: the shape of a `status` block; no contract defines one.

### sc0.Q2
No. SC4-question.md says Question "contains no Survey, round, Director, interpretation, or outcome-axis semantics", and that "a composing process owns placement such as round and ordinal."
SC0 "Composition" says a process "must not inject process fields into the Question resource" and gives the pattern: a wrapper such as `SurveyQuestion` whose `spec.question` holds the complete Question and `spec.survey` holds `round: 1`, `ordinal`, `intentDimension`.
Stated reason: "This wrapper boundary keeps the neutral resource closed and prevents Survey evolution from changing the Question contract."
Mechanically, `spec` has `additionalProperties: false` in question.schema.json, so a `round` field would be rejected anyway.

### sc0.Q3
Structural (JSON Schema) validation first, then semantic validation (SC0 "Validation").
The reason given: "each semantic validator assumes the shape the schema guarantees - it reads fields without first checking they exist."
Semantic validators exist because they check things JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 cannot express, e.g. unique option IDs and satisfiable cardinality for Question, and duplicate scope, givens and terms for ContextFrame.
catalog.json maps each resource kind to both its `schemaId` and its `semanticValidator`, and consumers can preload every schema it lists to resolve URN references.

### sc0.Q4
SC0 "Territory" divides contracts "by whose instances they govern".
(1) The corpus's own files: documents this corpus is made of, checked on every change by the gate `tools/check-all.sh`. These are SC1 (catalogue frontmatter), SC2 (standing context), SC3 (skill bodies) and SC6 (entry bodies).
(2) Process resources: documents a process writes, neutral of any one process, checked by their semantic validators and the schema suite. These are SC4 (Question) and SC5 (ContextFrame).
SC0 notes SC2 and SC3 are portable too, but "their instances are documents of the kind this corpus is made of", so they sit in row 1.
SC0 itself is the charter (README.md), not a contract.

### sc0.Q5
A change to an existing one, SC1 catalog-entry.
SC0 "Boundaries and composition": "one concern, one contract - catalogue frontmatter is one schema with per-category conditions, not a schema per layer."
The schema already works this way (measured): category-specific fields such as `trace`, `members` and `sovereignty` are optional properties, made required by `allOf` if/then clauses keyed on category and ID.
A second schema would be the "Two contracts for one concern" fault in SC0 "Faults".
The same edit also has to add the category to SC1's `category` enum and its prefix to the `id` pattern, because `additionalProperties: false` refuses undeclared fields.
GUESS: if the layer's bodies are governed too, add it to SC6's entry-body.json. SC0 says SC6's categories and SC1's category list "are held together by a test".

### sc0.Q6
Yes. SC0 "Territory" ("Gaps the territory exposes") lists: "the evaluation suites and their results, the human-evaluation suites, `catalog.json` itself, and `bundles/*.yaml`". Each is "checked only by hand-written code in a tool or not at all".
One example is `bundles/*.yaml` (the corpus does have a `bundles/` directory).
SC0 "Faults" names this "The ungoverned file", one "whose shape is whatever the last writer chose".

### sc0.Q7
SC0 "Faults" lists six:
- an unchecked contract, which reads as enforced and holds nothing;
- two contracts for one concern, free to drift;
- a drifting pair of contracts that must agree, with nothing holding them together;
- an unmapped resource missing from `catalog.json`;
- a contract ahead of its consumer, maintained on speculation;
- an ungoverned file the corpus relies on.
SC0 says two of these are present today: EntryBody is unmapped, and SC4 and SC5 have no consumer.
Also relevant, from "Boundaries": a tool that re-implements a contract by hand (`check-standing-context.sh` against SC2) can drift from it, and the contract is authoritative.

### OBSERVATIONS
- catalog.json "schemas" gives the SC1, SC2 and SC3 frontmatter schemas `role: "resource"`. But they are not apiVersion/kind envelopes, so "resource" means something different here than in the SC0 resource convention. They also have no entry under "resources".
- EntryBody (SC6) uses the apiVersion/kind envelope and its schema is listed in catalog.json "schemas", but it is missing from "resources". SC0 admits this as a fault ("The unmapped resource"). SC6 also has no semantic validator, while SC0 says the catalogue maps each resource to "its semantic validator".
- SC0 says `catalog.json` is itself ungoverned, yet it is the file consumers are told to preload. Its shape has no contract.
- SC0's frontmatter `related` lists SC1, SC6, A2, A8 and E4, but every member entry SC1-SC6 has `related: []`.
- The SC1-SC3 bodies start with a lower-case sentence ("the frontmatter every catalogue entry must satisfy.") that looks generated from the title.
- SC4 and SC5 say their sections "Moved here from the schemas charter, SC0, when it was converted to E4". That is only a provenance note, but it is the only hint of the conversion.
- check-all.sh says "check-charter-shape.sh is SUSPENDED", so any charter-shape check of SC0 does not run.
