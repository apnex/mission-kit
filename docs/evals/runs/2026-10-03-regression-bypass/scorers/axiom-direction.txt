PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-408bde8c ---
An axiom is a direction to build toward, not a test to pass: A0 calls it "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward", and "a system is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it" (axioms/README.md).
So when I change a bound system, the axiom tells me two things at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
Inside my change's scope I must not repeat a gap it names and should close what I touch; outside that scope I consider the gap and either close it opportunistically or record it, never pass it silently.
A0 carries a correction banner rejecting the pass/fail reading ("The conformance reading" fault), because read that way nearly every system fails and the rule "gives no direction".
A gate checks only the binary part of this: that the change moved the system no further from any limit in force.
All axioms in force bind together and none may be traded against another ("orthogonal in statement and conjunctive in force").

--- answer a-71451489 ---
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward. It is not a pass-or-fail test (A0, "Standing commitment, not situated move"; the CORRECTION banner retracts the invariant reading).
It binds each change in two directions: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
A system that is short of the limit is not wrong for that. It is wrong only if it moves away from the limit. So the axiom gives the change a direction, rather than a verdict on the whole system.
Inside the change's scope, the axiom requires you not to repeat any gap you touch and to close what you can. Outside the scope, improvement is opportunistic or recorded, never silently skipped.
A gate checks only the direction rule: that the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched" (A0).
A0 names the misreading as "The conformance reading", under which "no change advances the system, and the gaps the axioms name become permanent."
