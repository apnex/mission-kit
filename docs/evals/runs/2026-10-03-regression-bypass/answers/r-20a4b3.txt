### component.C1
Y: your change touches X and leaves Y's contract unchanged, so Y is outside its mandated scope ("A change touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", axioms/README.md). But Y is adjacent, because X consumes Y's declared interface, and the closer kind: "the one the other stands on is the closer for compounding learning" (entities/E6-component.md). So improving Y is "considered, never mandated": it "may be closed opportunistically" when A14's tangent discipline holds - payback is near, the yield is captured, and the cost "does not significantly distract" (axioms/README.md; axioms/A14-compounding-learning.md "Friction -> root cause, never workaround"). A five-minute fix to friction that cost twenty minutes passes that test, and it must be made "as a change of its own" that answers to every system it alters (axioms/README.md). NOT SETTLED: whether Y's error text is part of its declared interface. GUESS: if it is, X and Y's other consumers have their contracts altered too. Z: X does not use Z, so Z is not adjacent, and two days would "displace the goal", so Z is "recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5) rather than closed", leaving the choice of next work to the director (axioms/README.md; methodology/M5-anti-amnesia-deferral.md). The gap Z holds is A1's "Hidden State Problem" (axioms/A1-sovereign-state-transparency.md), but A1 binds only if the service is stateful (traits/T1-stateful.md), and whether in-process state counts is NOT SETTLED.

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it", with "one duty" at that altitude, and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". A system is defined in entities/E5-system.md as "a unit of engineering with a boundary", which traits describe and axioms bind. They are not different kinds of thing: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system". The difference is which question you ask. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6 Discriminators). A system that nothing contains, such as a whole product seen from outside, "is not a component of anything" (E6). A component does not have to be registered in components/, and it is not defined as a file, module, package, repo or SysML part (E6 Boundaries).

### component.C3
No. entities/E6-component.md says: "A component's directory addresses it... Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". There is no declared interface, so "nothing separates it yet". parts/thing and parts/other are therefore "one system, with the traits of both" for deciding what a change answers to, and a change to thing's internals reaches other (E5 Boundaries; axioms/README.md; E6 blast-radius row). The undeclared dependency "is itself a fault against A3", which requires that "no unit reaches into another's internals" (E5; axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md Air-Gap Principle). The remedy is "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it" (E5). A3's "Bypass as Signal" applies to a unit reaching past a *declared* interface, so here it applies only by analogy (NOT SETTLED). GUESS: the evaluation it asks for still fits - check what other needs against thing's duty when you design the interface.

### component.C4
Your change modifies P, so it answers to P and to "every system containing it" - the service. It also answers to "any system whose contract with it the change alters", and here there is none (axioms/README.md "An axiom binds a system..."). Q composes with P through an unchanged declared contract, so Q "is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md; entities/E5-system.md). One condition applies: Q must actually honour the interface. If Q calls P's internals or relies on undeclared behaviour (a "contract in fact"), the two are one system and the change may reach Q (E5 "Declared against honoured"). Q is adjacent, because it consumes what P exposes, so it is a candidate for opportunistic improvement under A14's tangent test (entities/E6-component.md; axioms/README.md "one it stands on, or one that stands on it"). But Q stands on P, not the reverse, so this is "adjacency with less payback, not none" (E6), and any improvement is optional, made as its own change, and recorded either way. NOT SETTLED: does the service, which contains Q, carry Q's traits, so that axioms Q brings in bind your change through the service?

### OBSERVATIONS
- Neither axioms/README.md nor E6 says whether an error message is part of a declared interface. That decides whether a five-minute "improve Y" change alters Y's consumers (C1).
- There is a tension between "Q is not altered, its axioms do not bind" and "a change answers to every system containing it". E5/A0 do not say whether a containing system's traits are the union of its contained modules' traits. If they are, Q's axioms reach the change indirectly through the service (C4).
- A3 "Bypass as Signal" and E6 "Boundary against directory" are both worded for reaching *past a declared* interface. The no-interface case (C3) is covered only by E5's "undeclared boundary" rule, so the A3 interface evaluation is not clearly triggered there.
- T1/A1: the corpus does not say whether in-process memory that lives across operations, but not across restarts, counts as "persists beyond a single operation". A1 itself treats restart-durability as part of the limit. So it is unclear whether a service like Z is stateful.
- A0 says the gap is recorded "either way", but for an adjacent gap that you choose not to close it gives no explicit record form. Only the non-adjacent/failed-test case names M5.
- INDEX.md tells readers to resolve links from a raw GitHub URL if they have no checkout. In a sandboxed copy, that points outside the corpus.
