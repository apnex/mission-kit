### component.C1
Y is outside your change's scope: A0 (axioms/README.md) says a change "touches what it modifies, what it newly depends on, and any consumer whose contract it alters", and X already depended on Y, so nothing in Y must be improved. But Y is adjacent, because X consumes Y's declared interface (E6: "adjacent ... when the two share a declared interface"), and Y is the one X stands on, which E6 calls "the closer for compounding learning". So A0 says Y's gap "may be closed opportunistically" under A14's tangent discipline, provided the payback is near, the yield is captured, and the cost "does not significantly distract". A five-minute fix to friction that cost twenty minutes appears to pass that test, and it must be made "as a change of its own", not folded into the X fix you have not landed yet. Z fails the test: X does not use it, so it is not adjacent, and two days of work would push aside the goal. A0 therefore says Z's gap "is recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5) rather than closed", because what work happens next is the director's choice (R4, AR3). For both Y and Z, "Either way the gap is recorded. Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids" (A0); Z's hidden in-memory state matches A1's "Hidden State Problem" if the service is stateful. NOT SETTLED: whether Y's error text is part of its declared interface; GUESS: if it is, changing it alters Y's consumers and answers to their axioms too (A0: "if it changes a component's interface, its consumers are altered too").

### component.C2
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it", with "one duty" at the altitude where it appears as a part, and it "exposes and consumes interfaces". System is defined in entities/E5-system.md: "a unit of engineering with a boundary: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it". They are not different kinds of thing: "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing" (E6). What separates them is the question you are asking. "Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component" (E6, Discriminators). A system that nothing contains is not a component of anything (E6). E6 also says a component is not a file, module, package, repository, deployable or SysML part by definition, and it does not have to be registered in components/.

### component.C3
No. E6 says "A component's directory addresses it ... Its boundary is its declared interfaces", and E5 says a directory "is the boundary of neither the system nor the component". There is a fault: parts/other/ depends on parts/thing/'s internals with no declared interface. E5 calls "the undeclared dependency ... itself a fault against A3", which breaks A3's Air-Gap Principle ("no unit reaches into another's internals"). As a result the two are "one system, with the traits of both" for deciding what a change answers to (E5 Boundaries; A0), so a change to either answers to the axioms that either unit brings. E6 says such a part "is still a component, but nothing separates it yet". The two are not "adjacent" either, because adjacency needs a shared declared interface (E6). The remedy is "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it" (E5); GUESS: A3's Bypass as Signal (review the interface against the component's duty) applies in the same way, although its text is about reaching past a declared interface, and there is none here.

### component.C4
Your change answers to P and to every system that contains P, which here includes the service (A0: "the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters"). Q is not altered and its axioms do not bind the change, because "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered" (A0). E6's blast-radius row agrees: a change reaches "an adjacent component only if the interface between them changes". This holds only if Q honours the interface. E5 says the separation "is per pair", and if Q bypasses the interface, or depends on behaviour that is not declared ("a contract in fact"), Q and P are one system. Q is a candidate for opportunistic improvement: it consumes P's declared interface, so it is adjacent, and A0 includes "one that stands on it". E6 says that direction is "adjacency with less payback, not none", so it is still subject to A14 tangent discipline, done as a separate change, and recorded if it is not done. NOT SETTLED: whether the service, as the containing system, takes on traits from Q (for example if Q is stateful), which would bring those axioms in through the service.

### OBSERVATIONS
- A0 defines "touched" as including "what it newly depends on", but it does not say how to treat a dependency that already existed (X->Y in C1). I read that as not touched; the text does not rule on it explicitly.
- A3's Bypass as Signal is worded only for "reaching past a declared interface". The case with no interface at all (C3) is covered by E5's "undeclared dependency is itself a fault against A3", but the A3 mechanic that should trigger, and whether an interface review is owed, are not stated.
- No entry says whether a containing system's traits (and so its axioms) include the traits of its contained parts. E5 says "the traits of both" only for collapsed undeclared pairs, and traits/ has no text on containment. This affects which axioms a change to P answers to through the service.
- No entry says whether error messages, or other output that is not formally specified, belong to a declared interface. E5's "parsing output the interface does not promise" hints that it matters but does not settle it.
- A10 (friction -> root cause) applies only to multi-agent/autonomous systems, so it may not bind an ordinary service. A14 (any-system) carries the friction-mining obligation instead.
