### system-boundary.S1
Only the launcher's axioms: A3, A4, A8, A9, A14, the `any-system` floor (axioms/README.md applicability matrix).
The launcher has no traits: it holds no state (T1), runs one-shot rather than reconciling (T2 boundary: "a script run once"), has one operator (T3), is watched by its operator (T4 test), and has no model (T5). E5 says "A one-shot script with no traits is a system, bound by the axioms every system carries."
The service is not bound. A0 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind", and E5 gives this exact case: "A launcher that hands sessions to a service through a declared interface composes with it."
No containing system is declared, so no containing system's axioms are added (A0: "every system containing it").

### system-boundary.S2
The union of both systems' axioms. A0 says a change answers to "the system it modifies, every system containing it, and any system whose contract with it the change alters", and this change alters the launcher-service contract.
Launcher: A3, A4, A8, A9, A14. Service: stateful (T1) brings A1; multi-agent (T3) brings A5, A6, A7, A10, A13; autonomous (T4, no human in the synchronous loop) brings A7, A10, A13; plus the floor.
Total: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14. No model is in the loop, so A11 and A12 do not bind.
A2: NOT SETTLED. The prompt does not say whether the service is declarative (T2). If it is, A2 binds too.
NOT SETTLED: whether the interface is versioned. If it is, E5 says "Publishing a new version of an interface alters no consumer that has not adopted it". In that case the service's axioms bind when the service adopts the new version, as a change to the service, not at publication.

### system-boundary.S3
The service's full set: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14, plus A2 only if the service is declarative (NOT SETTLED, as in S2).
E5: "A module inside a service is a system, and the service contains it, whether or not the module's interfaces are declared". Also: "changing a module changes the service it sits in."
A0 says a change answers to "every system containing it". So the module being stateless on its own does not limit what the change answers to.
E5 "Why precision matters" names the alternative as the error: "a stateful system escapes its own axioms by being described as stateless modules, each of which passes on its own."

### system-boundary.S4
Only the library's own axioms: A3, A4, A8, A9, A14 (the floor; A0 says "a plain library takes `any-system`").
The ten consumers compose with the library through a declared, versioned contract that the change leaves unchanged, so they are not altered and their axioms do not bind (A0; E5 "Contains against composes").
E5: "Publishing a new version of an interface alters no consumer that has not adopted it; each adoption is a change to that consumer, and answers to that consumer's axioms; the component is not altered by being adopted."
Each consumer's later adoption is that consumer's own change, bound by its own axioms (for example A1, A5, A6, A7, A10, A13 for the stateful multi-agent ones). Those axioms do not bind this change.
E5 rejects the opposite reading: "a small shared library would carry the axioms of every system that uses it, and the most-reused code would carry the most obligations."

### system-boundary.S5
The union of both systems' axioms, the same set as S2: A1, A3, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A13, A14, plus A2 only if the service is declarative (NOT SETTLED).
The service depends on the launcher's output and no boundary is declared. A0 says such units "are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". E5 adds: "Behaviour another unit depends on without any declaration is a contract in fact ... and a change to it reaches across."
Something else is wrong. A0 says "the undeclared dependency is itself a fault under A3", and A3's Air-Gap Principle says "units interact only through declared adapters and contracts".
There is also a practical risk: changing the log format may break the service's parsing.
A0 says work that touches a gap "closes as much of it as the touch allows". E5 gives the fix: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again."
GUESS: this change touches the contract-in-fact, so the gap is inside its scope. If the gap is not closed, it must at least be recorded with a revival trigger (M5), because A0 says "Passing it silently is what the axiom forbids."

### system-boundary.S6
It is defined in entities/E5-system.md: "A system is a unit of engineering with a boundary: what is inside it, and the contracts through which anything outside reaches it."
A boundary separates two units "only where it is declared and honoured - written down, and the only way the neighbour reaches it". Where it is not, "the two are one system for deciding what a change answers to."
Traits describe a system and axioms bind it. Systems relate in exactly two ways, contain and compose.
E5 says a system is "Not a repository, a deployment unit or a process by definition" and "Not defined by size or by traits."
E6 defines a component as "a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it."

### axiom-direction.Q1
The service is stateful and multi-agent, so A1 and A5 bind every change to it. The component's private in-memory state is the gap A1 names: "Hidden State Problem - state lives inside a process; other actors reason about a different reality" (A1 Faults; see also its Mandate, "No functional unit possesses private, opaque, or transient truth").
For your own feature, A0 says "new work never repeats a gap the axiom names". So any state your feature adds must be durable and visible to every authorized agent, not held only in process memory.
If your feature modifies the component, newly depends on it, or changes its contract, the component is inside your scope. Then you must close as much of the gap as that touch allows, and A0 says a component's gaps "must not be built on".
If your feature does not touch it, fixing it is "considered, never mandated" (A0). You may fix it as a separate change only if it is adjacent (E6: it shares a declared interface with what you touch) and passes the A14 tangent test: near payback, yield captured, no significant distraction from your goal.
Otherwise you record it with evidence and a revival trigger (M5) and leave the decision to the director (R4, AR3). Either way, A0 says "the gap is recorded"; you must not pass it silently.

### axiom-direction.Q2
An axiom names a limit the system is built toward, not a test that a change passes or fails. A0 calls it a "load-bearing asymptote", and says a system "is not wrong for being short of it; it is wrong for moving away from it."
When you change a system the axiom binds, the axiom constrains your change in two directions at once. A0: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
So the axiom sets the direction of the change: do not regress, and close what you touch. Gaps beyond your scope you consider, and you record any you do not close.
The axioms in force are conjunctive and cannot be traded against each other. A0: a change that improves on thirteen limits and moves away from the fourteenth "is a regression with thirteen consolations."
A0 names the pass/fail reading as a fault ("The conformance reading"), and a correction banner in A0 records that the director ruled for the asymptote reading over it.

### OBSERVATIONS
- The prompt does not say whether the service is declarative (T2), so whether A2 binds in S2, S3 and S5 is NOT SETTLED.
- S2 does not say whether the launcher-service interface is versioned. Under E5 that decides whether the service is altered when the change is made or only when the service adopts it.
- A0 says a component the change newly depends on is "touched but not altered ... its axioms do not bind the change". In Q1 the component is inside the service, so the service's axioms bind through containment anyway. A reader could misread the "do not bind" sentence as applying here.
- E5 and E6 call a separately published shared library a "component" (S4) while treating it as composed with its consumers, not contained by them. E6 defines a component as a part of a containing system. This is a mild terminology tension.
