PROBE component.C2

Question:
In this corpus, what is a 'component', and how does it differ from a 'system'? Cite where it is defined, or say that it is not.

Key:
E6: a system seen as a part of a containing system, with one duty at its altitude and interfaces of its own; every component is a system - 'component' names its place in a larger whole. Registration in components/ is not part of the definition.

Rubric:
2 for part-of-a-containing-system, one duty at an altitude, interfaces, and the relation to system, with a correct citation. 1 for part. 0 for a fabricated citation or 'not defined'.

--- answer a-f2945be7 ---
It is defined in entities/E6-component.md: "A component is a system seen as a part of a larger system that contains it." It "has one duty at the altitude where it appears as a part, and it exposes and consumes interfaces", and its boundary is those declared interfaces. E6 says "Every component is a system; component names its place in a containing system, not a different kind of thing". So the difference is which question you are asking, not what kind of thing it is. Traits, axioms and what a change answers to are questions about a system. Duty, interfaces, neighbours and blast radius are questions about a component, and they only have answers relative to the containing whole (E6 "Component against system"; E5 "A system against a component"). A system that nothing contains is not a component of anything (E6). A component is not a file, module, package, repo or deployable by definition, not a SysML part, and not necessarily registered in components/ (E6 Boundaries).
