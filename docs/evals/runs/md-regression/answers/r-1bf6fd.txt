### explain-routing.Q6
ST1, with M10 as the deciding entry. M10 asks for "one decision at a time" and "Ask for one decision per message". Step 3 says that if a decision depends on understanding, you establish that understanding first and confirm it has landed. ### OBSERVATIONS
- ST1 says K5 ("three questions a round") and AR3 ("many moves at once") conflict with M10's one question or decision at a time. It leaves both tensions unresolved, so an agent that reaches K5 or AR3 for Q5 or Q6 gets the opposite instruction (ST1's "Opposite instructions" fault).
- S15 says "End with two or three picks". It also says that when a decision is asked, "each option is a Continue pick", while Explore and Return keep their meanings. With three options (Q5), that gives at least four picks, so the two rules conflict.
- M10 derives "nothing extra reaches them" from A12. But A12 is scoped `applies-to: [llm-in-the-loop]` and is about LLM token context, not human readers, so the derivation is an analogy and not a direct application.
- ST1 records gaps for bad news and teaching ("each tested in one pair"). No entry covers deadlines, progress updates or dependent decisions, so Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 and Q6 are each settled only partly.
- W24 says it is "a director-mode ceremony, not an agent-executable task" with `roleEligibility: [director]`, yet it cites M10, an agent-facing procedure, as the method that conducts it. It is ambiguous who runs M10 in a walkthrough.
- R4 repeats the bullet "A13 (Director Intent Amplification)" twice in its Axiom alignment section.
- S15 and M10 cite evidence at `docs/evals/human/...`. I did not check whether those paths exist, because they are outside the files I was told to use.
- The corpus INDEX.md says to fetch from GitHub when no local checkout exists. I used only the local corpus, as instructed.
