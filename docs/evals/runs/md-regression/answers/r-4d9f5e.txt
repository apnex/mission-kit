### work-layers.L1
This belongs in the rules layer (`rules/`, prefix RU). M0's placement test says "Guidance that only *requires* a record to carry something - a field, a reason - produces nothing itself, and is a rule", and it uses this exact case as its example: "*every deferral records its revival trigger* is visible in text and is still a rule, because it governs what the work records" (methods/README.md). An entry already covers it: RU3 (rules/RU3-anti-amnesia-deferral.md), "When you defer, park, or cut a unit of work, record - at the moment of deferral - a concrete **revival trigger**". RU3 makes the trigger "a required field, not an optional note", and its trace is "every deferred, parked or cut item's record carries a revival trigger". The document that holds such rows is the backlog artifact AR5 (artifacts/AR5-backlog.md), which rejects any non-closed row whose trigger "is a date or the word 'later' rather than an observable condition". The old ID M5 (methods/M5-anti-amnesia-deferral.md) is superseded and only points to RU3. So the team should cite RU3 and keep an AR5-shaped backlog, not add a new entry, because M0 says "Prefer strengthening an existing entry to adding a neighbour".

### work-layers.L2
This belongs in the skills layer (`skills/`, prefix K). K0 says a skill "names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output, and it is expected to be executed largely as written", and that it "earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary"; a fixed output layout means templates, which are assets (skills/README.md). It also meets K0's admission test: "executable, repeatable, and would otherwise be reconstructed from scratch by whoever needs it next". M0's boundary table agrees, putting "a capability you execute" in `skills/` because "the object is a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed", and it names filing one in methods as a fault: "The procedure that is really a skill" (methods/README.md). It would be two files, a `K*` stub plus a portable `SKILL.md` body (K0, "A skill is two files, on purpose"). Something close already exists: K3 substrate-audit (skills/K3-substrate-audit.md) is "structured around a fixed artefact layout and executed as a **five-pass meta-process**", with its body and seven templates in skills/substrate-audit/, but its scope is "an OSS substrate ... for adoption or comparison", so check K3 before adding a neighbour. One caveat: M0's five-question placement ladder has no question for skills, and its question 2 ("produce a result of its own") would send this to methods/, so the skills placement rests on K0's wording (see L6).

### work-layers.L3
This belongs in the work-types layer (`work-types/`, prefix W), because a work-type is the entry that carries `roleEligibility`, `domainEligibility`, an `evidenceContract`, an `evidenceAuthority` and a `falsifier`: who may do the work, what it may target, and what proof closes it (work-types/README.md, "A candidate is taxonomy-grade iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node"). W10, adversarial-design-review-upstream, already covers it: "A verifier red-teams a design **before** build or merge" (work-types/W10-adversarial-design-review-upstream.md). Who may do it: `roleEligibility: [verifier]`, and `evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation` requires an attester who "cannot be the executor" (W0 constraint 2). What it may target: all seven domains with `domainFreedom: free`, and a parameter `target`, "the design under review", bound `provided-by-trigger`. What ends it: the evidence is a "critique doc before build/merge with specific failure hypotheses", and the falsifier is "review after the decision, or no falsifiable concerns raised". W10 cites M1 as the method that conducts it (`methods: [M1]`), so the steps of the review live in methods/ and not in the work-type. Extend W10 rather than add a second entry: W0 lists "Two work-types for one act" as a fault and says a new work-type "is ratified by the director".

### work-layers.L4
It is a methodology entry (`methods/`), because following it produces a verdict, and M0's placement question 2 reads: "Does following it produce a result of its own - a verdict, an artifact, a record? A procedure, here" (methods/README.md). It is not a skill, because nothing is packaged as scripts or assets (K0), and it is not a work-type, because it does not compile to a claimable node with roles and an evidence contract (W0), although work-types cite it. M1, Triangulated review (methods/M1-triangulated-review.md), already covers it: "require **at least four genuinely independent inputs** before reaching a verdict", with the trigger "reviewing a patch or design that ships to production or upstream". M0 calls M1 the set's primitive: "independent inputs before a verdict - is the independence the verifying procedures specialise". W8 and W10 cite it (`methods: [M1]`), and ST0 lists "independent verification ... `M1`, `W8`, `K21`, `R3`, `A8`" as a concern that no set gathers yet (sets/README.md). Note that M1 asks for independent *inputs*. The demand for a reviewer other than the author is a separate rule, in W0 constraints 2, 8 and 9 ("a same-agent review never stands in").

### work-layers.L5
NOT SETTLED which layer, because the corpus places guidance by its form, and here the form has not been chosen: a procedure goes in `methods/`, a rule that leaves a trace goes in `rules/`, and a practice that leaves no trace goes in `practices/` (M0 placement). All three charters record the absence as a "Handing over" gap. M0 says "handing work in progress to another agent or a later session has no procedure at all" and that "a lone agent hands over to its own next session" (methods/README.md). RU0 says "*Handing over:* a handover record names what is unfinished - a rule that would leave a trace, and none exists" (rules/README.md), and PC0's row "how to leave work so the next reader can pick it up" is "none - gap" with "no checked candidate yet" (practices/README.md). The closest standing commitment is axiom A4 (any-system): "A cold pickup from the record alone reproduces the same outputs as a warm actor who never lost context - zero decision context is lost at handover". On the traits, T1 says notes kept for a next session make an agent stateful, and T3 says such a handover is "one actor across time, not two". M0 says such a gap is "not a defect until something fails there", and PC0 forbids minting a practice "to fill an empty moment" without observed evidence; GUESS: RU0's own candidate, a handover record that names what is unfinished, would be the first entry, in rules/.

### work-layers.L6
The methods test is M0's placement question: "Does following it produce a result of its own - a verdict, an artifact, a record? A procedure, here" (methods/README.md). A skill differs by packaging and invocation: "A method is a procedure held as guidance, the single source of its content; a skill packages a procedure so an agent can install and invoke it, and earns its packaging where scripts or assets make it necessary" (skills/README.md), which M0's table repeats as "invoked rather than followed". A work-type differs in kind: "A candidate is taxonomy-grade iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node" with roles, domains, an evidence contract and a falsifier, and "The work-type cites the procedure that conducts it, in `methods`" (work-types/README.md). So a work-type is easy to tell from the other two, because it is a unit of work and not a procedure; for example, W10 cites M1. Method against skill is the hard pair: both are procedures that produce a result, M0's five-question ladder has no skill question (so a skill run through it stops at question 2, methods), and the line rests on soft words, "invoked" against "followed" and "where ... necessary". The corpus records that this confusion has already happened: M0 lists the fault "The procedure that is really a skill", K0 says "A skill that only describes is a method ... as `M9` was before it moved", and K1 is superseded by M9.

### work-layers.L7
Rules: RU1 (rules/) applies to "any improvement sweep, refactor program, or audit cycle". It makes reject the default, sets the bar to land as "delivers more value than it costs, measured concretely against the current state", and requires the report to lead with yield ("how many candidates considered, how many landed, ... what was verified vs. asserted") before any narrative, which is also its trace. RU3 (rules/) applies to each of the three cuts: record a revival trigger "at the moment of deferral" as a required field, and when an item revives, re-triage it rather than resume it; RU2 (rules/) forbids later rewriting of design records, and A0 (axioms/) says a gap outside the change's scope is recorded with a revival trigger because "larger choices of what work happens next are the director's (R4, AR3)". Artifacts: the cuts become rows in an AR5 backlog (artifacts/; the Retired columns are "reason cut | revives on"), each with evidence, never deleted, and closed "in the same commit as the work that closed it"; if there is an AR3 board, it must not drop a deferred item "without its reason being written back as a revival trigger". Work-types: each of the two landings is a W6 merge-and-land, which needs a pre-approved PR because "the independent review gate is upstream of the merge"; that gate is W8, which cites the method M1 (at least four independent inputs for work that ships to production or upstream), under W0 constraints 2, 8 and 9 (no same-agent review), and W9 applies if the sweep is an audit. The report to the director: style S15 (point first, decision frame, picks, explain IDs), plus method M10 if you ask for a decision (one decision per message, every option with its cost including doing nothing, reasoning recorded in AR4 through W23), both gathered in set ST1; axiom A13 applies only if the system is multi-agent or autonomous (A0 tags), and role R4 says ratification "cannot be satisfied by architect narrative". The standing context, AGENTS.md (no ID), also applies: say whether each claim was measured or inferred, "A caveat does not repair a headline", "Check the splits that favour you", one concern per commit, and no AI attribution. Gaps: M0 says "Deciding what to keep" holds no procedure, and ST1 lists RU1 as a tension not yet reconciled ("RU1 honest yield is triggered by sweeps and audits, not by any report"); GUESS: the two fit together here, because the yield line can serve as S15's point-first line.
