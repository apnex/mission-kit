### w0.Q3
D0, "Bimodal freedom": **free** means object-level build/ship/assurance work "*acts on* a surface, so the domain is a free, discriminating choice" (D1, D2, D3, D7 are reached this way).
**pinned** means "meta/substrate work whose *type names its own surface*: the domain is a constant function of the work-type (N=1), so the generator does not vary it", and pinned work lands on D4, D5 or D6.
W0 constraint 3 says the same: "`domainFreedom: pinned` means the generator does not vary the domain", and the schema says `domainEligibility` is "a single value when pinned".
`domainFreedom` is declared on the work-type, not on the domain (D0: "`domainFreedom` therefore lives on the **work-type**, not the domain"). Each domain entry shows a generated view of it.
For example, W22 and W23 are both `domainFreedom: pinned` to `authority-governance`.
W0 says honestly that the "aggregate > sum" claim holds fully only for the free third and is "`pinned` for the meta third".

### w0.Q4
No, and not even when it is marked as weaker.
W0 constraint 2: if the live roster puts attester and executor in one agent, the seed fails, "and the gate waits for an independent agent or is met by director ratification (constraint 9); a same-agent review never stands in."
Constraint 9: in a thin roster the check may degrade "ONLY" to defer-until-independent-seat or director-ratification, never to the architect when the architect is (or could be) the executor, and "Same-agent review is never a valid degradation."
roles/README.md (R0) agrees: "the independence a gate requires never falls to the same agent", and even a fresh instance of the same agent "is still a self-check ... so it cannot provide independent assurance".
R0 also says the agent's own check is still mandatory ("The agent's own check happens regardless; only its standing as assurance changes"). It produces the author's own evidence, but it does not seal the gate.
So the options are to wait for a second agent or to get director ratification (and "the director does not become a verifier").

### w0.Q5
W0, "The order a cold agent assembles one unit of work in":
1. The systems the change alters give the traits, and so the axioms in force (E5, T0). The organisation doing the work is also a system, and its axioms govern how the work is done and recorded.
2. The work-type gives the eligible roles and domains, and its `evidenceAuthority` says who may satisfy the evidence.
3. The domain is the surface the work's evidence lands on (D0).
4. The role is who performs the work, and what it may attest is "set by role and work-type together" (R0).
5. The method and artifact are the ones the work-type cites, in its `methods` and `produces` fields.
W0 adds that these attach "by citation or by system, never by widening the triple".

### w0.Q7
W0, Territory: "A recurring kind of work that compiles to a closeable claimable node and fits no work-type is closed by adding one, not by stretching a neighbour to cover it", because a stretched work-type carries two evidence contracts under one name.
Until the new work-type exists, the work is "recorded against the gap, as a row in the project's backlog (AR5)".
If the work cannot compile to a node (no trigger, nothing to close), it gets a routing note instead.
A new work-type is authored from a peer exemplar (PC1), against the W0 entry schema, and lands through the gate. Because it "changes how work is generated, so it is ratified by the director".
Faults lists the unhandled case as "The uncovered work ... hand-routed every time".
The test for qualifying is that a candidate "is taxonomy-grade **iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node**".

### w0.Q8
W0, "Faults" (population faults, "visible across the set, invisible to any one work-type"):
- Two work-types for one act: the same node can be minted under two names.
- The restated constraint: an entry copies the canonical set instead of satisfying it, and the copy drifts.
- The doubly-declared pairing: a work-type/domain pairing is stated on both sides and the two can disagree. Only the work-type's list governs.
- The uncovered work: a recurring kind of work with no work-type, so it is hand-routed every time.
- The uncited procedure: a procedure that conducts work but that no work-type cites, so a coordinating system cannot attach it.
GUESS: a stretched work-type that carries two evidence contracts (Territory) would also count, though Faults does not list it.

### OBSERVATIONS
- W22 frontmatter says `evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation`, while its own compositionHooks say "plain kind:review, not verifier-attestation per README constraint 2", and W0 constraint 2 says verifier-held gates "never" use verifier-attestation. Its body says "`evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation`, held by the verifier" and then that it "seeds as a plain `kind:review` node". This looks internally inconsistent, or at least ambiguous about authority versus evidence kind.
- W22's title and its INDEX trigger say "per-item" / "a single item", but its Definition covers "a design or a set of items". The trigger may not route set-level checks.
- methods/README.md says M7 is "cited by primitives and ... cited by none", yet W22 cites M7 in `methods`. This is probably meant as "cited by no other method", but it is ambiguous.
- W0's entry schema requires a body section `## Origin`. W22 and W23, the two entries I read, have none.
- W0's ordering puts director ratification under constraint 9 (roles README cites 9), while W23 cites "constraint 8" as "the director-ratification path". Both mention it, so this is not a contradiction, but the citations differ.
- W0 cites "M7 guardrail #2" for the no-fork rule, but M7 as written contains no numbered guardrail list.
- AR4 has no back-reference to W23, so the produces link can only be found from the work-type side.
