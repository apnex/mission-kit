PROBE axiom-direction.Q2

Question:
In your own words, and citing the corpus: when you change a system an axiom binds, what is the axiom for?

Key:
A limit (asymptote) the system is built toward: no change may move the system away from it, and each change moves toward it as far as its scope allows.

Rubric:
2 for a direction or limit to move toward with each change. 1 for both readings, or improvement as secondary. 0 for only a constraint not to break.

--- answer a-0d4e488f ---
An axiom is a limit the system is steered toward, not a pass-or-fail test. A0 calls it "a load-bearing asymptote - a limit a system is built toward to stay aligned with its architecture's intent". When you change a system the axiom binds, it pulls the change in two directions at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows". A system falling short of the limit is not wrong. What is wrong is a change that moves away from the limit, or that touches a gap and passes it silently. A0 names the opposite reading as a fault, "The conformance reading". Under that reading a system short of the limit is either already failed, so nothing is owed, or passing, so nothing moves, and the gaps become permanent. The axioms are also conjunctive, so none may be traded against another (A0, "How the set composes"). A gate checks only the direction rule: that the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched".

--- answer a-def53646 ---
An axiom is a limit, an "asymptote", that the system is built toward. It is not a pass/fail test (axioms/README.md, "Standing commitment"; the CORRECTION banner there retracts the invariant reading).
When you change a system it binds, it pulls in two directions at once: "No change may move the system away from the limit, and each change moves it toward the limit as far as the change's own scope allows."
So it rules out regression, and it obliges you to improve whatever your change touches. Beyond that scope, improvement is encouraged and any gap you see is recorded rather than passed silently.
It is not something to trade against deadlines or against other axioms ("no axiom may be traded against another"). A gate can check whether the change "moved the system no further from any limit in force, and repeated no gap it touched".
The A0 Faults section explains why: under "the conformance reading", the gaps the axioms name become permanent.
