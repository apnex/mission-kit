Question:

Key:
Declare an interface for what N reaches and move N onto it - inside the change's scope, because the change alters what N reaches; it is not merely recorded and not optional. Had the change not touched what N reaches, the gap would be recorded.

Rubric:
2 for declaring and honouring the interface as in scope, with the reason that the change alters what N reaches. 1 for declaring it as optional or recommended, or recording it only. 0 otherwise.

So the change must declare an interface for T and get N using it ("honoured"). E5 Discriminators ("Declared against honoured") and A3 "Bypass as Signal" say how: first evaluate the interface against T's duty, then either move N onto the interface or adjust the interface "to what the consumer legitimately needs". Until that happens the change also answers to N's axioms, because N is a consumer whose contract (in fact) it alters (A0: a change touches "any consumer whose contract it alters"). The undeclared reach is itself an A3 fault (E5; A3 "Air-Gap Principle"). NOT SETTLED: whether the interface work must land in the same commit/PR as the T change or in a separate change. GUESS: within the same unit of work, since E5 says it is "inside its scope".

Question:
While adding a small feature to component K, you notice K's internals tangle two concerns, and splitting them would give K's three consumers a cleaner interface. K's interface is not versioned. The split is within K's own duty. What do the axioms ask?

Key:
The new feature arrives as its own unit, composed with K, not bolted onto K. The split is encouraged as opportunistic improvement within K's duty - not mandated - including re-cutting K's seams and recomposing for its consumers; it is made as a change of its own, and since it changes K's interface, it answers to each consumer it alters.

Rubric:
2 for the feature as a new unit, the split encouraged not mandated, and the consumers' axioms binding the interface change. 1 for two of the three. 0 for 'never modify K' or 'the split is required'.

--- answer a-2a75f3ae ---
First, the direction rule (A0): your feature must not make the tangle worse. "New work never repeats a gap the axiom names", and A3's Law of One forbids dual-purpose modules. Whether you must split K depends on whether your change "touches" the tangle. A0 says "code that merely sits beside it, in the same file or module, is not touched". If the feature modifies the tangled code, you must close "as much of it as the touch allows". If not, the split is outside your scope, so it is "encouraged, never mandated", and you are "expected to weigh doing it, not merely permitted to". A3 "Composable by Default" endorses this exact move ("fracture it, re-cut its seams and recompose it ... within the unit's own duty"), "as a change of its own". Because K's interface is not versioned, that separate change alters all three consumers and answers to each one's axioms (A0; E5 versioned-contract discriminator). Decide by A14's tangent discipline (near payback, yield captured, little distraction from the goal). If you do not split, record the gap with evidence and a revival trigger (RU3), because "Either way the gap is recorded."
