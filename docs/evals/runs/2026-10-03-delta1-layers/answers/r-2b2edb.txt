### work-layers.L1

M0's placement test says to stop at the first yes, and this guidance produces nothing of its own but "leave[s] a trace in the record of the work that a check could test" (methods/README.md, Placement Q4). M0 uses this very case as its worked example: "*every deferral records its revival trigger* is visible in text and is still a rule, because it governs what the work records" (methods/README.md). ### work-layers.L2
It belongs in the skills layer (`skills/`, prefix K). K0 says a skill "is *invoked*, not followed: it names a procedure with inputs, steps and an output, and it is expected to be executed largely as written". It earns an entry "when it is executable, repeatable, and would otherwise be reconstructed from scratch by whoever needs it next" (skills/README.md). M0's own table says the same thing: "a capability you execute -> `skills/` - the object is a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed". M0 also names misfiling it as a methods fault: "The procedure that is really a skill" (methods/README.md). Something close to this already exists. K3 substrate-audit runs a "five-pass meta-process" (Sweep, Triangulation, Promotion audit, Synthesis, Reconciliation) over a pinned upstream repository and writes to "a fixed artefact layout" of 00-summary, 02-architecture, 03-mapping, 04-assessment, 05-coverage, features/ and sources.md (skills/K3-substrate-audit.md). So the team should first check whether K3 already does the job, or could be extended to do it. GUESS: the corpus's "prefer strengthening an existing entry" advice is written for methods (methods/README.md), but it plausibly applies to skills too.

### work-layers.L3
It belongs in the work-types layer (`work-types/`, prefix W). W0 says each work-type declares `roleEligibility` (who may do it), `domainEligibility`/`parameters` (what it may be pointed at), and `evidenceContract`/`evidenceAuthority`/`falsifier` (what proof closes it). W0 also says "a candidate is taxonomy-grade iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node" (work-types/README.md). It is already covered by W10 adversarial-design-review-upstream: "A verifier red-teams a design **before** build or merge, producing a critique doc that raises specific, falsifiable failure hypotheses" (work-types/W10-adversarial-design-review-upstream.md). In W10, who may do it is `roleEligibility: [verifier]`, with `verifier-attestation` from someone who "cannot be the executor" on the live roster. What it may be pointed at is `target`, "the design under review", in any of the seven domains. What closes it is a `kind: review` critique doc, and its falsifier is "review after the decision, or no falsifiable concerns raised". The team should use or amend W10 rather than add a new entry.

### work-layers.L4
It is a methodology entry, and one already exists: M1 "Triangulated review - minimum 4 independent inputs" (methods/M1-triangulated-review.md). M1 requires "at least four genuinely independent inputs before reaching a verdict", none of them "produced by the reviewer in the loop being audited". Its trigger is reviewing "a patch or design that ships to production or upstream". It is a method because M0's placement test asks "Does following it produce a result of its own - a verdict...?" and a review verdict is such a result (methods/README.md). M0 files M1 under the Verifying moment and calls it the primitive the verifying procedures specialise. It is not a skill, because it is not a capability with fixed inputs and outputs that you invoke (skills/README.md). It is not a work-type, because it does not compile to a claimable node with a role, domain and evidence contract (work-types/README.md). GUESS: the verification work-types, such as W8 verify-gate-reactive, are the units of work in which M1 would be applied, but no work-type cites M1 (grep of the corpus).

### work-layers.L5
The corpus names this moment "handing over": "passing work in progress to another agent or a later session". It says that moment "has no procedure at all" and that "a lone agent hands over to its own next session" (methods/README.md, Territory). Where the guidance would go depends on M0's placement test. If following it produces a handover artifact, it goes in `methods/`. If it only constrains what the record must carry, it goes in `rules/`, and RU0 names the missing rule: "*a handover record names what is unfinished* - would leave a trace, and none exists" (rules/README.md). If it is judgement that leaves no trace, it goes in `practices/`, which also says handing over "hold[s] no practice today" (practices/README.md). M0 records the gap on purpose: it is "recorded rather than filled", "Neither is a defect until something fails there, and both are where to look first when something does". PC0 adds that "A practice is not minted to fill a moment". At the axiom level, A4 Zero-Loss Knowledge already sets the target: "A cold pickup from the record alone reproduces the same outputs as a warm actor" (axioms/A4-zero-loss-knowledge.md). NOT SETTLED: which of the three layers a real handover entry would land in, because no entry has been written.

### work-layers.L6
Methods versus skills: M0 says a method is a set of "Named steps that **produce a result of their own** - a verdict, an artifact, a record". It says a skill is "a procedure with inputs and outputs, invoked rather than followed" (methods/README.md). K0 draws the line as "A methodology entry governs how you conduct work you are already doing; a skill is a capability you pick up to do a thing you could not otherwise do" (skills/README.md). A work-type is a different kind of thing: a verb-family that a `role x work-type x domain` triple compiles into "a claimable WorkItem with a complete evidence contract", and it qualifies "iff it compiles to a closeable claimable node" (work-types/README.md). The hard pair is method versus skill. Both are "procedures" that produce output, and M0's ordered five-question placement test (axiom, procedure, style, rule, practice) has no skill step, so a skill read literally stops at Q2 and lands in methods. K0's description of methods is also out of date: M0 now says guidance that "governs how work is done and produces nothing of its own is a rule ... or a practice". The corpus admits the blur in its own history: M9 History content scrub, a step-by-step git filter-repo procedure, was moved from skills (K1) to methods in Delta-1 (skills/K1-history-content-scrub.md). M0 also lists the fault "The procedure that is really a skill". NOT SETTLED: no single test in the corpus separates all three.

### work-layers.L7
RU1 (rules) is the core: "The default disposition for a candidate is *reject*", and you land only what "delivers more value than it costs, measured concretely against the current state". Its trace is that your report "leads with the delivered outcome - candidates considered, landed, and verified against asserted - before any narrative" (rules/RU1-default-reject-honest-yield.md). RU3 (rules) requires each of the three cuts to record an observable revival trigger at the moment you cut it, keeps terminal states reopenable, and says "Revival re-triages, it does not resume". AR5 Backlog (artifacts) is where the cuts live: each row needs an id, finding, cited evidence, state and trigger ("later" is not a trigger), cut rows are retired, never deleted, and a row "is closed in the same commit as the work that closed it". RU2 (rules) freezes any planning or review record of the sweep at authorship: when it later diverges, add a status banner, not a rewrite. For the two landings, M1 (methods) requires four independent inputs before a verdict if they ship to production or upstream. W6 merge-and-land (work-types) lands only "an already-approved PR", with the independent review gate upstream (work-types/README.md constraints 2 and 8). When you report, A13 (axioms) says to surface a decision-shaped digest at gate points, to "never go silently dark", and never to narrow the option set or synthesise the director's decision; R4 (roles) adds that director-ratification "cannot be satisfied by architect narrative", and if the director is to choose among candidates, AR3 Board (artifacts) requires two-dimension scoring, Held items scored, and no deferral "dropped without its reason being written back as a revival trigger". NOT SETTLED: whether A13 binds depends on whether the system has the multi-agent or autonomous traits (`applies-to`), and GUESS: if the sweep is claimed as a unit of work, it is W9 audit-a-surface (work-types).

### OBSERVATIONS
- M0's ordered placement test (axiom, procedure, style, rule, practice) has no skill step. Read literally, it sends any skill to methods at Q2, which contradicts M0's own boundary table and K0.
- K0 (skills/README.md) still defines a methodology entry as one that "governs how you conduct work you are already doing". After Delta-1, M0 assigns exactly that description to rules and practices, so K0's version is stale.
- M1's title says "minimum 4", but its body says "sometimes 3 is enough". M1, M9 and the K3 stub each use a "## Rule" heading even though none of them is in the rules layer.
- RU0 and PC0 say their members "came from `methodology/`", but the directory is `methods/`.
- methods/README.md line 106 is followed directly by `---` with no blank line. In CommonMark that turns the previous paragraph line into a setext heading instead of a horizontal rule.
- MREQ-0 lists the "tactical `S`/`M`/`P`/`K` entries" and leaves out RU and PC.
- W0 constraint 2 says "Verifier-held gates use plain `kind:review`, never verifier-attestation", yet W8, W9 and W10 declare `evidenceAuthority: verifier-attestation`. W8 reads the constraint as "never verifier-attestation-on-its-own-work", so the wording is ambiguous.
