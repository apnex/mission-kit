PROBE boundary-bypass.B4

Question:
A check detects that several components bypass T's declared interface to reach T's internals. What does the corpus ask you to do?

Key:
Treat the bypass as a signal, not only as faults in the consumers: evaluate T's interface against T's duty and vision. Either the consumers are wrong and are rerouted onto the interface, or the interface falls short of T's duty - the consumers have an unsignalled change request - and the interface is adjusted. An adjusted interface alters its consumers, so their axioms bind that change.

Rubric:
2 for the interface evaluation against duty and vision with both outcomes. 1 for only 'fix the consumers' grounded in A3, or only 'adjust the interface'. 0 for no action, or for forking or replacing T. Splitting T because the evaluation finds a second duty counts as adjusting the interface.

--- answer a-a1b8359c ---
Each detected bypass is an A3 fault: the Air-Gap Principle, and success signal 2, "nothing reaches into another unit's internals" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md). The check itself meets A3 success signal 5, "Boundary violations are detectable by review or tooling." Until each bypass is fixed, every bypassing component is one system with T for binding, so a change to T's internals answers to all their axioms (E5, E6 blast radius, A0). Under A0, a change that touches a bypass closes as much of it as the touch allows. Bypasses outside the change's scope may be closed opportunistically only if they meet A14's tangent discipline. Otherwise each is recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5), and the director decides what work comes next (R4, AR3) (axioms/README.md). The gap must not be passed silently. NOT SETTLED: the corpus names no specific work-type or procedure for fixing a set of bypasses found by a check.
