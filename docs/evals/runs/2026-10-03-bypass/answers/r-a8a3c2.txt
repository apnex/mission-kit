### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. Because O reaches past T's declared interface, T and O are one system for deciding what a change answers to, "with the traits of both". E5 Discriminators, "Declared against honoured": "A unit that reaches past it - calling internals ... - stays one system with it". A0, "What brings an axiom into force", agrees: where a boundary is "one written and reached past ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind".
On the bypass itself, A3 "Bypass as Signal" calls it "a fault in the consumer and a signal about the interface". It says "A detected bypass triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision, ending in the consumer being moved onto the interface or the interface being adjusted". It adds: "Until then the two remain one system". A0 also calls the undeclared or bypassed dependency "itself a fault under A3".
NOT SETTLED: whether your change to T's internals must itself carry out that evaluation or fix. GUESS: A0's scope rule ("work that touches a gap closes as much of it as the touch allows"; otherwise record it with a revival trigger per M5) means you must at least record the bypass, and should close it if your touch allows.

### boundary-bypass.B2
O, not P (as separate systems). P uses the declared interface and you leave it unchanged, so P only composes with T. A0 says: "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". O bypasses the interface, so it is one system with T, and its axioms bind. E5 says: "The separation is per pair: a neighbour that uses the interface composes with the unit, while one that bypasses it does not."
The change also answers to the service, because T sits inside it: A0 says "every system containing it", and E5 "Contain" says the same. P's axioms do not bind as P's own.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No. E5 says: "declaring an interface and leaving the bypass in place separates nothing". It also says the boundary separates "only where it is declared and honoured". E5 "Boundaries" says separation needs both steps: "Declaring the boundary, and moving the dependency onto it, is what makes them two again". E6 says the same: "Declaring its interfaces, and its neighbours using them, is what separates it". T and O therefore stay one system until O's calls go through the new interface. (Separately, both are still contained by the service.)

### boundary-bypass.B4
A3 "Bypass as Signal" says each detected bypass is a fault in that consumer and a signal that the interface "may ... fall short of the component's duty". It "triggers an evaluation of the interface against the component's duty and its vision". The evaluation ends either with consumers moved onto the interface or with the interface adjusted. A3 Success signal 5 says each detected violation "is evaluated against the component's duty rather than only corrected in the consumer". E6 says: "A detected crossing is a signal about the interface as well as a fault in the consumer". Until each bypass is resolved, T is one system with each bypassing component (E5, A0). Under A0, any interface adjustment answers to every consumer whose contract it changes, or for a versioned interface to those that adopt it.
NOT SETTLED: whether several bypassers count as stronger evidence than one, and who owns or schedules the evaluation. GUESS: several independent bypasses point more strongly to an interface shortfall than to faults in individual consumers. Under A0/M5, work beyond the current change's scope is recorded with a revival trigger, and choosing it falls to the director (R4, AR3).

### OBSERVATIONS
- A3 says "the component's duty and its vision". The corpus defines no "vision" for a component; AR6 vision is per programme. It is unclear whose vision is meant.
- E6 defines adjacency as "a shared declared interface". A bypassing O therefore does not count as adjacent to T, even though it is more tightly coupled (one system). A0's tangent discipline uses adjacency, so its wording does not obviously cover improving a bypasser.
- The question premise says the change leaves T's "behaviour" unchanged. But changing internals that O calls directly can change what O observes. E5 says undeclared dependence is "a contract in fact ... a change to it reaches across". So "behaviour unchanged" holds only at the interface, not for O.
- A3 "Until then the two remain one system" does not say whether the current change author must start the evaluation, or may record it and move on. A0's scope and deferral rules have to be combined to answer this, and they do not settle it directly.
- Minor: every component here (T, P, O) is inside one service. So the service's axioms bind any change to T whatever the bypass status. The P/O distinction matters only for axioms that P or O bring as subsystems through their own traits.
