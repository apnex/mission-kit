### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. O depends on T's internals, which no declaration covers; E5 calls this "a contract in fact, not a declared boundary - and a change to it reaches across" (entities/E5-system.md, Discriminators). E6 says code "that reaches past the interface ... has crossed the boundary", and its blast-radius row includes "any unit that reaches it, or that it reaches, with no declared interface" (entities/E6-component.md). A0 adds that where "no boundary between them is declared ... the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind" (axioms/README.md). So for binding, T and O are one system, and O's axioms bind your change. The service that contains T binds as well, because a change answers to "every system containing it" (A0). On the bypass itself: it is an A3 fault (Air-Gap Principle: "no unit reaches into another's internals", axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md; E5 Boundaries). A0 asks you to close as much of a touched gap as the touch allows, or to record it with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5). It must not be passed silently: "Either way the gap is recorded."

### boundary-bypass.B2
O, and not P (P only through the service that contains them both). P composes with T through a declared contract that your change leaves unchanged. A0 says such a system "is not altered, and its axioms do not bind" (axioms/README.md). E6 agrees: an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes" (entities/E6-component.md, blast radius). O reaches T with no declared interface, so O is inside the blast radius and its axioms bind (E6 blast radius; E5 "contract in fact"; A0). Your change also answers to T itself and to the service that contains T (A0: "every system containing it"). P's own axioms only bind if they are also the service's, because the service binds as the container and P does not.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No, by the most direct reading: for O, T and O stay one system for binding. E5 says "Declaring the boundary is what makes them two again", but the boundary in question is the one between the two units. O's calls do not go through the new interface, so O still depends on behaviour that is "a contract in fact, not a declared boundary" (entities/E5-system.md). E6 says code "that reaches past the interface ... has crossed the boundary". Its blast radius still includes "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface" (entities/E6-component.md). The declaration does separate T from consumers that use it. The bypass is still an A3 fault. NOT SETTLED in one respect: the corpus does not say what happens if the new interface happens to declare exactly the functions O already calls. GUESS: in that case O's calls would go through a declared contract, and T and O would compose.

### boundary-bypass.B4
Each detected bypass is an A3 fault: the Air-Gap Principle, and success signal 2, "nothing reaches into another unit's internals" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md). The check itself meets A3 success signal 5, "Boundary violations are detectable by review or tooling." Until each bypass is fixed, every bypassing component is one system with T for binding, so a change to T's internals answers to all their axioms (E5, E6 blast radius, A0). Under A0, a change that touches a bypass closes as much of it as the touch allows. Bypasses outside the change's scope may be closed opportunistically only if they meet A14's tangent discipline. Otherwise each is recorded with its evidence and a revival trigger (M5), and the director decides what work comes next (R4, AR3) (axioms/README.md). The gap must not be passed silently. NOT SETTLED: the corpus names no specific work-type or procedure for fixing a set of bypasses found by a check.

### OBSERVATIONS
- E5's Definition and Boundaries are written for the case where nobody declared any boundary ("across a boundary nobody declared", "nothing declares the boundary between them"). The case where T has a declared interface and O goes past it is covered only indirectly: by E5's "contract in fact" discriminator, and by E6's "reaches past the interface" and blast-radius row. E5's sentence "Declaring the boundary is what makes them two again" could be misread to mean that declaring an interface for T separates T from O even if O keeps bypassing it (B3).
- A0's scope rule says a change "touches ... any consumer whose contract it alters". It does not say directly whether changing internals that O depends on in fact counts as altering O's "contract". The binding rule (A0, "What brings an axiom into force") settles that O binds, but the scope rule (how far the change must close the A3 gap) is less explicit.
- In these questions O and T are both inside the same service, so the service's axioms bind either way. The question of O's own axioms only matters where O has traits the service-level reading would not already include.
