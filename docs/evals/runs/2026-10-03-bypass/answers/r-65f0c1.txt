### boundary-bypass.B1
Yes. O's dependency on T's internals is undeclared. E6's blast-radius row (entities/E6-component.md:100) includes "any unit that reaches it, or that it reaches, with no declared interface". A0 (axioms/README.md:118) says that where no boundary is declared, "the two are one system with the traits of both, and the axioms either unit would bring all bind". The change also answers to the service, because the service contains T (A0:116, E5:23). On the bypass itself, the corpus treats it as a fault. A3's Air-Gap Principle says "no unit reaches into another's internals" (axioms/A3-sovereign-composition.md:22), and E5:83 says reaching in without a contract "is an A3 fault, and it collapses the two into one system for binding". A0 says a gap must never be passed silently ("Either way the gap is recorded", axioms/README.md:84). GUESS: because your change touches the internals O calls, the bypass is probably inside your scope, so you would close as much of it as the touch allows (A0:79) rather than only record it. The corpus does not say this for this case.

### boundary-bypass.B2
O, and not P. That is in addition to the service that contains T (A0, axioms/README.md:116). P reaches T only through the declared interface, and your change leaves that interface unchanged. A0:117 says "A system it only composes with, through a contract the change leaves as it was, is not altered, and its axioms do not bind". E6:100 agrees: an adjacent component is reached "only if the interface between them changes". O reaches T with no declared interface, so for binding it is one system with T and its axioms bind (E5:38, E5:65, E6:100). So the same change on the same T answers to one consumer and not to the other. What decides this is how each consumer reaches T, not T itself.

### boundary-bypass.B3
No, judging by the corpus's reasoning, although no file names this exact case. E5:66 says "Declaring the boundary is what makes them two again". Read alone, that sentence suggests yes. But E5:38 says behaviour depended on "without any declaration is a contract in fact, not a declared boundary". O's calls still go to internals that the new interface does not cover. E6:44 says code that "reaches past the interface ... has crossed the boundary", and E6:100 keeps "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface" in the blast radius. So declaring the interface separates T only from consumers that use it. O stays one system with T until O's calls move onto the declared interface, and the bypass is still an A3 fault (A3:22). NOT SETTLED explicitly: E5:66 does not say that declaring an interface is insufficient while a consumer bypasses it.

### boundary-bypass.B4
The corpus treats every bypass as an A3 fault ("no unit reaches into another's internals", A3:22). A3 success signal 5 names exactly this kind of check: "Boundary violations are detectable by review or tooling" (A3:56). Each bypassing component is one system with T for binding (E5:65, A0:118), so a change to T's internals answers to all their axioms. A0's direction rule applies (axioms/README.md:72-85). No change may add a bypass or deepen one. A change that touches a bypass closes as much of it as the touch allows. A bypass outside the change's scope, or not adjacent through a declared interface, is not closed on the spot. You record it "with its evidence and a revival trigger ([`M5`])", and the director chooses larger work (R4, AR3). NOT SETTLED: the corpus gives no specific procedure for finding several bypasses at once. GUESS: you would file one recorded gap per bypass, or one per component, and rerun the check as a gate (A0:87-88).

### OBSERVATIONS
1. E5 and A0 phrase the collapse rule as "no boundary between them is declared" (A0:118, E5:16, E5:65). That fits a T with no interface at all. It is ambiguous for a T whose interface exists but is bypassed, because a reader could say a boundary between T and O is declared. Answering the bypass case needs E5:38 ("contract in fact") and E6:44/E6:100 to be put together. No sentence states it directly.
2. E5:66 "Declaring the boundary is what makes them two again" is unqualified. Read literally, it gives the wrong answer for B3, where an interface is declared but a consumer keeps reaching past it.
3. E6:100's wording "any unit that reaches it ... with no declared interface" is ambiguous. It could mean "when T has no declared interface" or "reaching without going through a declared interface". Only the second reading covers bypass.
4. T, P and O are all inside one service, and the change answers to that service in every case (A0:116). The corpus does not say how "P's axioms do not bind" fits with the fact that the service's axioms bind. If P's traits make the service stateful (for example), P's axioms arrive anyway through the container. It is unclear whether excluding P in B2 has any practical effect.
5. A0:77 says a change touches "any consumer whose contract it alters". The questions assume T's behaviour is unchanged, yet O's contract in fact is T's internals. The corpus does not say whether an internal change that keeps behaviour the same "alters" O's contract in fact. E5:65 sidesteps this with "nothing shows that a change to one leaves the other unchanged".
