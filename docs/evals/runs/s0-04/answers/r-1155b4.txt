### explain-routing.Q1
The set ST1 (sets/ST1-explain.md) gathers the guidance for this and names two members: S15 for "the form of any message to a human" and M10 for "leading a human to understanding, then a decision". S15 (style/S15-message-to-a-human.md) asks for the point first ("the first line is the conclusion"), so the slip goes in line one, followed by one step, explained or omitted system-specific names, and two or three closing picks (Continue/Explore/Return). It also says to "Use narrative only where it carries understanding" and to leave out what you did when it does not change what the human understands or decides. M10 (methods/M10-guided-dialogue.md) asks that the human see "what you see on the point - nothing missing that matters, nothing extra". If a decision follows from the slip, M10 adds: one decision per message, with every option costed, doing nothing included. A13 (axioms/A13-director-intent-amplification.md) asks that you never go "silently dark on an action or decision the Director is owed", and that what reaches the director be "decision-shaped" ("digest over dump, decision over status"). ST1 records that "Bad news ... [was] tested in one pair", so NOT SETTLED: no entry gives guidance written for bad news as such. GUESS: the AGENTS.md doctrine "A caveat does not repair a headline" (section 5.1, an unnumbered file) also applies to how you word the slip.

### explain-routing.Q2
S15 applies: the "point first" line gives the conclusion, you "Show where you are: the step, and the finish line", you explain every unfamiliar name, label or ID or leave it out, and the message ends with picks. S15 also limits the narrative of what you did to the parts that change what the human understands or decides. M10 applies with special force because its "When to apply" says "especially when they have not followed the work". It asks you to know the finish line and "say how many steps remain", to give one step per message, and to keep depth "available on request and never pushed". ST1 is the set that routes you to both. A13 asks that "Status-chatter, ceremony, how-to" never reach the director, and that surfacing happen "at gate-points rather than per-step". That puts it in tension with sending a pure progress update at all. NOT SETTLED: no entry says when a progress update is owed rather than an attention leak, apart from A13's two-sided floor ("under-surfacing is as much a fault as over-surfacing").

### explain-routing.Q3
S15 asks for the answer first, then one step, explained names, and picks at the end. S15 also says to "Use narrative only where it carries understanding - for instance when the path you took is the evidence", which bears directly on a "why" answer. M10's understand limit asks that the director end up "seeing what you see on the point - nothing missing that matters, nothing extra", reached "one confirmed step at a time, with depth available on request". ST1 routes you to both. The standing context AGENTS.md (an unnumbered file, not a ledger entry) adds in section 5.2: "Source settles mechanism ... Before stating why something behaves as it does, read the code". Its section 5.1 rule "Measured or inferred ... State which" also applies to a causal answer. Its section 5.3 adds "Separate observation from explanation when sending". NOT SETTLED: no ID-bearing entry is written specifically for answering a "why" question.

### explain-routing.Q4
M10 is the main entry: its understand limit is "the human ends up seeing what you see on the point - nothing missing that matters, nothing extra". Its steps are to know the finish line, give one step per message, and confirm that each step has landed (A8, as cited in M10). S15 governs each message: point first, one step, and "Explain any system-specific name, label or ID the human has not seen, or leave it out", although "General engineering terms may be assumed". ST1 routes you to both. ST1 also records "Bad news and teaching were each tested in one pair", so evidence for the teaching case is thin. NOT SETTLED: no entry covers preparing the director to pass the concept on to others. GUESS: K28 (an ASD-STE100 skill, triggered for "documentation that must be readable by a non-native English speaker") could help if the audience needs it, but its trigger names documentation, not messages.

### explain-routing.Q5
S15 gives the decision frame: "what is being decided; why now; the options, each with its cost, including doing nothing; a recommendation, labelled and kept separate; then ask for a pick". Each option is "a Continue pick naming it", and a request to act is "never hidden in an optional pick". The deadline belongs in the frame's "why now" slot. M10 step 4 asks for one decision per message, with all real options and none hidden, and step 5 asks you to record the decision and its reasoning, saying where in one line. A13 asks that you never narrow, rework or pre-empt the option set, and that any preference lean appear only as "a labeled tie-break input". R4 and W23 make ratification the director's act alone, and AR4 is the record the decision goes into. ST1 names AR3 (board, "many moves at once") as related, with an unreconciled tension against one decision at a time. NOT SETTLED: no entry says what to do if the deadline passes without a pick, or how to fit three options plus "doing nothing" into S15's "two or three picks".

### explain-routing.Q6
M10 is the main guide: "one decision at a time" and "Ask for one decision per message". So the first decision is asked, and recorded under step 5, before the dependent one is put. M10 step 3 ("If a decision depends on understanding, establish the understanding first, and confirm it has landed") and its citation of A8 ("each step lands before the next builds on it") support that order. M10 step 1 asks you to say how many steps remain, so the director knows a second decision is coming. S15 governs each message: its decision frame, "One step, one question, asked at the end", and "Show where you are: the step, and the finish line". A13 also applies, through the risk of "narrowing the choices": the first decision may change the second's options, but you must not pre-narrow them. A13 also notes that a single approval may execute a whole downstream chain. AR4 and W23 cover recording and ratifying each decision. NOT SETTLED: no entry speaks explicitly to decision-on-decision dependence (step 3 covers dependence on understanding), and GUESS: you could preview the second decision's options in each branch of the first, but nothing in the text says to.

### OBSERVATIONS
- S15 says "End with two or three picks", but it also says that when a decision is asked "each option is a Continue pick" and Explore and Return keep their meaning. With three options plus the "doing nothing" option the frame requires, that comes to more than three picks. The two instructions conflict for Q5.
- ST1 records its own gaps: bad news and teaching were "each tested in one pair" and "no move is isolated". The routing for Q1 and Q4 therefore rests on thin evidence.
- ST1 lists K5 (three questions a round), AR3 (many moves at once) and RU1 as related with unreconciled tensions. A cold agent asked for a multi-option or multi-decision message could reach any of them.
- A13 bars status-chatter from reaching the director, yet also bars going "silently dark". No entry says where a progress update (Q2) falls between the two.
- The useful guidance on "why" answers (Q3: read the source before stating mechanism, measured or inferred) sits in AGENTS.md, which has no ID and no ledger row, so the ledger-driven routing does not reach it.
- R4's "Axiom alignment" section lists A13 twice, as two separate bullets.
- M10 states that the director "proceeded from the decide limit without confirming it in words". The decide half of M10 is therefore less directly confirmed than the understand half.
- INDEX.md says to resolve links against `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apnex/mission-kit/main/` when no local checkout exists. I read only the local corpus copy.
