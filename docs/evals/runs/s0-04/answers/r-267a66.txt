### explain-routing.Q1
ST1 (sets/ST1-explain.md) is the set for "communicat[ing] with a human who has limited context"; it routes to its two members, S15 and M10. S15 (style/S15-message-to-a-human.md) asks that you put the point first ("the first line is the conclusion"), so the slip goes in line one, not after the narrative. It also asks for one step and one question at the end, explained or omitted system names, and narrative "only where it carries understanding". M10 (methods/M10-guided-dialogue.md) asks that the director "ends up seeing what you see on the point - nothing missing that matters, nothing extra", one confirmed step at a time. If the slip needs a decision, M10 says to give the options with their costs, including doing nothing, and to record the decision. A13 (axioms/A13-director-intent-amplification.md) asks that you never go "silently dark on an action or decision the Director is owed", that you send "digest over dump", and that you never narrow the choices. NOT SETTLED: no entry is written for bad news as such. ST1 itself says "Bad news and teaching were each tested in one pair; no move is isolated".

### explain-routing.Q2
ST1 routes to S15 and M10, and M10 says it applies "especially when they have not followed the work". S15 asks for the point first, "Show where you are: the step, and the finish line", system-specific names explained or left out, and references "in one line or a pick, not through the body". It ends the message with two or three Continue/Explore/Return picks. M10 asks you to "Know the finish line before you start, and say how many steps remain", give one step per message, and keep depth "available on request and never pushed". A13 cuts against plain status: "Status-chatter, ceremony, how-to ... never reach the Director; what reaches the Director is decision-shaped". But A13 also says "under-surfacing is as much a fault as over-surfacing". RU1 (rules/RU1-default-reject-honest-yield.md) asks you to lead with delivered outcome before narrative, but only if the update reports a sweep, refactor programme or audit. ST1 says RU1 "is triggered by sweeps and audits, not by any report".

### explain-routing.Q3
ST1 routes to S15 and M10. S15 asks that the first line be the conclusion, so the cause comes first. It also says: "Use narrative only where it carries understanding - for instance when the path you took is the evidence." M10's understand limit asks that the director end up seeing what you see, with "nothing missing that matters, nothing extra", and with "depth available on request and never pushed" (for example, an Explore pick). A13 asks that the answer be a digest, not a dump. NOT SETTLED: no entry addresses causal explanation as such. GUESS: the standing context's measured-vs-inferred discipline applies, and AR4's provenance tags ([MEASURED]/[ARGUED]) suggest marking which parts of the cause you observed. But AR4 is written for decision records, not messages.

### explain-routing.Q4
ST1 routes to S15 and M10. M10's understand limit asks that the director come to see the concept as you see it, with nothing missing and nothing extra. It also asks that understanding be "reached one confirmed step at a time", so you confirm each step has landed before building on it (step 3, and A8 as cited in M10's rationale). S15 says "Explain any system-specific name, label or ID the human has not seen, or leave it out. General engineering terms may be assumed". S15 also asks for one step per message and picks at the end. NOT SETTLED: the goal of "so they can describe it to others" is not covered by any entry. ST1 records teaching as only "tested in one pair". K28 (ASD-STE100) is for documentation read by non-native English speakers, so it does not obviously apply here.

### explain-routing.Q5
ST1 routes to S15 and M10. S15's decision frame asks for "what is being decided; why now; the options, each with its cost, including doing nothing; a recommendation, labelled and kept separate; then ask for a pick". Here "why now" is where the deadline goes. S15 also says that "each option is a Continue pick naming it" and that "A request for the human to act is never hidden in an optional pick". M10 step 4 asks for one decision per message, with every real option shown and none hidden. Step 5 asks you to record the decision and its reasoning. A13 asks you never to rework the option set, narrow the choices or pre-empt the decision. A preference lean may appear only "as a labeled tie-break input". AR4 and W23 govern recording and ratifying the result, and R4 confirms the ratifying act is the director's alone. NOT SETTLED: three options plus doing nothing makes four picks, but S15 says to end "with two or three picks". The corpus does not reconcile these.

### explain-routing.Q6
ST1 routes to S15 and M10. M10's decide limit is "one decision at a time", and step 4 says "Ask for one decision per message". So you ask for the first decision, record it, and only then frame the second. M10 step 1 asks you to state the finish line and how many steps remain, and S15 asks you to "Show where you are". That tells the director up front that a second, dependent decision follows. M10 step 3 asks that you establish and confirm any understanding a decision depends on before you ask for it. Each decision uses S15's decision frame and is recorded per M10 step 5, AR4 and W23. ST1 notes a tension with AR3, which "offers many moves at once, against one decision at a time"; AR3 requires each "Decisions required" entry to name what it blocks. A13 still applies: show the full option set and do not pre-empt the second choice on the strength of the first.

### OBSERVATIONS
- S15's "End with two or three picks" conflicts with the decision frame when there are three real options plus doing nothing. The text does not say whether doing nothing becomes a pick or how many picks are then allowed (Q5).
- ST1 lists RU1, K5 and AR3 as related "with a tension to reconcile before it joins", but does not reconcile them. An agent who also hydrates K5 (three questions per round) or AR3 (many moves at once) gets advice that cuts against M10's "one at a time".
- S5's trigger ("You are about to name a version, date or other point-in-time identifier in prose") literally matches naming a new release date in a slip message. But its rule and "When to apply" are about user-facing documentation, so it probably does not apply. The trigger is broader than the rule.
- A13 bars "status-chatter" from reaching the director, which is in tension with a request for a progress update (Q2). It is unclear whether an update the director asked for counts as status-chatter or as something they are "owed".
- S0's trigger also matches ("a message a human will read"), but its content just points to S15.
- ST1 openly records gaps: bad news and teaching were tested only once, and no move is isolated. So the guidance for Q1 and Q4 is weaker than for decisions.
- S15 and M10 cite evidence in docs/evals/human/..., which is not in this corpus, so it could not be checked.
