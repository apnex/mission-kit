# M12 - Investigate a set

## Rule

An investigation is a dated analysis of a set's population against its charter's vision, scope and growth policy.\
It produces a partition, the place of each member in it, and the gaps; the charter is unchanged by it.

1. **Read the charter and every member.** The vision and scope say what the set is for; the growth policy says how it grows and what members are balanced on; the operation section states the territory-stress pass that tests the scope beyond the members currently held.
2. **Propose a partition of the scope and place every member in it** - cutting across the dimensions the scope and the charter's operation stress pass name, as well as the members' triggers, subjects, and the moments or risks they address - so that every member lands in it, and in exactly one place. A member that fits nowhere, or everywhere, is evidence the partition is wrong. More than one partition is usually defensible; state why this one was chosen. Where the charter measures the set against another set's population - its scope is that population, or covering it is a dimension of succeeding - read that measure as a cover, beside any partition of the set's own scope: the cells are that population's members, and a member here may sit in several without the cover being wrong; read which members it covers from the relation's declarations and any view generated from them, and where no declaration exists, building the view by hand is itself a finding.
3. **Let the scope set the extent, not the population.** A cell no member fills is kept and named as a gap; removing it to match the population erases the gap it exposes. Under such a scope, a member of the other set that nothing here covers is such a gap, and a member here that covers nothing is outside the scope, or shows a member the other set lacks. Where the relation runs the other way - this set's charter requires each of its own members to be covered by another set's members - a member here that nothing covers is a fault of this set, not a gap in the other. Run the territory-stress pass stated in the charter's operation section - sampling artifacts across claimed media, synthesizing reference architectures, walking exchange moments, or stressing the root set of layers against the corpus vision - so unpopulated regions of scope, seam collisions, and bundled moves surface even when the current population is small or lopsided.
4. **Test each gap.** Search the corpus for a member, or guidance elsewhere, that already fills it, and name the searches run. A gap that survives is real; a candidate that would fill it is named where one exists.
5. **Show a gap or argue there is none.** A partition that names at least one checked absence has shown it can detect one; one that names none must say why the population is complete against it.
6. **Check the balance** against the growth policy: a crowded cell beside empty ones is a finding.
7. **Record it in the project's own record**, dated, citing the charter it measured against. The charter does not link to it.
8. **Carry each gap to the backlog** ([`AR5`](../artifacts/AR5-backlog.md)) as a row with a revival trigger, naming the set it is about; the board ([`AR3`](../artifacts/AR3-board.md)) weighs them for the director.
9. **If the vision, scope or growth policy is itself shown wrong,** propose a re-aim under [`M11`](M11-change-a-charter.md); a finding about members - overlap, crowding, a candidate - is acted on by [`M13`](M13-change-a-set.md).

---

## Rationale

A charter describes the ideal and cannot see its own population, so completeness is found by measuring, not by declaring.\
Cutting the set is a judgement that can be made more than one way, so it is held in a dated analysis a later investigation may replace, rather than in the charter where it would freeze the set.\
The obligations in steps 2 to 5 are what make a partition able to find anything: a well-formed partition that cannot detect a gap is decoration.

---

## When to apply

- Before deciding what a set should admit next.
- When a set reads as complete to the people who use it, which is when a missing kind is least visible.
- After a change to a charter's vision, scope or growth policy, to measure the population against it again.
