# M11 - Change a charter

## Rule

A charter is changed to make it the best version of itself in context, and its existing words may be improved where improving them advances that.\
What is preserved is meaning, and the check on meaning is as strict as a check on words would be: **every changed or removed sentence is accounted for.**

| Change | Permitted | Evidence the change carries |
|---|---|---|
| **move** | yes | none |
| **re-aim** | when the vision, scope or growth policy is shown wrong, typically by an investigation ([`M12`](M12-investigate-a-set.md)) | the evidence, and a director ratification |
| **reword** | when it materially improves clarity or precision | old and new side by side, and the reason |
| **merge** | when two passages state one thing | both originals, and why one suffices |
| **correct** | when an error or contradiction is exposed | the error cited, and the old text kept in the record |
| **delete** | only when the claim is wrong, unpaid, or held elsewhere | the claim, and which of the three applies |

1. **Weigh cost against quality.** Changing settled text invalidates a reader's memory and puts reasoning at risk, so an edit is made when it advances the document, and a marginal one is not made. An added passage answers a question the charter could not, and improves what a reader can do.
2. **Account for each sentence** in the change's record, by the table above.
3. **Test before ratifying.** A change that would alter what a reader decides is tested by [`M2`](M2-test-drive-docs-by-execution.md), against the charter's probes, with the previous version as the comparison. The author's own reading of old against new is not that test.
4. **Ratify.** The director ratifies a change to any charter; a re-aim always needs it.

The previous version stays recoverable at its commit, so the record of what a charter said is never lost where the charter no longer says it.

---

## Rationale

A charter is what a set's population is measured against, so an unaccounted change silently moves the measure.\
Accounting for each sentence keeps that visible without freezing the text.

**An earlier rule preserved every original sentence character for character, and was withdrawn.**\
It proved nothing was lost and capped every revision at the original plus an appendix.\
Its decisive failure was a sentence it forbade fixing: the sentence was ambiguous, the rule allowed only flagging it, and the flag recorded a contradiction that did not exist.\
Fixing the sentence removed both the ambiguity and the paragraph that had been defending a false conflict, and the charter got shorter.

---

## When to apply

- Any edit to a charter's text.
- A re-aim: an investigation shows the vision, scope or growth policy is wrong.
- A change to the growth policy itself, chosen on the board and ratified by the director.

> **Correction.** This list once included *replacing a member in a capped set*. Replacing a member changes the set, not the charter, and is done by [`M13`](M13-change-a-set.md).
